## How it was done

**The app.** The e2e server ran as `E2E_PORT=4997 node e2e/server.mjs`, which is:

- the production build;
- an in-memory MongoDB;
- the seeded owner account and its week of data.

Everything listened on loopback only and was stopped afterwards.

**The browser.** A Playwright script, not committed, drove Chromium as real players:

- a visitor;
- two guests;
- a guest who makes a challenge and a visitor who takes it;
- two adult accounts who play each other and become friends;
- the seeded owner.

**The states.** 77 states were captured, each at 390×844, 768×1024 and 1280×800 and each in light and dark:

- home, for a visitor, a guest and an account;
- the three lobbies, for a visitor and a guest;
- the invite chooser and the waiting room;
- playing and the result, for Rock Paper Scissors, Tic-Tac-Toe and chess, including a selected chess piece, the
  resign dialog and both sides of a result;
- pass and play: setup, start, mid-game and result;
- the daily puzzle, for a visitor, a guest and an account;
- the leaderboards (overall, weekly, chess and RPS), plus a loading state from a slowed API;
- the visitor, guest, account and public profiles;
- friends: visitor, empty, request, list and the request toast;
- challenges: own, received as a visitor and as a guest, taken back, the "Challenge taken" popup and the inbox;
- the Feedback dialog, the phone menu and the sound control;
- login and sign-up, each with an error;
- privacy, terms and the 404;
- three dead ends: a missing challenge, a missing profile and an invalid friend link;
- the owner console: overview, statuses, players, games, retention, conversion, engagement, feedback, ages and
  flags.

**The checks run on every state:**

- **axe:** the CC-183 rule set (WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 A and AA, plus best practice) in light. In dark only the
  colour rules ran, as in `e2e/accessibility.spec.js`. Each ran at 390 and at 1280, on 67 states.
- **Sideways overflow:** any element wider than the screen.
- **Tap targets:** anything under 24 px or 44 px at 390.
- **Reduced motion:** anything still animating or transitioning under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.
- **Type:** the font sizes, families and weights in use.

**Checks run on key states only:**

- a 30-press keyboard walk, recording the focus order and whether each stop has a visible ring;
- a hover check, recording which links and buttons change at all on hover.

**Build and Lighthouse.** `npm run build:web` gave the chunk sizes. The main chunk was split by its source map.
Lighthouse 13.5 ran in mobile mode (slow 4G, 4× CPU) on `/`, `/games/chess` and `/daily/chess`.

**The code.** A static read of `apps/web/src` checked tokens, components and states, with file and line references.

**The screens.** The screenshots were reviewed state by state against the identity ADRs.

## Scales

- **Severity, Nielsen 1–4:**
  - **1** cosmetic: fix when there is time.
  - **2** minor: low priority.
  - **3** major: important to fix.
  - **4** catastrophe: fix before release.
- **Impact, 1–5:** how many players see it and how often, multiplied by how much it hurts the premium arcade feel
  or blocks a task.
  - **5:** every player, every session, clearly visible.
  - **1:** one rare screen, or only the owner.
  - The owner console has one user, so its impact is capped at 2.

## Summary

**What is already strong.** The hero and the arcade art. The 404. The invite chooser. The visitor profile, which
has art, a clear button and a preview.

**Accessibility and motion basics are clean:**

- Lighthouse accessibility scores 100 on all three pages.
- axe finds no colour-contrast failures in either theme.
- Reduced motion leaves nothing moving on any of the 77 states.
- No raw colour is used outside `tokens.css`.

**What holds the site back from feeling premium:**

1. **The header breaks at tablet width on every page.** At 768 px every one of the 77 states scrolls sideways. The
   header is 813 px wide for a visitor and up to 1203 px for the owner. A guest's name wraps one letter per line.
2. **Too many looks for the same thing:**
   - five or six button styles on one screen;
   - at least five card styles;
   - four layouts for a dead end;
   - two copies of the tabs;
   - browser-default inputs next to arcade fields.
3. **Results and rewards pile up.** A result screen stacks seven or more actions of equal weight. It repeats the
   outcome, and lets the XP pop, confetti, toasts and the save-progress card collide. ADR-050 asks for the opposite.
4. **Light mode loses the identity.** Dark mode carries the neon look. Light mode is pale lavender on white, the win
   colours are brown and olive, and it reads like a utility app.
5. **The first route is over budget.** The main chunk is 192.2 kB gzip against a budget of 150 kB, and chess.js
   leaks into it.
6. **Empty, loading and error states are text only.** There are about 24 bare "Loading…" lines. A visitor's
   `/friends` stays on "Loading…" forever. The chess lobby shifts its layout by 0.37 CLS as it loads.

**Root cause.** There is no route-level code splitting: only boards and the console are lazy. On top of that, one
import that looks harmless brings chess.js into home.

**The fix:**

- `React.lazy` every route except `/`.
- Move `colourName` and `opposite` into a module without chess.js.
- Add a size check to CI.

At a rough estimate this takes the first route to about 135–145 kB.

## Since CC-314

CC-314 merged into `main` (#160) after these screenshots were taken. The code was read again on the new `main`.

**What it fixes or addresses:**

- **Focus no longer escapes a dialog.** Modal now makes the page behind it `inert` and returns focus on close
  (`components/Modal/Modal.jsx`). This addresses the focus-escape findings on the Feedback and Resign dialogs.
- **`dialogQueue`** shows one dialog at a time.
- **The z-index clashes are gone,** replaced by `--layer-*` tokens.
- **Tokens are split** into `styles/tokens/*.css`, with a CI check for raw values (`scripts/check-css-tokens.mjs`).

  [CONTRACT.md](CONTRACT.md), and a living page at `/admin/design`. This audit did not capture that page.

**Still true on the new `main`:**

- **A click on the backdrop still closes every Modal,** destructive confirms included (`onClick={onClose}` on the
  backdrop).
- **`--text-base` and `--space-5` are still used and still undefined.** The token check does not catch unknown
  `var()` names.
- **Every other finding here is unchanged,** because CC-314 adds no `<Card>`, no Button `danger` variant or link form,
  no EmptyState, no Field variants and no header change. CC-314 writes the contracts; the top 15 above turn them
  into components and use them.

The next review under CC-316 should take its screenshots again on the new `main`.

## Limits of this audit

- The 390 px shots use a desktop Chromium context resized to 390, not phone emulation with touch. Tap-target sizes
  and layout are right; touch-only styles (`pointer: coarse`) were not seen.
- **The guest shots show sound muted.** The capture script clicked the mute toggle while looking for sound settings.
  For the same reason the sound settings popover itself was not captured.
- **The connection banner was not seen.** `context.setOffline` does not close an open WebSocket in Chromium, so the
  `room-offline-banner` shots show an ordinary connected room. This is not a finding about the app.
- **The friend-request toast** may have left before the capture, 2.5 s after the request. Re-test before filing it.
- **Lighthouse ran against the local server, without compression.** Its FCP and LCP are worse than in production;
  CLS, unused JavaScript and the bundle numbers are not affected.
- **Hover changes were measured on the element itself.** A card whose hover style sits on a parent would show as
  "no change". The home cards were checked by eye too.
- **Firefox and WebKit were not captured.** CI covers them functionally.
