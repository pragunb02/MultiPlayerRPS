## How it was done

**The app.** The e2e server ran as `e2e/playwright.config.js` starts it: the production build (`npm run build:web`)
and an in-memory MongoDB with the seeded owner. The HTTPS front and the app listened on loopback only. Both stopped
when the run ended.

**The walk.** A Playwright spec was run with `--workers=1` and not committed. It reused `e2e/matchStates.js`, so every
match state is built the way the match-shell tests build it. It ran twice, once on a desktop (1280 × 720) and once on
a phone (390 × 844, touch). It played these people:

- a visitor on every public route;
- a guest and a new adult account on their own pages;
- two players through every state of each game:
  - waiting, my turn and their turn;
  - win, loss and draw;
  - a draw offer, both sides;
  - rematch asked, asked of me, declined, asked while away, asked while offline, and asked on another page;
- a visitor against each game's computer character;
- pass and play for all three games;
- the daily puzzle;
- two accounts becoming friends from a result screen;
- a challenge link, made by one person and taken by a visitor;
- log in and sign up, with errors;
- the legal pages, the 404 and four dead ends: a missing room, challenge, player and friend link;
- the owner console, as the seeded owner only.

For each state the spec saved a screenshot and a dump of the page: the title, every h1, the visible buttons, every
control with no visible word and its accessible name, and the main text.

**The code.** Three readers read the copy in `apps/web/src`, one area each, so that states which are hard to reach
were covered too: a crash, a server error, a room that is full, an aborted game, chess draw rules and so on. They
covered:

1. the match screens;
2. the site pages;
3. the shell, auth, errors and the owner console.

Each claim they made that a top finding rests on was checked again against the code or a screenshot.

**Scoring.** Each screen and state was scored against the checklist below. Only failures are listed, with what
already works in [Summary](#summary).

**Severity:**

- **B, blocks play.** A first-time player can get stuck, or cannot learn how to play.
- **C, confuses.** The player can go on, but has to guess what something means or what happens next.
- **P, polish.** The words could be clearer, but nobody is misled.

## The checklist and its sources

Every screen and every state answers three questions in plain words, without scrolling on a phone:

- **Q1. What is this?**
- **Q2. What can I do here?** One obvious primary action.
- **Q3. What happens next, or how do I get back?**

Then C1 to C12. The exact wording, as the contract keeps it, is in
[CONTRACT.md section 7](CONTRACT.md#7-clarity-cc-348).

**What the game onboarding research adds for casual games.** The research above agrees on four points:

1. Teach by letting the player do the thing in a safe moment, not with a wall of text.
2. Give one tip at a time, at the moment it matters.
3. Keep the rules short and easy to find again later.
4. Never show a tip again once it is learned.

For ClassiCade that means three things. The three short steps already written for each game (CC-259) are enough. They
must also reach the player who arrives by an invite link and never sees the lobby. And each board gets one hint, at
the first move, which goes away after it.

**What already works, and should be kept:**

- **Blocked presses explain themselves.** Every button that cannot act says why, and every button that waits says
  what it waits for ("Sending your move", "Checking whether you are signed in"). See `roomView.js` PLAY_REASONS and
  `useAskReason`.
- **The three How to play steps** (`games/howToPlay.js`) are short and plain. They are only in the wrong place.
- **The pass the phone screen** ("Pass the phone to Player 1 / Player 2, look away… / I'm Player 1, show my turn",
  `phone/pnp-rps-handover`) is the clearest screen in the app: what is this, the one action, and what happens next.
- **Clear pages:** the invite chooser (`desktop/room-invite-chooser`), the 404 (`desktop/visitor-404`), the
  challenge page (`desktop/challenge-received-guest`) and the closed-room reasons each have one clear button.
- **The waking-up screen** says what it waits for and that the page carries on by itself.
- **Tic-Tac-Toe** says "You play X". The computer character's tips after a loss teach the game.
- **Hinglish follows the rule.** No Hinglish line anywhere in `apps/web/src`, `packages/shared` or `apps/server`
  uses "aap", "aapka", "aapke", "kijiye", "karein" or "dijiye". Every line uses "tum" or a plain command, which
  passes C9's first half. Two lines fail its second half, saying less than the English: CL-09 and CL-86.
- **Every captured screen has exactly one h1, and every icon-only control has an accessible name** (see the next
  section). C6 still fails, because sighted players see no word.

**What holds clarity back, in four root causes:**

1. **The rules live only in the lobby, at its very bottom.** A friend who arrives by an invite link goes from the
   invite chooser straight into the game and never sees how to play (CL-23, CL-24, CL-15).
2. **The server's words reach the player.** A failed action shows the server's message, such as "Internal server
   error", "Not in room" or "Invalid email or password", with no next step. The crash screen has no button at all
   (CL-01, CL-02, CL-27, CL-91).
3. **The same question is asked twice, with two timers.** A rematch is asked in the banner, with 1:00, and in the
   result panel, with "Rematch available for 30:00". "New opponent" and "Leave room" do the same thing (CL-45 to
   CL-47).
4. **Words that only the builders know:** "host", "rematch request", "time control", "Create room", "player
   console", "PGN", "1500?", "Lv", and quest names like "Throw down". There are no first-time hints anywhere
   (CL-38, CL-39, CL-59).

## Limits

- **What the walk did not stop on:**
  - **Hard-to-reach states** were judged from the code, not a screenshot: a crash, a full room, an aborted game,
    the chess draw rules, a claimed win, an opponent losing connection, and a lost connection of your own.
  - **One phone state**, a Tic-Tac-Toe rematch asked while the other player is away, timed out on the phone run. The
    desktop run has it.
  - **The minor's journeys** (CC-052) and the legacy player were not walked.
- **Who judged.** The scoring is one researcher's reading, backed by the sources, not a study with players. A short
  test with five first-time players on a phone would confirm the top 10. That is the cheapest next step, as NN/g
  recommends.
- **Light theme only.** The screenshots are of the light theme. Clarity does not depend on the theme, and
  AUDIT-2026-10 covers both themes.
- **Not repeated.** Findings already in AUDIT-2026-10, such as the result screen's pile of rewards and the header at
  tablet width, are not repeated here.
