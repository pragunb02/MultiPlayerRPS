## Problem

On 2026-10-20 the owner said: "we are adding a lot of things to the website, a lot of text, a lot of pop-ups ...
make sure that we have proper monitoring of all those things", and "we are not increasing the friction". The site
already shows XP and level toasts after every match, achievement and quest toasts, "Save your progress" under a
guest's win, "your streak ends today", the connection banner, request banners and requests from other rooms on any
page (CC-207, PR #152 in review), the feedback dialog and confirm dialogs, and each new ticket adds more. Nobody
knows how many of these a player sees in one visit or one game, how many are closed unread, how often one covers
another, how long the key screens' text has become, or whether the first move now comes later. CC-174 to CC-177
count pages, presses and errors, but nothing names an interruption or a step, and no ticket has to say what friction
it may add.

## Job to be done

When the owner or a builder adds a toast, banner, dialog, request, line of text or step, they want its cost to
players counted from day one and judged against a budget, so they can keep what helps and remove what gets in the
way before it costs games.

## Appetite

L (up to 2 days of agent time). **Cut line:** first the runtime text measure (keep the CI text budget test), then the
alert badge (keep the weekly report's red and amber), then the flag-side field on events. The surface registry, the
two interruption events, per visit and per game counts, the budget, the guardrail rule and the weekly report are
never cut.

## Proposed solution

### 1. One registry of interruptions

`packages/shared/src/telemetry/surfaces.js`: every toast, banner, dialog, card, line and request the site can show,
each with:

| Field      | Values                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `id`       | a fixed name, `kind.what`: `toast.xp`, `toast.level_up`, `toast.achievement`, `toast.quest`, `card.save_progress`, `line.streak_at_risk`, `banner.connection`, `banner.request`, `request.site`, `dialog.feedback`, `dialog.confirm_leave`, `dialog.sound`, `screen.waking_up`, … |
| `kind`     | `toast`, `banner`, `dialog`, `card`, `line`, `request`, `screen`                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `blocking` | whether it blocks the page (a modal dialog, a full screen)                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `class`    | `essential` (the player cannot play right without it: connection, waking up, the opponent's request in this room, an error, a confirm the player asked for) or `optional` (everything else: rewards, prompts, promotions, requests from other rooms, new-feature notices)         |
| `ticket`   | the ticket that added it                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

A test reads the source and fails on any `ToastProvider` push, `Modal`, `RequestBanner`, `SiteRequests` entry or
component marked as an interruption that does not pass a registered `surface`, and on a registered surface that no
code shows. Adding a pop-up is one registry line and its ticket.

### 2. Two events and how each interruption ended

One hook, `useInterruption(surface, { open })`, used by every surface, and one host in context that knows which are
visible. CC-174's `track` sends:

| Event                  | When                                                                         | Fields                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `interruption_ended`   | the surface closed, or the page was left while it showed                     | `surface`, `kind`, `class`, `blocking` (from the registry), `outcome`: `acted` (its own action was pressed), `dismissed` (closed, Escape, swiped), `timed_out` (went away by itself), `covered` (another surface opened over it or in its place and it ended without an answer), `page_left`; `shown_ms` bucket (under 1 s, 1 to 3 s, 3 to 10 s, over 10 s); `phase`: `before_play`, `playing` (between the first move and the result), `result`, `elsewhere`; `trigger`: `player` (opened by their own press) or `system`; `stack` (0, 1, 2 or more other surfaces visible when it opened) |
| `interruption_counted` | once per visit, with `visit_ended`, and once per game, when the result shows | `scope` (`visit` or `game`), `game_id` for a game, `optional` bucket (0, 1, 2, 3 to 5, over 5), `blocking_optional` bucket (0, 1, 2 or more), `essential` bucket (same as `optional`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

One event per interruption, sent when it ends, keeps the volume at about one event per pop-up. "Shown" is the sum of
every outcome. Nothing in either event says who saw it.

### 3. Text length on key screens

Two measures, neither ever sends text:

1. **A CI text budget**, `e2e/textBudget.spec.js`: the browser suite opens each key screen in its usual states
   (home, a game's page, room waiting, room playing, the result, sign-up) at 390×844 and 1280×720 and counts the
   visible words in `main`, all of them and above the fold. It fails when a screen goes over its budget (Open
   decisions) and writes the counts to the job summary, so every PR shows the change.
2. **A runtime measure**, `screen_text_measured`, once per route per page load, 1 s after the route settles: `route`,
   `words` bucket (0 to 50, 51 to 100, 101 to 200, 201 to 400, over 400) and `fold_words` bucket (same), counted in
   the browser from `innerText` of `main`. It shows what players actually see (guest against account, long names,
   Hinglish and English pairs).

### 4. Time to first move, rage and dead clicks, drop-off at each step

- **Time to first move:** CC-220's `gameplay_started` gains `interruptions_before` (0, 1, 2, 3 or more optional
  interruptions shown since the page loaded). The report shows the p75 by entry type and by this bucket.
- **Rage and dead clicks:** CC-174's `rage_clicked` and `dead_clicked` gain `surface` when the press landed inside an
  interruption, so a toast that covers a button shows up as the hotspot it causes.
- **Steps:** a funnel registry, `packages/shared/src/telemetry/funnels.js`: each funnel is a fixed list of step ids,
  each step with its ticket. `funnel_step_reached` (`funnel`, `step`) is sent once per page load per step. Drop-off
  between two steps is one minus the ratio of their daily totals, labelled as a ratio of totals (ADR-034). A ticket
  that adds a step to any flow registers it here; the first funnels are visit to play, link to seat, sign-up and
  the CC-207 challenge flow.

### 5. The interruption budget

**The metric:** optional interruptions per visit, the total of `interruption_ended` with `class: optional` and
`trigger: system`, divided by the total of `visit_ended`, per India day. Read with two more numbers from
`interruption_counted`: the share of visits with 3 or more optional interruptions, and optional interruptions per
game during play. Interruptions the player opened themselves never count.

| Measure                                                 | Budget (green) | Amber             | Red                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------- |
| Optional interruptions per visit (mean)                 | 0.5 or less    | over 0.5 to 1.0   | over 1.0                  |
| Visits with 3 or more optional interruptions            | 5 % or less    | over 5 to 10 %    | over 10 %                 |
| Optional interruptions during play, per game            | 0.05 or less   | over 0.05 to 0.2  | over 0.2                  |
| Blocking optional interruptions during play             | 0              | —                 | any day with 5 or more    |
| A surface's dismissed share (7 days, at least 50 shown) | 50 % or less   | over 50 to 70 %   | over 70 %                 |
| A surface's covered share (7 days, at least 50 shown)   | 2 % or less    | over 2 to 5 %     | over 5 % (a stacking bug) |
| Time to first move p75 against the 4 weeks before       | within 10 %    | 10 to 25 % slower | over 25 % slower          |
| Rage plus dead clicks per 100 presses                   | 1 or less      | over 1 to 3       | over 3                    |

The nightly job (CC-176) works the colours out for the day and the week. Alerts are worked out on exact totals on
the server, but anything shown keeps ADR-036.2: a total under 5 is `"<5"`, and a share of fewer than 5 shows no
colour.

### 6. The friction guardrail every feature ticket names

From the day this ticket merges, every full ticket of type `feature` names in its Success metric table at least one
**Friction guardrail** row, chosen from: optional interruptions per visit, its own surface's dismissed share, time
to first move p75, the drop-off at its own step, rage and dead clicks on its route, or the text budget of the screen
it changes, each with a threshold and the CC-312 flag compared on against off. A feature that adds a surface or a
step registers it (sections 1 and 4) in the same PR.

1. `docs/tickets/TEMPLATE.md` gains the row and one line on how to pick it.
2. `scripts/tickets/checkTickets.mjs` fails a full `feature` ticket at Ready or later, started on or after the merge
   day, with no `Friction guardrail` row (older tickets are not checked, as CC-311 does).
3. PROCESS.md's flag section: a ramp step goes on only when the guardrail is not red with the flag on against off.

### 7. Flag sides on friction events

To compare a ramping feature's friction on against off without any id, the events in sections 2 to 4 carry
`flag` and `side` for at most one flag: the one ramping with a friction guardrail, named in the registry. No other
flag is ever attached.

### 8. The weekly UX report in `/admin/ux`

A **Week** view in CC-177's tab, opened by default on Mondays: the last full India week (Monday to Sunday) against
the week before.

1. The budget table of section 5 with colours and the change.
2. Top 10 surfaces by times shown: outcome mix (acted, dismissed, timed out, covered, page left), median time shown,
   phase, and the ticket that added it.
3. Interruptions per visit and per game as distributions (the buckets), by device.
4. Text per key screen: the CI budget's last numbers and the runtime buckets.
5. Time to first move p75 by entry and by interruptions before it.
6. Rage and dead click hotspots, with the surface when there is one.
7. Each registered funnel with drop-off per step, new steps marked.
8. Each flag ramping with a friction guardrail: its guardrail on against off.
9. A red or amber badge on the console tab when any line is red or amber.

Worked out from CC-176's daily totals on read, cached 5 minutes, `"<5"` applied. No email: CC-293 has not shipped.

### 9. Behind flags

`interruption_counts` (CC-312, everyone, client) for the events, and `console_ux_week` (admins) for the report.

## Non-goals

- **Changing what players see.** This ticket measures. It does not queue, delay or remove any pop-up, except that a
  test now fails when an optional blocking surface can open during play (section 5's red line, which should be 0
  already). Queueing optional interruptions is a follow-up ticket once there are two weeks of numbers.
- Any id, session, sequence across page loads, or text leaving the browser.
- Satisfaction surveys or "was this helpful?" prompts: they would be interruptions themselves.
- Ops alerts. Product numbers stay in the console; Grafana keeps ops (ADR-055).

## Rabbit holes

- **Finding every surface.** The source test is the safety net; start from the components named in Research and
  let the test list the rest.
- **What "covered" means on a phone.** Use the host's own record of what is visible, not layout geometry: a surface
  is covered when another one opened while it showed and it ended without an answer.
- **Counting words in Hinglish and English.** Count every visible word; both lines are what the player has to read.
- **Per game counts on the client.** A game is counted when the result shows on this page load; a game finished
  while the tab was hidden is counted on return, or not at all (`page_left`), and the report says so.

## Instrumentation

New events, counts tier only (ADR-034), no ids, no text, no URLs:

1. `interruption_ended`: `surface`, `kind`, `class`, `blocking`, `outcome`, `shown_ms` bucket, `phase`, `trigger`,
   `stack`, and optionally `flag` and `side`.
2. `interruption_counted`: `scope`, `game_id` (games only), `optional`, `blocking_optional` and `essential` buckets.
3. `screen_text_measured`: `words` and `fold_words` buckets (with the common `route`).
4. `funnel_step_reached`: `funnel`, `step`.
5. New fields on CC-174's events: `gameplay_started.interruptions_before`; `rage_clicked.surface`,
   `dead_clicked.surface`.

The flags' success events: an `interruption_ended` accepted by CC-175; the owner opened the Week view.

## Threat model

1. **What are we building:** more anonymous events about what the site showed and how it ended, a budget worked out
   nightly, a report for the owner and a CI rule for tickets.
2. **What can go wrong:** the text measure sends words (a name, a message); field combinations (surface, phase,
   flag side, device, route) single out a rare player on a small day; forged events paint a surface red so the owner
   removes something useful; the CI rule blocks urgent security work.
3. **What we do about it:** the text measure counts in the browser and sends two buckets, with a payload test;
   only closed values; `"<5"` and no colour for small shares; at most one flag side; CC-175's rate limits; the
   guardrail rule applies to `feature` tickets only, never `security` or `bug`.
4. **Good enough?** The new events say what the site did, not who the player is, and stay in our own database for
   30 days raw and 13 months as totals.

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** which surface showed, how it ended, how long, in which phase; word counts as buckets; steps reached;
  about every visitor, guest and account, with no id.
- **Retention:** raw 30 days (CC-175), daily totals 13 months (CC-176).
- **Who sees it:** the owner's console only, with `"<5"`; CI job summaries show only word counts of test screens.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** the same counts tier as everyone, no id, so nothing is linked to a minor; no new prompt is
  shown to anyone.
- **Defaults:** off with the CC-174 opt-out, GPC and DNT; the flags start off.

## Alternatives considered

- **PostHog surveys and its own interruption events:** PostHog captures `survey shown`, `survey dismissed` and
  `survey sent` with a person id, in a third party. We copy the idea (an event per outcome), not the service.
- **Count only shows, not outcomes:** cannot tell a helpful toast from an ignored one.
- **One event at open and one at close:** twice the volume for no new answer; the close event carries everything.
- **Enforce a hard cap now (queue every optional pop-up):** changes what players see before we know which surfaces
  cost anything; kept as the follow-up.

## Pre-mortem

| Risk                                                                                 | Mitigation                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Builders skip `useInterruption` and new pop-ups go uncounted                         | The source test fails on any toast push, `Modal` or banner without a registered surface                         |
| The budget is always red because the XP toast fires after every match                | It is optional and counted; if the first week says so, that is the finding, and the queueing follow-up uses it  |
| The friction guardrail becomes a box ticked with a meaningless threshold             | A fixed list of metrics to choose from, each with its flag on against off, and spec review checks the number    |

## Research (written before any code)

1. **Our code today.** Interruptions are spread over many components with no shared hook: `Toast/ToastProvider.jsx`
   (4 s, twice as long with an action), `GamificationToasts.jsx` and `ProgressToasts.jsx` (after every match),
   `SaveProgress/SaveProgressCard.jsx` (once per room, closing closes it for the visit) and `StreakAtRisk.jsx`,
   `ConnectionBanner/` (after 1 s), `RequestBanner/` and `SiteRequests/` (CC-207, more in PR #152), `TurnBanner/`,
   `WakingUp/` (CC-232), `Feedback/FeedbackDialog.jsx`, and `Modal` in seats, sound settings, pass and play, confirm
   leave, the RPS fairness lock and the chess promotion picker. None reports anything; CC-174 to CC-177 are not
   built. `data-cc-control` (CC-170) and the CC-311 decision gate exist; the CC-312 flags (PR #151) count exposures
   and successes per flag and side.
2. **Other products and research.**
   - Nielsen Norman Group, "Popups: 10 Problematic Trends and Alternatives" (Anna Kaley, 30 June 2019,
     [nngroup.com/articles/popups](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/popups/)): never show several pop-ups at once,
     not right after arrival or login, ask after a key task, never during one; prefer small nonmodal overlays,
     thin dismissible banners and on-page messages. Hence `phase`, `stack`, `covered` and the during-play lines.
   - Nielsen Norman Group, "Modal & Nonmodal Dialogs: When (& When Not) to Use Them" (Therese Fessenden, 23 April
     2017, [nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/)):
     modals interrupt and demand an action, are for critical warnings and information needed to continue, not for
     nonessential content, and repeated misuse trains people to dismiss them unread. Hence `blocking`, `class` and
     the dismissed share per surface.
   - Nielsen Norman Group, "How Little Do Users Read?" (Jakob Nielsen, 5 May 2008,
     [nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/)):
     people read about 20 % of a page's words, and half of it only on pages of 111 words or fewer. Hence word
     budgets per key screen, most under 120.
   - Google's HEART framework (Kerry Rodden, Hilary Hutchinson and Xin Fu, "Measuring the User Experience on a Large
     Scale: User-Centered Metrics for Web Applications", CHI 2010,
     [research.google](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-the-user-experience-on-a-large-scale-user-centered-metrics-for-web-applications/)):
     Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task success, each mapped from a goal to signals to metrics. Here
     the goal is "play without being in the way"; the signals are outcomes, stacks and steps; the metrics are the
     budget lines. Task success is time to first move and step drop-off; the friction guardrail is HEART's advice to
     pair every goal metric with one that would show harm.
   - Google Search Central, "Helping users easily access content on mobile" (August 2016): pop-ups that cover the
     main content right after arrival are treated as intrusive; legal notices, login dialogs and small banners are
     not. Hence "essential" is narrow and the home screen's first view has no optional interruption in the budget.
   - PostHog: autocapture's `$rageclick` (three clicks within 30 px and 1 s, configurable) and opt-in dead clicks
     ([docs](https://posthog.com/docs/product-analytics/autocapture), read 2026-10-20); PostHog's custom surveys
     capture `survey shown`, `survey dismissed` and `survey sent`, and a sent or dismissed survey is marked seen so it
     does not show again ([docs](https://posthog.com/docs/surveys/implementing-custom-surveys), read 2026-10-20).
     Shown and how it ended is the model for `interruption_ended`; "seen, do not show again" needs a per-person
     record, so our budget is per visit instead.
   - Plausible ([data policy](https://plausible.io/data-policy), updated September 2026; funnels of goals in
     [docs](https://plausible.io/docs/funnel-analysis)): no cookies, no persistent id, custom events and funnels
     built from counted goals. Our funnel registry is the same idea with no visitor hash at all.
3. **Best for ClassiCade.** A registry so every pop-up and step is named once, one event per interruption with how
   it ended, counts per visit and per game worked out in the browser, a small budget table with fixed colours, a
   CI-enforced friction guardrail in every feature ticket, and one weekly page for the owner. It answers the owner's
   two asks (monitor everything we add, add no friction) without one more pop-up and without any id.

3. **Outcomes.** A toast's link is `acted`, its close button `dismissed`, its time `timed_out`. A dialog closed with
   Escape or its backdrop is `dismissed`, closed by one of its buttons `acted`. Anything else that goes without a mark
   went by itself. `covered`: it ended without an answer while a surface opened after it still showed. `page_left` on
   `pagehide`.

   result shows on this page; pass and play is not a game here. During play a system-opened optional blocking surface
   throws in development; none exists (every optional blocking dialog is opened by the player's own press).

   budget (`e2e/textBudget.spec.js`, desktop project, both sizes, job summary) measured on 2026-10-21: home 25 above
   the fold, a game's page 61 (phone) and 72 (laptop), sign-up 39, room waiting 27, room playing 14 outside the board,
   **the result 69 and 74, over the owner's 60**. The result is listed in `OVER_BUDGET_TODAY` at 80 (its measured size and a few words for varying rewards; the test runs in Chromium only, as fonts wrap differently elsewhere), so
   the test fails only if it grows: a finding for the owner (trim the result screen, or set its budget).
