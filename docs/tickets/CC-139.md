Built together with the rest of CC-207 because both are prerequisites 4 and 5 of CC-052 (invites to friends,
part 2). The friends builder works in parallel on `cc-052-friends`. This pull request touches no friends code.
The brief was written before the CC-311 templates, so the coordinator dispatched it straight to Building, and the
open decisions below are the builder's defaults for the owner to change (ADR-069, "Accepted by default").

## Problem

Alice wants to play chess with Bob tonight. She creates a room and sends the link on WhatsApp at 7 pm. Bob opens it
at 8 pm and finds "Room closed", because a waiting room lives 30 minutes. If Bob had come while Alice was on the
home page, nothing would have told her. And if a rematch question reaches Alice while she is mid-game in another
room, it pops up over her board.

## Job to be done

When I want to play a particular friend who is not online right now, I want to send a link that still works when
they open it, so we end up in the same game without both watching the screen.

## Appetite

L. **Cut line:** the inbox stays minimal (no history, no badges beyond a count), and challenges to one person ship
as an API and domain rule only, reachable when CC-052 names friends.

## Proposed solution

See ADR-069 for every rule. In short:

1. The lobby's "Play a friend" panel gets **Make a challenge link**, with the same game options as a room. It opens
   `/challenge/:id`, where the creator gets the link, Send on WhatsApp, Copy link and Take back.
2. Whoever opens the link sees "A ClassiCade player challenges you to Chess" (the name only for an adult account),
   plays as a guest or logs in, and presses **Accept and play**. A room is made with both seated, and they land in
   it, waiting for the challenger.
3. The challenger, on any page, gets a popup "Bob took your Chess challenge" with **Go and play** and **Not now**.
4. A challenge popup left for about a minute, or Not now, moves to the **Inbox** in the header.
5. Popups, their sound and the tab alert wait while the player is in a game on the room page.
6. An expired, taken back, taken or unknown link gets an honest page with the way to play anyway.

## Non-goals

- Friends, presence for friends and "In a game" for the sender (CC-052 part 2).
- Push and email (CC-148, CC-293). A full notifications inbox with history (CC-147).
- Challenge again from history (CC-277). Showing the game's options on the challenge page.

## Rabbit holes

- **Presence of the challenger in the new room.** The room component cannot know about sockets. The room seats
  them offline and the gateway marks them away when a tab of theirs is connected (`markOnSite`).
- **Two people taking one link at once.** One conditional update decides; a failed room opens the challenge again.
- **Expiry sweeps.** None: expired is worked out from `expiresAt` on every read, and MongoDB's TTL index deletes.

## Instrumentation

None new. The routes write named log lines without ids beyond the actor's own (`challenge made`,
`challenge taken`, `challenge answered`), which the operations telemetry counts (ADR-055).

## Threat model

1. **Building:** a public, unguessable link that seats two people in a room, and pushes to named players.
2. **What can go wrong:** guessing ids (100 random bits, rate limits), leaking a player id (views name people by
   display name only, a crawl test), revealing a block (blocked reads as expired), spam (3 open, 10 minutes to one
   person, rate limits per player and address), a minor's name on a shared link (adults only).
3. **Done about it:** all of the above, with tests. Room limits still apply to the taker.
4. **Good enough:** the security reviewer checks it before merge (CLAUDE.md rule 8).

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** the `challenges` collection (DATA.md): the creator, their name, the game and options, who took it.
- **Retention:** two days, then MongoDB's TTL index deletes it. Account deletion deletes it at once.
- **Who sees it:** anyone with the link sees the game and the creator's name only for an adult account. The creator
  sees who took it. No player id anywhere. The owner.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** a minor's or guest's name never shows on a link. Challenges to one person wait for CC-052,
  which brings the minors' friends rules.
- **Defaults:** nobody can be named in a challenge to one person until CC-052.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep waiting rooms open for a day.** A room is a game in progress to many parts of the code (limits, presence,
  bots, sweeps). A challenge is a cheaper, separate promise that makes a room only when someone takes it.
- **Store "expired" with a sweep.** Needs a timer and a write per challenge. Working it out on read is exact.
- **Show the creator's name to everyone.** Breaks ADR-028.7 for minors and guests on a forwarded link.

## Research (written before any code)

### 1. How our code does it today

1. **Rooms are the only way to play a friend.** `roomComponent.createRoom` seats the host, and the room link
   (`/games/:slug/rooms/:code`) is the invite. A waiting room closes 30 minutes after its last activity
   (`roomLifetimes.js`), so a link sent on WhatsApp in the evening is dead by the morning. There is no record of
   "I challenged someone" outside a room.
2. **Requests live inside a room** (`domain/rooms/roomRequests.js`, ADR-019 amended): a rematch (60 s) and a chess
   draw offer (30 s). They are pushed on the player's channel as `request:updated` and drawn on any page by
   `components/SiteRequests` with "Accept and go" and "Decline" (CC-207, #67). The popup shows over a game in
   another room, so a player mid-game is interrupted. Nothing lasts long enough to need an inbox, and there is none
   (CC-147 is still Todo).
3. **Presence** is already three-way (here, away, offline). Leaving the room page sends `room:away` and the
   opponent's seat reads "away from the game screen" at once (`Seats.jsx`, `usePresenceNotice.js`,
   `e2e/presence.spec.js`). That part of CC-207 shipped in #67 and is kept as it is.
4. **The tab alert** (`useTabAlert`) flashes the title once a second whatever the player's reduced motion setting.
   The request sound goes through the sound player, which follows `cc.sound` and the request category's own switch
   (CC-208).
5. **Blocks** are silent (ADR-053). Everything that brings two players together asks `blockComponent.blockedWith`.
6. **Identifiers** (DATA.md, ADR-057 and CC-297): no client ever receives another person's player id. Profiles have
   a random 80-bit handle (ADR-065), which CC-052 will use to name a friend.
7. **Link previews** (ADR-042): the server writes each page's head from `describePage` in `@classicade/shared`, so
   WhatsApp, which runs no script, gets a title, a description and an image. Room heads never name a player.

### 2. How other products do it

1. **Lichess** ([lila `Challenge.scala`](https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/blob/master/modules/challenge/src/main/Challenge.scala),
   read 2026-10-20): a challenge has its own random 8-character id and its own page (`lichess.org/<id>`), separate
   from the game. Status is `created`, `offline`, `canceled`, `declined` or `accepted`. An open challenge (anyone
   with the link) expires one day after it is made, a challenge to one person after two weeks. A decline carries a
   reason (`generic`, `later`, `tooFast`, …).
2. **Lichess blocks** ([lila `ChallengeGranter.scala`](https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/blob/master/modules/challenge/src/main/ChallengeGranter.scala),
   read 2026-10-20): a challenge to someone who blocked you is refused up front with "You cannot challenge X". That
   tells the sender about the block. ADR-053 chose the opposite for us: a block must look like an ordinary no.
3. **Discord** invite links expire after 7 days by default, and an expired one opens an honest "Invite invalid,
   this invite may be expired" page with a way into the app instead of an error.
4. **Steam and Discord** show a game invite as a toast on whatever screen the friend is on, and never during a
   full-screen game: Steam's overlay holds notifications while a game runs in some modes, and Discord's "do not
   disturb" holds them. Both keep missed invites in a list.
5. **WhatsApp link previews** read the Open Graph tags of the HTML the server sends
   ([ogp.me](https://ogp.me/)), without running scripts, which is why ADR-042 writes heads on the server.

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **A challenge is its own record with its own random id**, like Lichess, so its link outlives any room. 100 random
   bits in lower case Crockford base 32 (20 letters, the profile handle alphabet), never a player id, a room code or
   a handle. Stored in MongoDB with a TTL index, so it survives restarts and is deleted by MongoDB after its time.
2. **24 hours** (CC-052 scenario 3), the same for an open link and for a challenge to one person. Lichess keeps a
   challenge to one person for two weeks, but our players are friends on WhatsApp, and a day-old invite is already
   stale.
3. **States:** open, accepted, declined, expired, withdrawn. Expired is worked out from the time on every read, so
   nothing has to sweep. Accepted means the room was made with both players seated: the one who accepted, and the
   challenger, away until they open it. The game starts when both are on the room page (CC-112's rule).
4. **The creator picks the game and its options** in the lobby, the same options a room has, checked by the engine.
5. **Blocks never show**, unlike Lichess. A blocked person who opens the link sees exactly the expired page, and
   accepting answers exactly what an expired challenge answers. A challenge to one person in a blocked pair is
   never delivered, so the sender sees it wait and then expire, like a challenge nobody answered.
6. **The page head never names anyone**, and the page names the challenger only when they are an adult account
   (ADR-028.7: a share never carries a minor's or a guest's name). Everyone else is "A ClassiCade player".
7. **Limits:** at most 3 open challenges per sender, at most one every 10 minutes to the same person (CC-052 rule 2),
   and rate limits per player and per network address on making, answering and reading.
8. **CC-207 finished:** the popup says "Accept and go" or "Not now". A challenge that waits about 60 seconds without
   an answer, or gets "Not now", moves to a small inbox under the header (the minimal CC-147). Rematch and draw
   requests end on their own within 60 seconds, so they never need the inbox. A player in a game on the room page
   is never interrupted: popups, sound and tab alert wait until the game ends. Under reduced motion the tab title
   changes once and does not flash.
9. **Ready for CC-052 part 2:** the domain already takes a challenge to one person (`to`), with its own delivery,
   inbox, decline and block rules. Who may be named is a policy passed in by the composition root
   (`recipients.resolve`), which today names nobody. CC-052 replaces it with "a friend, by profile handle".
