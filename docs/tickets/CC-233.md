## Why

The owner has no way to see whether anyone plays, comes back or signs up. Telemetry (CC-173 to CC-178) is weeks
away and needs a privacy decision first. Everything below can already be worked out from what the site stores for
gameplay: finished matches, guest transfers, accounts, achievements, quests and leaderboard entries. This gives the
owner the North Star (PRODUCT_VISION.md §7.1) and most input metrics (§7.2) now, with **no new tracking**.

## Metrics and why

### What the industry treats as standard

Sources: GameAnalytics and AppsFlyer benchmark reports already cited in PRODUCT_VISION.md §7.2 (mobile install
cohorts, 2022 to 2024); Wikipedia "Active users" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_users, fetched 2026-10-17:
DAU, WAU, MAU, DAU/MAU as a "rudimentary" engagement and retention estimate, 0.15 to 0.2 cited as healthy);
Poki's developer guide (https://developers.poki.com/guide/reading-results, fetched 2026-10-17: conversion to play
65%+, average playtime 5+ minutes); Wikipedia "Lichess" (fetched 2026-10-17: Lichess publishes games played per
time control, 5.6 billion by May 2024, and shows players and games in play on its home page). Other points are
general knowledge, medium confidence.

1. **Active users (DAU, WAU, MAU) and stickiness (average DAU / MAU)**: the universal headline in GameAnalytics,
   Firebase, Amplitude and Mixpanel game templates. Casual games are healthy at 15 to 20% stickiness, top titles
   above 25% (medium confidence).
2. **Cohort retention D1, D7, D30**: the core health check in every game analytics tool. Classic retention ("came
   back exactly on day N") is what GameAnalytics and AppsFlyer benchmark. Casual mobile medians are about 25 to 30%,
   8 to 12% and 3 to 6% (PRODUCT_VISION.md §7.2).
3. **New versus returning players**, and **sessions and games per player**: games per player is the closest we can
   get to sessions today.
4. **Session length and time on page**: needs telemetry (CC-174), so it is in "Coming next".
5. **Conversion**: in free web games "guest to account" plays the role of "install to registration". Funnels
   (visit to first move) need telemetry.
6. **Churn**: tools derive it from retention (players last seen more than N days ago). The cohort table shows it
   without a separate number.
7. **Chess sites** measure engagement as games played (per day and per time control), players online and games in
   play now, rated players and rating distribution, and puzzles solved. Lichess publishes games played. Chess.com
   publishes players online and games today on its home page (general knowledge, high confidence). We have no
   ratings or puzzles yet (CC-042), so games per game and the busy hours cover it.

### What the console shows, and why each one

All days, weeks and hours are India time (Asia/Kolkata, ADR-013/ADR-015 amendments). Weeks start on Monday.

| Page       | Metric                                                                                                                                        | Why                                                                                     |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Overview   | Headlines with the period before: active players, matches, new players, WRP, matches per player, guests converted, guest to account rate      | One glance answers "is it growing?"                                                     |
| Overview   | **Weekly returning players (North Star)**: a counted match (ADR-021) on 3+ days of a week, with the person share and account share guardrails | PRODUCT_VISION.md §7.1 (match part only: daily puzzle and story do not exist yet)       |
| Players    | Average daily actives, WAU, MAU (as of the range end), stickiness, guest share                                                                | Industry standard (1). Guests and accounts reported apart (§7.1 measurement limits)     |
| Players    | Active players and new players per day, week or month (first finished match ever)                                                             | Standard (1, 3). A player counts once per bar                                           |
| Games      | Matches per bucket by game, each game's share, median length, rematch rate                                                                    | Chess site style engagement (7). Rematch rate is our best "one more game" signal        |
| Games      | Who played whom (accounts only, guests only, both, with a bot)                                                                                | Social health. Bot matches are counted by `bot_` ids, so it lights up when CC-138 lands |
| Games      | Did not count share by reason (CC-095)                                                                                                        | Farming check, and a check that limits are not too tight                                |
| Retention  | Cohort table by first-play week: D1, D7, D30 return with ±95% margin and the eligible count                                                   | Standard (2), §7.2 definitions. Only players whose return day is over are counted       |
| Conversion | Guests whose games moved to an account, accounts created, guest to account rate                                                               | §7.2 "Guest to account". Rate = converted / (active guests + converted)                 |
| Engagement | Busiest hours heatmap (weekday × hour), top achievements, quests completed, board sizes                                                       | §6.0 evening hours check, gamification health, ADR-029 board gates (25 and 50 entries)  |
| All        | "Coming next": time on page, funnels, dead and rage clicks, errors, gates, circles, bot XP                                                    | Needs telemetry or features that do not exist yet (CC-173 to CC-178, CC-214, CC-138)    |

**Limits, said on the page.** A guest is a cookie, so one person on two devices counts twice until they sign in.
A guest's matches move to the account when they sign in (ADR-014), so after that they count as an account's.
Quest days are kept 8 days (TTL), so quest totals only cover the last 8 days. Room codes can be reused after a room
closes, so the rematch rate can very slightly overcount over long ranges.

## Privacy

1. Aggregates only. No pipeline returns a player id, name, email or room code: ids are grouped on inside MongoDB
   and only kinds ("guest", "user") and counts leave the database. The integration tests assert the JSON has no
   ids, names or codes.
2. Any count of people from 1 to 4 in any breakdown (and any share or median of fewer than 5) is replaced by `"<5"`
   on the server, shown as "fewer than 5" on screen and in CSV. The heatmap hides cells of 1 to 4 matches too.
   Totals could in theory be differenced against hidden cells; the console accepts that because only admins see it.
3. No new personal data is collected. Nothing is logged about who looked.
4. Minors and unknown ages (ADR-028): the console only ever shows aggregates, which is what §7.2 allows for them.

## Access

`ADMIN_ACCOUNT_IDS` (comma separated `user_…` ids, validated at boot, empty means nobody). Checked on the server for
every `/api/admin/*` request and every `/admin` page; everyone else gets the same 404 body as any unknown address.
`GET /api/session` adds `admin: true` only for admins, which the header uses for the **Console** link. The admin
page and API answer `Cache-Control: no-store` and `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`, and the page has
`<meta name="robots" content="noindex, nofollow">`. Owner steps: docs/DEPLOYMENT.md "Owner console".

## Design

1. `persistence/analyticsDao.js`: read-only aggregation pipelines. New indexes are declared by the owning DAOs
   (their `syncIndexes` would drop unknown ones): `matches.endedAt`, `player_transfers.createdAt`,
   `accounts.createdAt`, `player_achievements.unlockedAt`.
2. `data/analyticsAccessor.js` freezes rows. `domain/analytics/metrics.js` holds pure metric functions,
   `privacy.js` the small group rule, `analyticsComponent.js` builds the report and caches it 5 minutes per range
   (`utils/ttlCache.js`, which shares one piece of work between concurrent requests). India day maths live in
   `utils/indiaTime.js` (fixed +05:30, India has no summer time), shared with the `AnalyticsRange` model.
3. `service/http/adminAccess.js` (guard), `adminRoutes.js` (`GET /api/admin/analytics?from&to&bucket`, at most 400
   days), `webApp.js` serves `/admin` pages with 200 only to admins.
4. Web: `/admin` and `/admin/:section` via `paths.admin()`, lazy-loaded (`AdminRoute` loads the chunk only for
   admins), range and section in the URL so Back/Forward and bookmarks work. SVG charts with no new dependency
   (`charts.jsx`), colours from CSS classes (CSP: no inline styles), each with a screen reader summary, a table and a
   CSV export (`lib/csv.js`, formula-safe cells).

## Verification

Query timings, `node scripts/bin/bench-analytics.mjs` (50,000 seeded matches among 8,000 players over 180 days,
in-memory MongoDB on a heavily loaded laptop, median of 5):

| Read                                   | Median         |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| `listPlayerActivity` (every match)     | 395 to 430 ms  |
| `getMatchFacts`, 30 days               | 45 ms          |
| `getMatchFacts`, 365 days              | 255 ms         |
| sign ups, unlocks, quests, board sizes | 0 to 2 ms each |
| whole report, 30 days by day           | about 500 ms   |
| whole report, 365 days by week         | about 950 ms   |
| cached report                          | 0 ms           |

Range reads use the new `endedAt` index (IXSCAN). The per-player read scans every match by design (first match ever
and cohorts need all history). At about 500,000 matches it should move to the nightly rollup (CC-176).

Tests: unit tests for every metric (India day boundaries, guests vs accounts, cohorts, suppression), the range
model, cache, guard and routes; MongoDB integration tests for every pipeline (known answers, India midnight,
empty database equals the test double) and through the real server (admin vs non-admin vs guest 404, ranges,
suppression); browser tests in `e2e/owner.spec.js` (seeded week numbers, Back/Forward and reload, CSV download,
non-admin and guest 404). Screenshots: `/tmp/cc233-shots`.
