## Problem

A guest plays Tic-Tac-Toe on a laptop and the board is a 352 px square pushed to the left of a 770 px card; their
own marks are drawn in the opponent's colour; the only sign that it is not their turn is a pale grey pill. In chess
on a phone they see two clocks for one turn, their name and rating twice, and after the game the outcome twice,
about 1000 px apart. When they win at Rock Paper Scissors, "+30 XP" is drawn over "VICTORY!", confetti covers New
opponent, and seven actions of equal weight sit in one column (AUDIT-2026-10, RC-6, findings under "All three match
screens"). Each game sizes and frames its own board, and the result is a stack of independent components.

## Job to be done

When I am in a match, I want the board big and centred with the score, whose turn it is and the clock where my eyes
already are, and when it ends I want the outcome once beside the board and Rematch right under it, so I can read
the game at a glance and play again at once.

## Appetite

L (up to 2 days of agent time), in three PRs: (1) the stage frame, the scoreboard and the player strips for all three
games; (2) the result layout and pass and play; (3) the board polish (marks in player colours, turn on the board,
chess hints) and the browser tests. **Cut line**, dropped first in this order: the "View board" peek on phones (the
sheet stays up); the chess capture rings; the score bump animation. The centred board, one clock per player, the
turn on the board, the outcome once and the action order are never cut.

## Proposed solution

One `MatchStage` layout, owned by `components/MatchShell/`, for every game and for pass and play. Games plug in as
today through `GAME_VIEWS` and declare one new field, `fit: "square"` (chess, Tic-Tac-Toe) or `fit: "content"`
(Rock Paper Scissors). Nothing in a game draws its own frame, clock row, seat card or result card any more.

### The parts, top to bottom

1. **Scoreboard** (the match bar row, `data-cc-slot="scoreboard"`). Game icon, then `You 2 – 1 Riya` in
   `--type-section-title`, `--weight-heavy`, `tabular-nums`, the viewer's number in `--color-you` and the other in
   `--color-opponent`, each next to its name (never colour alone). Under it in `--type-caption`: `Game 4`, and for
   Rock Paper Scissors `Round 3 · first to 2`. When CC-161's `series.history` is present, up to five result dots
   follow the score (a list with one text label per dot, as CC-161 specifies) and `+N` beyond five. The sentence
   "Best of 1 · You 0 – Guest-22QG 0 · first to 1 wins" goes. A request banner takes this row while it is open
   (ADR-049 point 5), so the board never moves.
2. **Opponent strip** above the board, **your strip** below it (`PlayerStrip`, replacing the seat cards in a
   match). Each strip: the seat dot in its player colour, the name (one line, ellipsis), the chess rating badge
   (with "?" explained by its tooltip and `aria-describedby`: "Provisional: fewer than 10 rated games"), the
   captured pieces (chess), the turn label (`TurnBanner`, moved here, still `role="status"`) and **the one clock**
   for that player. Chess shows both players' game clocks, one per strip, and nothing else shows a clock. Rock
   Paper Scissors and Tic-Tac-Toe show the "choose" or "away" countdown only in the strip of the player it runs
   for. A spectator sees seat A and seat B in the seat order of the first game. The opponent strip's `⋯` button
   opens the existing menu with Add friend (secondary) and Block and Report (danger), so a social and a safety
   action are never look-alike links side by side.
3. **Board** (`data-cc-slot="board"`), centred in its column. The stage is a size container
   (`container-type: size`) and a square board is `inline-size: min(100cqi, 100cqb, var(--stage-board-max))` (the
   CC-172 rule, CSS only). `--stage-board-max` is a new space token: 40rem. A `content` board (RPS) takes the same
   width as a square board would at that size, and its own height. **Turn on the board:** the frame is a 2 px ring
   and glow in `--color-you` with `data-cc-turn="yours"`; on `theirs` the ring is `--color-border`, the glow goes,
   cells lose their hover preview and the board takes `filter: saturate(0.7)` (colour only, so every label keeps its
   AA contrast, the CC-163 rule). The word is always in the strip ("Your turn", "Riya's turn").
4. **Action bar** under your strip while the game is on: at most three buttons from `game.actions` and Leave, in
   CC-323's `Button` looks: Offer draw secondary, Resign **danger** (with CC-323's destructive confirm), Flip board
   and Leave ghost. On a phone it is the frame's last grid row, so it stays in reach without `position: fixed`, with
   `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` padding.
5. **Side column** (from 64rem): the game's `Panel` (the chess Moves list, which shows "No moves yet" in
   `--type-label` muted instead of an empty box with four disabled arrows) and a quiet room card (room code, who is
   watching). Under 64rem the Moves list folds into a "Moves (12)" disclosure under the action bar.

### The result layout

When the game ends, the board stays where it is with the deciding picture marked (the winning line, the checkmate
square, both throws), and the result goes **next to the board** in this fixed order:

1. **Outcome**: CC-163's stamp and headline in `--type-display` (`--type-title` under 30rem), then the line under it
   as a CC-332 two-language pair. Shown once: chess's `GameOver` panel is removed and its reason and score ("0–1,
   by resignation, you played Black") become the second line of this outcome. A game that did not count says so
   in the headline's line and takes the `quiet-win` tone with no burst (fixes "VICTORY!" over "This game didn't
   count").
2. **Beat slot**: one row, `3rem` high, reserved from the first frame, where CC-099's reward beats play one at a
   time. Until CC-099 lands it shows the XP line ("+30 XP") as text only.
3. **Rematch**: `primary`, `lg`, full width of the result column. Its window shows as `--type-caption` under the
   button ("Open for 42 s"), not as a separate row.
4. **New opponent**: `secondary`, full width.
5. **Quiet row**: `ghost` `sm` buttons on one line: Share (opens the share sheet, or WhatsApp where there is no Web
   Share), Leave room. The share caption preview and the WhatsApp row leave the result; the caption is only in the
   share sheet. Leave appears once on the screen.
6. **Summary**: CC-099's summary list and its save-progress beat. Until CC-099 lands, `SaveProgressCard` sits here
   and nowhere else (it leaves the space under the board).

Tones (CC-163): `win` takes CC-329's `--gradient-win` surface, `--color-gold-bright` border and `--glow-gold`;
`quiet-win` the same without the glow; `gentle-loss` `--color-surface-raised` with no red and Bit's thinking face
when CC-153b lands; `even` the primary look. **One standout effect:** a real win calls `play("win_burst")` (CC-328)
once; nothing else on the screen animates while it plays. Rewards never draw over the outcome or the buttons: they
go through CC-099's queue into the beat slot (CC-315 routes reward notices there while the result is shown).

Where "next to the board" is: from 64rem the result replaces the top of the side column, level with the board's top
edge. Under 64rem it is a **non-modal bottom sheet** inside the frame (ADR-049 point 5, CC-172 research): it rises
over the lower part of the stage, the board stays visible above it, and "View board" lowers it to a one-line peek
holding the headline and Rematch. Escape lowers it; it never takes focus from a request banner.

### Pass and play

The same stage. The two strips are named players, never "You": seat A wears `--color-you` and seat B
`--color-opponent`, each with its mark ("Ravi · X"). The handover line ("Ravi's turn") is shown once, in Ravi's strip.
The result is the same layout with `Play again` (primary), `Change players` (secondary) and a quiet `Leave`; a win
on the board gets the `win` tone and the burst, the "Not counted" note shows once as the outcome's second line.

### Marks in player colours

- Tic-Tac-Toe: the viewer's marks are drawn in `--color-you`, the opponent's in `--color-opponent`, whatever the
  letter; the mark's shape still says X or O and the strip says "You · X". The winning line is `--color-gold-bright`
  with a 2 px `--color-art-ink` outline (3:1 against the cell in both themes, WCAG 1.4.11); the losing marks keep
  their colour at full strength (no dull purple that reads as disabled).
- Rock Paper Scissors: your hand gets a `--color-you` ring and theirs a `--color-opponent` ring; the hands use the
  neon game inks on a `--color-surface-raised` well, not flat brown on lavender.
- Chess: the pieces stay white and black (the game's own colours); the strips' seat dots carry the player colours.
  The board uses CC-329's board tokens; legal moves are a `--color-board-hint` dot with a light ring, captures a
  ring on the target square, both visible on both square colours.

## Rabbit holes

- **Measuring the board in script.** The board size is CSS only (size container, `min()` of the container units);
  no `ResizeObserver`, no inline style (CSP, ADR-012).
- **A second result system for pass and play.** `PassAndPlayShell` renders the same `ResultLayout` with its own
  actions as data; no copy of the layout.
- **Re-laying the board at the result.** The board never changes size when the result shows: on phones the sheet
  overlays, on desktop the side column changes. A test checks the board's box before and after.
- **Seat card features scattered across strips.** Everything the seat card did today (away, connected, ratings,
  add friend, block) is listed in the strip's spec above; nothing else is moved.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** Given a playing room of each game at 320, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px wide, when the stage renders, then
  the board's box is centred in its column (left and right gaps within 1 px) and its side equals the table's value
  within 2 px. Test: `e2e/match-stage.spec.js` › "the board is centred and sized to the viewport for every game"
- **AC-2** Given a room in play, when the viewer looks at the scoreboard, then it shows both names with their
  numbers and the game number, and the old "Best of" sentence is gone. Test: `components/MatchShell/Scoreboard.test.jsx`
  › "shows both scores by name with the game number"
- **AC-3** Given a chess game, when it runs, then exactly two `role="timer"` elements are on the page, one in each
  strip, and no seat card shows "thinking". Test: `e2e/match-stage.spec.js` › "one clock per player, in its strip"
- **AC-4** Given it is the viewer's turn, when the board renders, then it has `data-cc-turn="yours"` and the strip
  says "Your turn"; on the opponent's turn it has `theirs`, no cell has a hover preview, and the opponent's strip
  names them. Test: `components/MatchShell/PlayerStrip.test.jsx` › "the turn is on the board and in words"
- **AC-5** Given a Tic-Tac-Toe game, when either player places a mark, then the viewer's marks use the `you` role
  and the opponent's the `opponent` role, whichever letter each plays. Test: `games/tictactoe/TicTacToeBoard.test.jsx`
  › "marks wear the player colours, not the letter colours"
- **AC-6** Given a finished game of each kind, when the result shows, then the outcome headline is on the page
  exactly once, followed in DOM and visual order by the beat slot, Rematch, New opponent and a quiet row of Share
  and Leave, and Leave appears once on the page. Test: `components/MatchShell/ResultLayout.test.jsx` › "outcome once,
  then Rematch, New opponent and the quiet row"
- **AC-7** Given a finished game, when the result appears, then the board's bounding box does not change (within
  1 px) at every width in the table. Test: `e2e/match-stage.spec.js` › "the board does not move when the result shows"
- **AC-8** Given a real win, when the result shows, then `play("win_burst")` is called once, and for a game that did
  not count or a forfeit it is not called. Test: `components/MatchShell/ResultLayout.test.jsx` › "only a real win
  asks for the burst"
- **AC-9** Given a 390 × 844 phone, when the result sheet is up, then Rematch is fully inside the viewport, at most
  two lines sit above it in the sheet, and "View board" lowers the sheet to a peek that still holds Rematch. Test:
  `e2e/match-stage.spec.js` › "the phone sheet keeps Rematch in reach"
- **AC-10** Given pass and play, when a game is on and when it ends, then the strips name each player with their

- **AC-11** Given a chess result, when it shows, then no separate game-over panel is rendered and the reason and

## Research (written before any code)

1. **How our code does it today.** `MatchShell.jsx` and `MatchFrame.jsx` (CC-157) give every game a match bar, a
   stage, an action bar, a result card above the stage and a side column with `Seats`. Boards size themselves:
   `TicTacToeBoard.module.css` fixes about 352 px, chess sizes by width, RPS by content. Chess adds `PlayerBar`
   clocks and a `GameOver` panel inside its board; seat cards also show a "thinking" countdown. The result card
   holds the action bar and `ShareResult`; `SaveProgressCard` sits under the stage. Audit evidence:
   `playing-tictactoe 1280-light`, `playing-chess 390-light`, `result-chess 390-light`, `result-rps-win 390-light`.
2. **How others do it.** lichess (lichess.org, read 2026-10-18 for CC-172): the board is `min(width, height)` of
   its area, clocks sit in player strips above and below the board on mobile, and the result is a small panel
   beside the board with Rematch first. chess.com's mobile game screen (general knowledge, high confidence): one
   clock per player strip, the game review sheet slides over the lower board. Board Game Arena (CC-172 sources): at
   most four action buttons. NN/g on modal dialogs (Fessenden, 2017): news is not a dialog. WCAG 1.4.1, 1.4.10 and
   1.4.11 set the colour-alone, reflow and graphic contrast rules used above.
3. **What is best for ClassiCade.** One stage for every game, sized in CSS only, so a new game (Ludo) plugs in with
   `fit` and nothing else. The result beside the board on wide screens and as a sheet on phones keeps the deciding
   picture visible and Rematch within a thumb's reach, which serves the series loop that brings players back. The
   decisions in the table follow from that.
