# CC-337 — Challenges made in the same millisecond can slip under the limit of 3 and the ten-minute rule

## Problem

A player can have 4 or more challenges open at once, not 3 (`CHALLENGE_LIMITS.openPerSender`), when they make them
in the same millisecond. In the same way, they can send two challenges to one person less than ten minutes apart
(`samePersonEveryMs`). Found while fixing CC-336.

### Root cause

`challengeComponent.create` stored the new challenge and then read the sender's challenges back. It sorted them
by `createdAt` and then by id, and refused the new one only when it was not among the first 3 open. Two
challenges made in the same millisecond have the same `createdAt`, so only their random ids order them. A
challenge made after three others, one after another or at the same moment, could sort ahead of an older one by
its id, count as within the limit, and stay, while the older one, already let through, stayed too. With 2 open, A
could also read the list before B was stored, and B could then sort ahead of A. On the real MongoDB DAO, the new
test made 4 open one after another in the same millisecond, and 13 at once ended with 4 open. The rule of one
challenge to the same person every ten minutes had the same shape: it kept the challenge that sorted first. A
second challenge to Arjun in the same millisecond, with a lower id, was made, and 6 at once made 2.

## Fix

Each open challenge holds one of its sender's 3 places for open challenges, `openSlot` 0 to 2, and a unique
partial index on `{ fromId, openSlot }` (`open_slot`) means only one challenge can hold each place. The limit now
holds in one write, whatever the order of reads and writes. In the same way, a challenge to one person is stored
with `recentTo: true`, and a unique partial index on `{ fromId, toId }` (`recent_to`) lets only one challenge from a
sender to a person hold the mark. The mark is let go once the challenge is ten minutes old, even if it was taken
back, as the rule always said.

- `challengeDao.insert` reports which key it hit: `DuplicateKey` with `field` "challengeId", "openSlot" or
  "recentTo". `freeSlots({ fromId, at, recentBefore })` lets go of the places of the sender's challenges that can
  never be open again: expired, declined, withdrawn, or taken with their room made. A hold whose room is not made
  yet keeps its place, since `release` opens it again if the room cannot be made. It also lets go of the marks of
  challenges made at `recentBefore` or earlier. `movePlayer` moves a guest's places and marks to the account. When
  the account holds one already, the challenge gives up its mark first, then its place.
- `challengeAccessor.add(challenge, { openSlots, recentMs })` lets go of the finished places and old marks, then
  tries each place in turn. It throws `ConflictError` with `details.field` "openSlot" when every place is held, or
  "recentTo" when the person was challenged less than `recentMs` ago. The component turns these into the usual
  409s, `open-challenges`, or `same-person` with `retryAt`.
- The component no longer decides either rule by sorting. After the write it still counts the sender's stored
  challenges: it refuses a new one when more than 3 are open, or when another challenge to the same person is
  under ten minutes old. Only a challenge stored without a place or mark can trip these checks, as described
  below.
- The in-memory DAO does the same, under the same contract tests.

**Research.** How our code does it now: every other once-only rule in the challenge DAO is a conditional update
(`claim`, `end`, `setRoom`), so two takers can never both win (ADR-069). The open limit was the only rule decided
by reading and sorting after the write. Options: a per-player counter with a conditional `$inc` would also need a
`$inc` back on every way a challenge stops being open, and expiry happens with no write at all, so the counter
would drift. A unique index on a fixed set of places makes the database refuse a second holder. Expired places are
let go when the next one is made, and that can never be wrong, because an expired challenge never opens again.
This follows MongoDB's guidance for uniqueness under concurrency: a unique index, not a check before or after the
write (https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/core/index-unique/, https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/core/index-partial/).

**Challenges already stored at deploy.** Open challenges made before this change have no `openSlot` and no
`recentTo`, so they hold no place and no mark. The checks after the write count them anyway, from the stored
challenges. A sender with 3 such open challenges gets a place for a new one, then the count finds 4 open, and the
new one is removed and refused with `open-challenges`. A sender who challenged Arjun 5 minutes before the deploy
is refused a new challenge to him with `same-person` and the right `retryAt`. Both cases are tested in
`challengeComponent.test.js` ("challenges stored before CC-337"). Those checks are reads after the write, so in
the first day after the deploy, two challenges made at the same moment next to old ones can both be refused.
Neither can get past a limit. Challenges stay open for a day, so after a day every open challenge holds a place.
The same checks cover a guest's challenges that gave up their place or mark when the guest signed in to an
account. No migration is needed.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** Given a player with 3 open challenges, when they make a fourth in the same millisecond, then it is
  refused with 409 `open-challenges`, and 3 stay open.
- **AC-2** Given a player with 1 open challenge, when they make 12 at once, then exactly 2 are made and 3 are open.
- **AC-3** Given a player at the limit, when one of their challenges is withdrawn or expires, then they can make
  one more, and never more than 3 are open.
- **AC-4** Given a challenge to Arjun, when the same player sends him another in the same millisecond, or 6 at
  once, then only one is made, and the others are refused with `same-person` until ten minutes have passed, even
  if the first was taken back.
- **AC-5** Given open challenges stored before this change, when their sender makes a new one, then the limit of
  3 and the ten-minute rule still count them.

## Test

`challengeLimit.integration.test.js` runs the component on the real MongoDB DAO, with ids that sort each new
challenge ahead of the older ones. Before the fix the three limit tests failed: the fourth was made, 13 at once
gave 4 open, and expiry left 4 open. Two of the three same-person tests failed too: a second challenge to Arjun in
the same millisecond was made, and 6 at once made 2. After the fix all six pass. The DAO contract (MongoDB and
in-memory) covers the unique places and marks, `freeSlots` and `movePlayer`. The accessor and component tests cover
a full set of places, a recent mark, an account over the limit after a guest signed in, and challenges stored
before this change. `challengeDao.js`, `challengeAccessor.js` and `challengeComponent.js`
have every line and function covered.
