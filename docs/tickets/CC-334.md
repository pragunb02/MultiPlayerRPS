## Problem

Riya, 14, reports Bob for the third time this week: he joins every link room she shares and stalls each game until
she gives up. The owner opens `/admin/reports`, sees six reports from four people, and can only dismiss them or
rename Bob to `Player_K7QM2X`. The rename does nothing about the stalling: Bob signs in again under the new name and
goes on. The console has no way to take an account off the site for a while (CC-044 future work 1, ADR-071.6).

## Job to be done

When a player keeps breaking the rules after the reports are in, the owner wants to take their account off the site
for a set time or until lifted, in a few presses and without telling anyone who reported, so the people they hurt
can play in peace and the player has a clear way back.

## Appetite

M (up to 6 agent-hours). **Cut line:** the "Suspended now" list on the reports page goes first (lifting then needs
the group's history), then the export for a suspended account goes to a follow-up.

## Proposed solution

On a group of `/admin/reports` the owner presses **Suspend**, picks 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days or until lifted and a
reason from a closed list, and confirms. The server writes one record to a new `account_suspensions` store, ends
every session of the account through its session version (ADR-030.4), cuts its sockets, makes it leave every open
room (a running game is forfeited to the opponent), withdraws its open challenges, friend requests and friend
links, and closes the group's open reports as acted on. Friends see it offline, its leaderboard rows are hidden
while the suspension lasts, and nothing is deleted. Signing in with the right password answers a neutral message
with the end date and an appeal line to the feedback form. A **History** button on the group lists the player's
suspensions, and a **Suspended now** list lifts one early. Detail in ADR-078 and the research below.

## Non-goals

- Banning guests or network addresses (research §3.5): a suspended player can still play as a guest or make a new
  account. Documented, not built.
- Telling the reporter anything, or the suspended player who reported.
- Automatic suspension from report counts (ADR-071.4: a person decides).
- A moderator role (CC-059): the owner's allowlist only (ADR-036.1).
- An appeal workflow inside the product: the appeal is the existing feedback form.

## Rabbit holes

- Rewriting leaderboard rows or ratings: rows are hidden at read time and never rewritten, so lifting or the end of a
  suspension needs no job. Opponents' ratings are not refunded (Lichess does for cheaters, research §2.1): a
  follow-up for CC-042 if the owner wants it.
- A restricted "suspended session" that can only export or delete: not built. A suspended account exports with its
  email and password in one request instead (decision 7).
- Escalating durations (Lichess playbans): the owner picks the duration; the history shows what came before.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** Given an open report group about an account, when the admin suspends it for 24 h, 7 days, 30 days or
  until lifted with a reason from the closed list, then a suspension is stored with the admin, the time and the
  reason, the group's open reports close as acted on, and anything else in the body, a guest, an admin account or
  a second suspension is refused. Test: `service/http/suspensionRoutes.test.js` › "suspends the account of a report
  for each duration with a closed reason, and closes its open reports"; "refuses another duration, a free text
  reason, a guest, an admin account and a second suspension"
- **AC-2** Given a suspension, when it starts, then every session of the account ends at once (the session version)
  and its sockets are cut. Test: `suspensionRoutes.test.js` › "ends every session of the account at once and cuts
  its sockets"
- **AC-3** Given a suspended account, when it signs in with the right password, then the answer is 403 with the end
  date (or until lifted) and nothing about reports, and a wrong password answers the usual 401. When it ends or is
  lifted, signing in works. Test: `suspensionRoutes.test.js` › "refuses sign-in with the end date only after the
  right password, and lets the player in once it ends or is lifted"; `pages/LoginPage/LoginPage.test.jsx` › "shows
  a suspended account the end date and the appeal line to the feedback form"
- **AC-4** Given a suspended account sitting in open rooms, when it is suspended, then it leaves each one as any
  player leaves: a running game is forfeited to the opponent. Test: `domain/rooms/roomComponent.suspension.test.js`
  › "takes the player out of every open room as a leave: a running game is forfeited, a waiting seat is given up"
- **AC-5** Given its open challenges, friend requests and friend links, when it is suspended, then they end, and its

- **AC-6** Given a suspended account on a leaderboard, when anyone reads the board or their standing, then its rows

- **AC-7** Given a suspended account, when it asks for its export with its email and password, then it gets its
  data, its own suspensions without who acted; nothing was deleted. Test: `suspensionRoutes.test.js` › "exports a

- **AC-8** Given the console, when the admin opens a group's history or the list of current suspensions and lifts

## Instrumentation

None. The owner reads the history in the console. A later ADR-034 count would be suspensions per week by duration
and reason, with no ids.

## Threat model

Building: an owner-only tool that takes an account off the site. Can go wrong: someone else reaching it (the
console's allowlist and 404 on every route, tested); the owner suspending the wrong player (a confirmation naming
the player and the duration, a lift that works at once); the player learning who reported (no report, reason or
id in what they see); the reporter learning the outcome (no event, view or export changes for them); a session
outliving the suspension (the version is counted up after the record is written, so a sign-in racing it is
refused at its next request); the owner locking themself out (admin accounts refused); evasion as a guest or a new
account (accepted gap, §3.5). Good enough for one moderator; revisit with CC-059 roles.

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** the suspended account's id (server only), its name then, the duration, a closed reason, start, end,
  lift, and the admin's id and name. No free text.
- **Retention:** 180 days after it ended or was lifted (TTL on `deleteAt`); while it lasts, kept. Account deletion
  removes it.
- **Who sees it:** the owner in the console; the suspended player sees the end date at sign-in and their own
  suspensions (without who acted) in their export; nobody else, never the reporter; logs carry the admin's id, the
  suspension id, the duration and the reason, never the player's name.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** the same tool and the same plain message for every age; nothing public names a suspended
  player (no "closed for fair play" label as chess.com shows), the rows just leave the boards. See §3.6.
- **Defaults:** only the owner can suspend; nothing for players to switch.

## Alternatives considered

- A `suspendedUntil` field on `accounts`: one read fewer at sign-in, but it would change a production collection,
  and the history and audit need a store of their own anyway. The new store is the one source of truth.
- The deletion stand-in in rooms (ADR-048.3): it would show "Deleted player", which is untrue and tells the opponent,
  who may be the reporter, that something happened to the account. A plain leave looks like any leave.
- Marking leaderboard rows: needs a job when a suspension ends. Hiding at read time needs none.
- IP or network bans: refused, §3.5.

## Research (written before any code)

Sources were read on 2026-10-21 unless another date is given. "General knowledge" carries a confidence level.

### 1. How our code does it today (`main` at `48f1511`)

1. **The console** (`/admin/reports`, ADR-071.6) groups open reports by player, named by the newest report's id,
   never a player id, and offers Dismiss and Rename. `reportComponent.act` closes the group's open reports up to the
   one shown. Each action logs the admin's id, the report id and the action.
2. **Ending sessions.** Each account has a `sessionVersion`; every `/api` request and socket handshake compares it
   with one read, and an old session goes on as a visitor (ADR-030.4). `accountAccessor.bumpSessionVersion` is
   the one write behind a password change, sign out others and the rename. `gateway.disconnectPlayer` cuts every
   socket of a player (CC-035, and the rename through `REPORT_EVENTS.PLAYER_RENAMED`).
3. **Sign-in** is `accountComponent.login`: one scrypt and one bcrypt whatever the email, the same 401 for every
   miss (CC-245), then `establishAccountSession` with the version read in the same account read.
4. **Rooms.** `roomComponent.erasePlayer` hands each seat to a `deleted_` stand-in that then leaves (ADR-048.3).
   `leaving` forfeits a running game, gives up a waiting seat and ends a finished room's rematch with
   `rematch.leftName` (CC-162).
5. **Challenges** (ADR-069) end as withdrawn, declined, accepted or expired. **Friends** (ADR-066): requests are
   withdrawn by their sender; friend links are kept by their maker; presence lives in memory and is offline when a
   player has no open tab (`presenceTracker.js`).
6. **Leaderboards** read the top rows and then drop moved guests (`getBoard`); standing counts the rows ahead.
7. **Export and deletion** (ADR-048) run participants in the composition root's order. Export needs a session and
   the password.
8. **Ids.** No client gets another person's player id (DATA.md Identifiers, ADR-057); tests crawl answers with
   `hasPlayerIdShape`. The admin's own account id is a player id too, so the console cannot show it either.
9. **No suspended state exists anywhere**, and `accounts` must stay compatible; the legacy `users` and its
   collections are never written (CLAUDE.md History).

### 2. How others do it

1. **Lichess** (source, https://github.com/lichess-org/lila, `modules/playban/src/main/PlaybanApi.scala`, read
   2026-10-21): temporary play bans (`TempBan` with minutes) for aborting, sitting and rage quits, kept in a capped
   history of the last 30 per account, checked by `currentBan`/`hasCurrentPlayban` only while `inEffect`, so they
   end by themselves; repeats lengthen them, and a severe pattern closes the account. Moderator closures and
   marks (`engine`, `boost`, `troll` for shadow muting) are separate, and Lichess refunds the rating points that
   opponents of a cheater lost (general knowledge, high confidence). A marked player's games and data stay.
2. **Chess.com** (https://www.chess.com/fair-play, read 2026-10-21): a fair play violation closes the account "and
   any of their ongoing games are awarded to their opponents"; closed accounts may be labelled publicly "closed for
   Fair Play violation(s)"; appeals are reviewed by the team (about 28,000 in early 2025, 0.2 % granted). Abuse
   leads to mutes and temporary suspensions (general knowledge, medium confidence). The reporter is not told who
   was closed beyond a generic "action taken" notice (general knowledge, medium confidence).
3. **Santa Clara Principles 2.0** (https://santaclaraprinciples.org/, read 2026-10-21): a suspended user should be
   told why (the rule broken, how it was detected, by category such as "user flags"), timely, in their language,
   with how to appeal, and that notice should stay reachable while suspended; appeals should be human, by someone
   not in the first decision, with a timeline. The principles name flaggers only by category, never by person.
4. **EU Digital Services Act** (Regulation 2022/2065, general knowledge, high confidence; EUR-Lex did not render to
   the fetcher): Art. 17 a statement of reasons for any suspension (the facts, the rule, the duration, the redress);
   Art. 20 an internal complaint system for at least six months after the decision; Art. 23 suspension "for a
   reasonable period of time and after having issued a prior warning" for frequent manifestly illegal content.
   Not law for us, but the clearest public standard for proportional, time-limited suspension.
5. **Digital Trust & Safety Partnership best practices** (https://dtspartnership.org/best-practices/, read for
   CC-044 on 2026-10-20): remedies for those actioned, appeals, records.
6. **India, IT Rules 2021, Rule 3(1)** (general knowledge, high confidence; see CC-044 §2): an intermediary tells
   users that breaking its rules can end their access, keeps the records 180 days, and answers grievances within 15
   days. **DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025** (https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules.html, read for CC-044): the right of
   access (s. 11), correction and erasure (s. 12), grievance redress (s. 13), and for children no tracking,
   behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising (s. 9(3)).
7. **IP bans** (general knowledge, high confidence): most Indian mobile users sit behind carrier-grade NAT, and
   schools and colleges share one address, so one address is often hundreds of people; IP bans are easily evaded
   with mobile data or a VPN. Large platforms use device signals or phone numbers instead, which we do not collect.

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **One store, one source of truth.** `account_suspensions`: `suspensionId` (random), `playerId` (server only),
   `nameThen`, `duration`, `reason`, `startedAt`, `endsAt` (`null` until lifted), `byId` and `byName`, then
   `liftedAt`, `liftedById`, `liftedByName`, and `deleteAt` (180 days after the end or the lift; none while a
   suspension until lifted lasts). Nothing in `accounts` changes. The record is written first and the session
   version counted up after, so a sign-in that read the account in between gets a session that the next request
   ends, and one that reads after sees the record.
2. **Owner actions.** Suspend a report group's account by the report's id (never a player id), with one of four
   durations and one of four closed reasons (decision 1). Lift by the suspension's own id. History per group by the
   report's id, and the current suspensions as one list. The admin is named by their account name, never their id.
3. **Effect, in order.** Write the record; end every session (`bumpSessionVersion`); cut the sockets (an event, as
   the rename does); then the followers, each one best effort and logged: rooms (`removePlayer`: leave as
   themselves, decision 4), challenges (withdraw their own open ones), friends (withdraw their sent requests,
   remove their links). Friends see offline because the tabs are gone. Leaderboards hide rows at read time
   (decision 6). The group's open reports close as acted on, with action `suspend`, so the reporters stay trusted
   (ADR-071.5).
4. **Sign-in refusal.** After the right password only (decision 3): 403 `FORBIDDEN`, `details: { reason:
"suspended", endsAt }`, and the page writes "Your account is paused until …" (India time) or "until the
   ClassiCade team lifts it", with "If you think this is a mistake, tell us through the feedback form" and a
   button that opens it. The reason is not shown (decision 2): with one moderator and many children, "harassment"
   on a sign-in screen can be read by a parent or sibling as an accusation and points at what happened in one
   room. It is in the player's export, which Santa Clara asks for, and the owner gives it on appeal.
5. **Guests.** Suspension needs an account: a guest has no password to be refused at, and their id dies with
   their cookie. The address-level options are written down, not built: (a) the existing per-address limits on
   guests, rooms and reports (ADR-035); (b) a network hash denylist for a day (the daily keyed hash of CC-107),
   which would refuse a whole school or carrier block; (c) device or phone signals, which we do not collect and
   will not for children. IP bans are refused for now (research §2.7). A suspended player who plays as a guest is
   still blocked by everyone who blocked their account (blocks name the account), and can be reported again.
6. **Minors and DPDP.** The same tool for every age, applied by a person, never by counts. A suspension is a
   safety measure on a rule break, not tracking or behavioural monitoring (s. 9(3)), and it keeps only a closed
   reason and dates. The message is short, plain, does not shame, and offers the appeal. Nothing public labels the
   player. The player keeps the right of access (the export, decision 7) and correction (the appeal); erasure
   waits until the suspension ends or goes through the appeal (decision 8), so deleting cannot be used to come
   back at once under the same email. The record is kept 180 days after it ends (the IT Rules record period, as
   reports), then removed. Before 13 May 2027 the lawyer checks the grievance path for children with the parental
   consent step (ADR-028.9).
7. **Privacy of reporters.** No socket event, push, view, log line or export field changes for any reporter; the
   reports page does not show reporters; the suspended player sees no report, reason, count or name. Logs name the
   admin, the suspension and the reason, never a reporter.
