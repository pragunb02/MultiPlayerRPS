## Research (written before any code)

### 1. How our code does it today (`main` at `bf7e832`, read 2026-10-20)

1. **Writing.** `domain/leaderboards/leaderboardComponent.js` writes `leaderboard_entries` from two places: the
   match pipeline's last step (`applyMatch`) and quest bonuses (`applyBonus`, through `connectLeaderboardBonuses`
   in `server.js`). Both start with `isAccountId(playerId)` (`models/identity.js`), so a guest is never written
   (ADR-015.1). There are two writes and both can only raise a score: `raiseScore` for totals (all time XP from the
   stored progress, wins from `matchDao.countWins`) and `addOnce` for this week's XP, keyed by match id or quest key
   (the last 200 keys are kept on the entry). Games against a computer never reach a board (ADR-025), and a game
   that does not count for a player (`creditFor`, ADR-021) moves them nowhere.
2. **Reading.** `getBoard` lists the top 50 (`LEADERBOARD_MAX_ENTRIES`) in one total order: score, then the
   earlier `updatedAt`, then the lower player id (index `{board, period, score: -1, updatedAt: 1, playerId: 1}`).
   Player ids never leave the server. Every name goes through `ages.adultsAmong`: anyone who is not surely an adult
   is shown as "Hidden player" with `hidden: true` (ADR-048.10). `adultsAmong` ignores ids that are not accounts, so
   a guest would come back as "not an adult" and be hidden. `standingFor` answers `{ listed: false, reason: "guest" }`
   for every guest, and the web app shows "Guests aren't ranked. Sign up".
3. **The guest transfer.** `transferPlayer` is the last ADR-014 participant. Because guests had no rows, it only
   recomputes the account's rows: all time XP from the merged progress, wins from the moved matches, and this
   week's XP from this week's moved matches (`matchXpSince`). ADR-015's consequences name the gap: a guest's quest
   XP from this week is not added to the account's weekly score.
4. **Names.** `identityComponent.createGuest` gives `Guest-` and four characters from a 32 letter alphabet without
   0/O/1/I, drawn again when the draw is reserved or offensive (ADR-022). Account usernames are
   `^[A-Za-z0-9_]{3,20}$` (`models/credentials.js`), so no account can be called `Guest-XXXX`: the hyphen tells the
   two apart. Nothing lets a guest choose or change their name today.
5. **Lifetimes.** A guest session lives 30 days after the last visit (`SESSION_TTL_SECONDS`, ADR-030.3). Guest data
   in the other stores is kept for ever until CC-098 (not built) removes it. The collection already has a TTL index
   on `expiresAt` (weekly rows: the week's end plus 28 days).
6. **Deletion and export.** `playerDataComponent` runs `leaderboardComponent.erasePlayer` under the account id and
   under every guest id the player had (ADR-048.2), and `exportPlayer` lists the account's rows.
7. **Caching.** There is no server cache for boards. The board response carries no `Cache-Control` header, and with
   a session it carries `you`, which belongs to one person.
8. **Abuse limits today.** 30 guest sessions per 15 minutes per address (`sessionRoutes.js`), 10 sign-ups an hour
   per address, 5 connections per session and 30 per address, 3 waiting rooms per player and 30 open rooms per
   network (ADR-035). Per player at most 20 counted games per clock hour and 10 per opponent in any 24 hours, and a
   game that ended too soon never counts (ADR-021).

### 2. How other products do it (read 2026-10-20)

1. **Unity Gaming Services.** Anonymous sign-in "creates a new player for the game session without any input from
   the player", and Unity recommends linking it to a real identity later "to allow your players to continue their
   progress" (https://docs.unity.com/ugs/en-us/manual/authentication/manual/use-anon-sign-in). A player with no name
   gets one: "If no player name is found for the player, a random name is returned and assigned"
   (https://docs.unity.com/ugs/en-us/manual/authentication/manual/player-name-management). Anonymous players with
   generated names, whose data follows them to the account they link, is the pattern of a large game backend. It is
   the same shape as ours.
2. **Lichess.** Its leaderboards need 30 rated games, a game in the last week and a settled rating, and the
   last-week rule exists "so that players who no longer use their accounts stop populating leaderboards"
   (https://lichess.org/faq#leaderboards). Two lessons: rows of people who left should go away by themselves, and
   a bar of real play keeps throwaway players off the top.
3. **Google Play Games Services.** It offers score limits "to discard score submissions that are clearly
   fraudulent", automatic tamper protection, and `Players.hide` to hide players from all boards
   (https://developer.android.com/games/pgs/leaderboards). Boards are expected to be attacked, and the defence is in
   what counts, not in who may appear.
4. **The ICO Children's code, standard 7.** Children's personal data must be "only visible or accessible to other
   users of the service if the child amends their settings to allow this"
   (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/7-default-settings/).
   That is why ADR-048.10 hides chosen names. A server-given `Guest-7K2Q` is not chosen by the player, says nothing
   about them and is not linked to anything else we publish, which is the owner's reason for the exception.

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **Show guests under their server-given name, and only that name.** The board shows a guest row's name only when
   the row is a guest's and the name has exactly the server-given shape (`Guest-` and four characters of the
   alphabet). Any other name on a guest row is hidden like an account of unknown age. So if guests can ever choose a
   name, the age rule applies to them without anyone having to remember the board (owner decision 2).
2. **Same writes, same fair play.** Guests go through the same `applyMatch` and `applyBonus` code as accounts: the
   same `creditFor` verdict, no computer games, scores that only rise. Nothing new decides what counts.
3. **Guest rows last as long as a guest session can.** Each guest row gets `expiresAt` 30 days after the guest's
   latest counted game or quest bonus (never later than a weekly row's own end), and every counted game moves all
   the guest's all time rows on. A session ends 30 days after the last visit and a game is a visit, so a guest's row
   never outlives the session that could still claim it, and the existing TTL index removes it. Nothing waits for
   CC-098, and when CC-098 removes guest data it finds no board rows left behind (it may also call `erasePlayer`,
   which works for any id). Accounts' rows keep their lifetimes. Like Lichess, a player who left stops filling the
   board.
4. **The transfer moves the rows exactly once.**
   1. Each weekly row of the guest is added to the account's row of the same week once, keyed `guest:<guest id>`,
      with the guest's remembered keys copied across. A match or quest the guest already had on that week can then
      never be added again for the account. This closes the ADR-015 gap: the guest's quest XP from this week now
      reaches the account.
   2. Games withdrawn by fair play in the merge (ADR-021.6, `withdrawnFrom` the guest) are taken out of what is added
      for their week, so the board agrees with progress.
   3. Then the existing recompute runs: all time XP from the merged progress, wins from the moved matches, this
      week's XP from this week's matches. These only raise, so they never count twice what the first step added.
   4. Then the guest's rows are deleted. Run again after a crash at any step, it adds nothing twice.
   5. A late pipeline step for the guest (it read the match before the history moved) must not leave a guest row
      behind. The step checks the guest transfers before it writes and again after: a guest who has started moving
      is not written, and a row written in that moment is removed. That can only under-count, never double count.
5. **"You".** A guest's `you` is now their rank and score like an account's, so the web app marks their row with
   the "you" tag, or shows "#rank You" under the list. A guest with no row yet sees "Win a match to get ranked", and
   every guest sees one line inviting them to sign up to keep their place.
6. **Caching.** A response with `you` gets `Cache-Control: private, no-store`, so no shared cache can hand one
   person's rank to another. The public response stays as it was.
7. **Abuse: no new limit is needed. The reasoning, with numbers:**
   1. A board row needs a counted game against another person: never against the computer, and never a game where
      someone did not move or left before two moves each (ADR-021.2). A guest costs nothing, but every row needs a
      real two-sided game in a room.
   2. Per guest, at most 20 counted games an hour and 10 per opponent in 24 hours, at most 30 XP a game. That is
      600 XP an hour, exactly what an account can earn.
   3. The binding limit for one person is the address: 30 connections and 30 open rooms per network. So one address
      can run about 30 players at once, guests or accounts. At 20 counted games an hour each that is at most about
      18,000 XP an hour across those 30 players, and the same 30 players can keep going for days (they have 435
      pairs among them, far below the pair cap).
   4. With accounts the same attacker gets the same 30 players in 3 hours (10 sign-ups an hour). With guests it takes
      15 minutes (30 guest sessions per 15 minutes). Guests change how fast the attack starts, not how big it gets.
      Slowing guests down for the boards would need a limit per address on board rows, which means storing
      addresses with players, against ADR-035's keyed daily hashes. It would also hurt honest players behind shared
      carrier addresses (most Indian mobile networks put many phones behind one address).
   5. What the board shows cannot be abused: a guest row's name is always server-given, so a flood cannot put words,
      links or slurs on the board, the usual harm of open boards with chosen names.
   6. A flood can still push real players down the list. The "You" row always shows each player their own rank. If
      it happens, the owner's options are listed below (hide guest rows from the public list, or a bar of several
      counted games against different opponents before a guest is listed). Both are small changes to `getBoard`,
      with no new data.

### Design notes

1. `models/identity.js` gains `isGuestId` and `isServerGivenGuestName`. The guest name alphabet and length move
   there from `identityComponent.js`, so the name the server makes and the name the board trusts come from one
   place.
2. `boards.js` gains `GUEST_ROW_LIFETIME_MS` (30 days) and `guestExpiry`. `server.test.js` checks it is never longer
   than `SESSION_TTL_SECONDS`, because only the composition root may import both layers.
3. The DAO gains three operations, all in the shared contract (MongoDB and the in-memory double): `mergeOnce` (add
   once by key and remember extra keys), `listWithKeys` (one player's rows of one board with their keys) and
   `extendLifetime` (move a player's rows' end later, never earlier). No new index: every query uses
   `{board, period, playerId}` or `playerId`, and the TTL index already exists.
4. The leaderboard component receives `transfers: { listFrom }` (the player transfer accessor), as the block
   component does.
5. `LEADERBOARD_UNLISTED.GUEST` goes away: no viewer is "a guest, so never ranked" any more.

## Why

Most players start as guests and never see themselves on a board, so the boards mean nothing to them until they
sign up.

## Decision (owner, 2026-10-20)

1. Guests appear on the leaderboards under the name the server gave them, `Guest-XXXX`.
2. This is safe because guests cannot choose their names. If guests can ever choose a name, the age rule
   (ADR-048.10, "Hidden player" for anyone not surely an adult) applies to them too.
3. It amends ADR-015.1 (accounts only).
4. A guest's entries move to the account when the guest signs up (ADR-014).
5. Fair play (ADR-021) still decides which games count.

## Scope

- In: writing guest entries, showing them as `Guest-XXXX`, moving them on sign-up, tests.
- Out: guest-chosen names.

## Plan / notes

1. Built as the Research section's design notes say. The files: `models/identity.js` (`isGuestId`,
   `isServerGivenGuestName`, the guest name alphabet), `domain/leaderboards/boards.js` (`GUEST_ROW_LIFETIME_MS`,
   `rowEnds`), `domain/leaderboards/leaderboardComponent.js`, `data/leaderboardAccessor.js`,
   `persistence/leaderboardDao.js` with its in-memory double and contract, `service/http/leaderboardRoutes.js`
   (`Cache-Control`), the wiring in `server.js` and `testing/testApp.js`, `LEADERBOARD_UNLISTED` in
   `@classicade/shared`, and the web app's leaderboard page and "Your rank" card.
2. The test app's guests are now named in the guest alphabet (`Guest-TAAB`, `Guest-TAAC` and so on), because
   `Guest-T001` holds a 0 and a 1, which the server never gives, and the board rightly hid it.
3. **For CC-098.** Guest rows already go by themselves 30 days after the guest's last counted game, so CC-098 has
   nothing to clean on the boards. If it removes a guest's data earlier, it can also call
   `leaderboardComponent.erasePlayer({ playerId })`, which works for a guest id.
4. **Options for the owner if guests flood a board** (no code now, ADR-060 point 8): hide guest rows from the public
   list while keeping their own "You" row, or list a guest only after several counted games against different
   opponents. Both change `getBoard` only.
