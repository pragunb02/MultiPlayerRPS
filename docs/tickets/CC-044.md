## Problem

Riya, 14, plays Rock Paper Scissors with someone from a shared link whose username is a slur the filter missed
(ADR-022.2 says reports cover joined lowercase words). She can block him (PR 1), but nobody who could rename him ever
hears about it, and there is no way to say he stalled every move or kept sending rematch requests either.

## Job to be done

When another player is rude, cheats or keeps bothering them, players want to tell the site in a few taps without
being found out, so they feel safe coming back and the owner can act on names and behaviour that break the rules.

## Appetite

M (up to 6 agent-hours) for PR 2. **Cut line:** the snapshot of the last moves and requests, then the report from a
public profile, are dropped first. Suspension, the reporter's status list and urgent reports are out (follow-ups).

## Proposed solution

A Report button next to Block on a room seat, beside each person in match history and on a public profile opens one
dialog: four closed reasons and no free text (owner, 2026-10-21), then a neutral thank-you with "Block too".
`POST /api/reports` names the other player only by where they met (ADR-071.1). The owner reads reports grouped by
player at `/admin/reports` and dismisses them or renames the account to a safe default. Details in ADR-071 and §6.

## Non-goals

- Suspending or banning accounts: there is no suspended state yet (follow-up).
- Telling the reported player anything, ever, or telling the reporter what happened to them.
- Automatic action on report counts.
- Reports on circles (CC-214), emotes (CC-169) or chat, which do not exist.

## Rabbit holes

- Renaming every copy of a name (stored matches, open rooms): only the account, its sessions and its leaderboard
  rows are renamed; stored history keeps the name it had (follow-up).
- A moderation workflow (assignees, notes, appeals): one owner, two actions.
- Reporter scoring maths: one simple set aside rule, written down (ADR-071.5).

## Instrumentation

None. The owner reads the counts in the console itself. A later analytics event (ADR-034) would be a count of
reports per day by reason, with no ids.

## Threat model

Building: a way for any player to name another to the owner. Can go wrong: probing ids or rooms through the route
(one 404, references only), flooding the queue or brigading one player (limits, one per pair a day, set aside, no
automatic action), a reporter found out (nothing reaches the reported player, the console shows no reporter), stored
XSS in the console (React text only, tested), a reporter's contact details in a report (no free text at all), a renamed player
locked out (they sign in again with the same email). Good enough for one moderator; revisit with CC-059 roles.

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** the reporter's and the reported player's ids (server only), the name then, the reason, where it
  happened and the last moves and requests by role. No free text.
- **Retention:** 180 days by a TTL index; deletion and export as AC-10.
- **Who sees it:** the reporter (their own report's id, their export); the owner in the console; never other
  players; logs carry the reason only, and the admin's actions.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** the same tool for every age; a child cannot type anything into a report; the thank-you shows
  Childline 1098 and 1930.
- **Defaults:** private by construction; nothing to switch on or off.

## Alternatives considered

- A masked note (the first brief): built, then removed on the owner's answer of 2026-10-21. A note may come later
  behind a CC-312 flag, with the feedback masker.
- One report per pair while open (§3.3.5): a day keeps a pattern visible and still floods nothing.
- Block by default on report (Lichess): a one-tap block after the thank-you keeps the choice with the player.

## Research (written before any code)

Sources were read on 2026-10-18 unless another date is given. "General knowledge" carries a confidence level.

### 1. How our code does it today

1. **Players cannot talk.** There is no chat and no free text. The only text a player chooses is an account's
   username, which passes the name filter (ADR-022). Guests get a random `Guest-<suffix>`
   (`identityComponent.js`), so a guest cannot choose an offensive name. ADR-022.2 accepts that joined lowercase
   words such as `bigdick` pass the filter "and reports (CC-044) cover it".
2. **Strangers cannot meet yet.** Rooms are joined only by code or link (`roomComponent.joinRoom`). Quick match
   (CC-051) will be the first way to meet a stranger. Emotes (CC-169), challenges (CC-139), friends (CC-052) and
   circles (CC-214) come later.
3. **What a player sees of another:** the seat's display name and player id in the room view
   (`components/Seats/Seats.jsx`), the opponent's name in match history, account names on leaderboards.
4. **Nothing to block, mute or report with.** No collection, route, socket event or button exists. Rematch can be
   asked again after a decline in the 30 minute window, which is the only way one player can press on another today.
5. **Moderation tools.** The owner console (`/admin`, ADR-036) is an allowlist of account ids, read only and
   aggregates only. There are no roles, no bans and no rename tool (CC-059 is Stage 3).
6. **Contact.** The privacy page's contact link is the public GitHub issues page (`PrivacyPage.jsx`). That is not a
   place for a child to report someone, and IT Rules and DPDP grievances need a named contact (below).
7. **Data rules that apply.** DATA.md's rule: a PR that stores something new updates DATA.md and the privacy page.
   ADR-034: analytics are counts with no ids. ADR-014: a guest's data follows them into an account.

### 2. How others do it

**Lichess** (source code, https://github.com/lichess-org/lila):

1. **Block** is a relation between two accounts (`modules/relation/RelationApi.scala`). Blocking also ends a follow
   the other way. The number of blocks is capped by a setting (`limit.block`), and the oldest are dropped above it.
2. Blocked pairs are **never paired** in the pools (`modules/pool/MatchMaking.scala`).
3. **Report reasons** (`modules/report/Reason.scala`): Cheat, Stall, Boost, VerbalAbuse, Violence, Harass, SelfHarm,
   Hate, Spam, Username, Other, plus automatic ones. **Every communication reason also blocks the reported player
   for the reporter** (`autoBlock = comm`, `ReportApi.isAutoBlock`).
4. Players who abuse reports get a `reportban` mark and their reports are ignored (`ReportApi.create`). A new report
   on someone with an open report joins that report rather than making a new one.
5. Blocks are silent: the blocked player is not told (general knowledge, high confidence).

**Chess.com, Roblox, Xbox and Discord** (general knowledge, medium to high confidence): block from a profile or a
player menu, silent, and it hides that player's messages and invitations. Report from the same menu with a list of
reasons, and a confirmation that the report was received. Roblox and Xbox let a player see that a report was acted
on without saying what happened to the other player.

**Mute** in games hides one player's messages, emotes or voice for the muter only, usually for the session or
for good, without telling the other player (general knowledge, high confidence).

**Children** (UK ICO Age Appropriate Design Code, standard 15, online tools,
https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/15-online-tools/):
reporting tools should be **prominent**, suited to the child's age ("suitable for use by the child without the help
of an adult" from 13 to 15), let the child or a parent **track the progress** of a report, let a child **mark a
report as urgent** with urgent ones handled first, and state response times. This is a UK code, not Indian law, but
it is the clearest public standard for children's reporting tools.

**OWASP.** No cheat sheet covers player safety tools directly. What applies:

1. Insecure Direct Object Reference prevention
   (https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Insecure_Direct_Object_Reference_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html):
   check access for every object on the server, scope lookups to the current user, and do not trust ids from the
   client. For us: a player may block or report only someone the server knows they met (same room or match), never
   an id typed in.
2. Rate limits against flooding (the reasoning of ADR-035), and logs that never hold free text or personal data
   (the Logging cheat sheet, and DATA.md's server log rules).

**India, IT Rules 2021 (as amended on 28 Oct 2022).** We host information that players provide (usernames, later
circle names), so we are very likely an intermediary (general knowledge, medium confidence. The lawyer confirms).
Duties under Rule 3 (general knowledge, high confidence. MeitY's PDF refused the fetcher, so the numbers need a check
against the gazetted text):

1. Publish rules, a privacy policy and terms, and remind users of them at least once a year.
2. Publish the **name and contact details of a Grievance Officer**. Acknowledge a complaint **within 24 hours** and
   resolve it **within 15 days**. Complaints asking to remove content that breaks the rules: **within 72 hours**.
   Content showing a person's private area, sexual acts or impersonating someone: removed **within 24 hours**.
3. Keep records of removed content and the user for **180 days**.
4. A user unhappy with the Grievance Officer's decision can appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee within 30
   days (https://gac.gov.in/).
5. The 2023 amendments for online gaming intermediaries and the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025
   (ADR-026) may add duties for a game. That goes to the lawyer (PRODUCT_VISION.md §6.10 item 7).

**India, DPDP Rules 2025** (https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules.html): Rule 9 asks for published contact details of a
person who answers questions about processing, and Rule 14(3) for a published grievance process answered within at
most 90 days. Both apply from about May 2027. A report holds personal data of two players, one possibly a child
(Rule 10).

**Child safety resources in India:** Childline 1098 and the National Cyber Crime portal, cybercrime.gov.in, with
helpline 1930 (general knowledge, high confidence. gac.gov.in names 1930 and the portal for cybercrime).

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **Block, by player id, for guests and accounts.** A guest's id lasts as long as their session, so a guest can be
   blocked too. Blocks follow a guest who signs in (ADR-014). Silent, never told to the blocked player. Effects,
   always both ways:
   1. Never paired in quick match (CC-051).
   2. A blocked player cannot take a seat in the blocker's room, and the blocker cannot take a seat in theirs. The
      refusal is the same "This room isn't available" as a full room, so it reveals nothing.
   3. In a finished room, rematch between them is off (the request is refused without saying why).
   4. Later features check the same rule: challenges, friend requests, presence, notifications, circle invites
      (ROADMAP.md §6).
   5. A game in progress is not stopped. The player can leave, and the block holds from the next room.
   6. At most 500 blocks per player. The oldest drops off above that, as Lichess does.
   7. Who can be blocked: anyone the server saw in a room or match with the player. The client sends a room code or a
      match id and a seat, never a raw player id (OWASP IDOR).
2. **Mute.** Today there is nothing to mute: no chat, no emotes, no sounds from the other player. Mute ships with
   emotes (CC-169) as "Mute <name>" in the seat menu, for that room, in the browser only, not stored, plus the global
   "Emotes off" setting CC-169 already has. CC-044 writes the rule in the ADR so CC-169 builds it.
3. **Report.**
   1. From the seat menu and from match history, with a **closed list of reasons** in plain words:
      "Rude or unsafe name", "Cheating or unfair play", "Keeps bothering me", "Something else". No free text box:
      every player counts as a minor until CC-152, a free text box invites a child to type a phone number or an
      address, and it would be the first free text we store (DATA.md). Revisit for adults after CC-152.
   2. An **"I feel unsafe"** choice marks the report urgent and shows, at once, help in plain words: "Tell a parent
      or a grown-up you trust", Childline 1098, cybercrime.gov.in and 1930.
   3. **Reporting also blocks** by default (Lichess's rule), with a box to untick.
   4. The server stores what it already knows: reporter id, reported id, the reported player's display name at the
      time, reason, urgent, game id, room code or match id, and the time. For "Cheating or unfair play" the match id
      is enough, because the match can be replayed (`replayGame`).
   5. One open report per reporter and reported player (a repeat updates it). At most 10 reports a day per reporter.
      Reports from a reporter whose reports are always dismissed can be set aside later (CC-059).
   6. The reporter sees "Thanks, we got it. We look at every report within 15 days, urgent ones within 24 hours" and,
      in their profile, the status of their reports (received, looked at, closed). Never what happened to the other
      player.
4. **Handling.** A "Reports" page in the owner console (ADR-036 allowlist, `no-store`, `noindex`): urgent first, then
   oldest. Actions in v1: dismiss, **reset the username** to a random `Player-<suffix>` and require a new name at the
   next login (the name filter list gains the word, with a test, ADR-022), and note. Bans and suspensions wait for
   CC-059. Every action is logged with the admin's id and the report id, never the reason text shown to players.
5. **Retention.** Open reports are kept until closed. Closed reports and the action taken are kept **180 days**, then
   removed by a TTL index (the IT Rules record period). Blocks are kept until unblocked or the account is deleted
   (CC-035 must remove both).
6. **The grievance contact.** A Grievance Officer name and an email on the brand domain (`grievance@<domain>`,
   forwarded to the owner by the registrar's free email forwarding, general knowledge, high confidence that common
   registrars and Cloudflare offer it), on the terms and privacy pages, with the 24 hour and 15 day promise. It
   replaces GitHub issues as the contact for anything personal. This needs CC-123's domain. Until it exists, the
   in-app report is the only channel and that is said on the page.
7. **Analytics.** Counts only, no ids (ADR-034): reports per day by reason, and the time to close, for the
   PRODUCT_VISION.md §6.10 measures ("reports per 1,000 matches, time to act on a report").

### 4. Addendum for PR 1, blocks (2026-10-19, before any code)

What the code on `main` at `b173cd4` changes in the plan above, and the choices the plan left open. Decided in
ADR-053.

1. **No pairing code exists yet.** Quick match (CC-051) is not built, so "never paired" cannot be wired into a pool
   today. The block component answers `blockedWith({ playerId })` (every player on either side of a block with this
   one) and `eitherBlocked({ a, b })`, both unit tested, and ADR-053 makes calling one of them a rule for CC-051 and
   for every later feature that brings two players together (challenges, friends, circles).
2. **Seat refusal.** `roomComponent.joinRoom` already refuses a full room with `ConflictError("This room is full")`,
   and the room page shows the message under "Can't join this room". The plan wrote "This room isn't available". A
   new sentence used only for blocks would itself tell a blocked player what happened, so the block refusal reuses
   the full room refusal word for word: same error code, same message, no details. A test compares the two answers.
   A game already started still answers "This game has already started" first, as it does for anyone.
3. **Rematch refusal.** A rematch asked or accepted between a blocked pair is refused with a plain
   `ConflictError("A rematch is not possible now")`, which says nothing about why. An open rematch request made
   before the block is left alone: the blocker cannot accept it, and the blocked player sees it end with no answer,
   as when anyone ignores one. Declining and withdrawing still work. Nothing in the room is rewritten when someone
   blocks, so the opponent's screen does not change at that moment.
4. **What the client sends.** `POST /api/blocks` takes `{ roomCode, seat }` (an open room where both players sit)
   or `{ matchId, seat }` (a stored match both played), where `seat` is the position in the room's seats or in the
   match's players. Never a player id. CC-253 (opaque seat ids) changes what the room view sends, not this request,
   so the two tickets do not collide. A seat that is the caller, a computer character or a deleted player's
   stand-in is refused like a stranger.
5. **One refusal for every miss.** Any reference the server cannot confirm (no such room, a closed room, a room or
   match the caller is not in, a seat out of range, themselves, a computer) answers the same `404 NOT_FOUND` "You can
   only block someone you played with". So the endpoint cannot be used to probe rooms, matches or ids (OWASP IDOR
   cheat sheet, as above).
6. **Unblocking by the record, scoped to its owner.** Each block gets a random `blockId`. `DELETE
/api/blocks/:blockId` deletes it only when the caller made it. Anyone else's id, or a made-up one, answers the
   same 404 as one that never existed. The list (`GET /api/blocks`) answers `blockId`, the name and the time only,
   never the blocked player's id.
7. **No way to learn who blocked you.** Nothing answers "who blocked me": the list and the export carry only the
   blocks the player made. The room and rematch refusals are the ones above.
8. **The name in the list** is the blocked player's display name at the moment of the block, kept with the record.
   A guest has no stored name anywhere else. When the blocked guest later signs in, the record moves to their
   account (ADR-014) but keeps the old name, so the blocker never learns which account the guest became.
9. **Limits.** At most 500 blocks per player: adding the 501st drops the oldest (Lichess `limit.block`, as above).
   Blocking and unblocking together are limited to 20 per 10 minutes per player and 100 per 10 minutes per network
   address, answered with the standard 429 and `RateLimit` headers (`service/http/rateLimits.js`). Blocking someone
   already blocked changes nothing and answers the same record.
10. **Guest to account (ADR-014).** A new participant `blocks` moves both sides: blocks the guest made become the
    account's, and blocks other players made on the guest now name the account, so signing in never escapes a
    block. A pair that would name the same player twice or block themselves is dropped. Running it twice changes
    nothing. The 500 cap applies after the move.
11. **Deletion and export (ADR-048.2).** A new participant `blocks`: deletion removes every record on either side,
    under the account id and every guest id the player had. The export lists the blocks the player made (name and
    time), never the blocks on them.
12. **Store.** Collection `player_blocks`: `{ blockId, blockerId, blockedId, blockedName, createdAt }`, unique index
    on `{ blockerId, blockedId }` (which also serves the list), an index on `blockedId` for the other direction, and
    a unique index on `blockId`. Pseudonymous, plus a display name. Kept until unblocked or deleted. A guest's
    blocks age out with guest data (CC-098). DATA.md and the privacy page say so.
13. **Mute** stays with CC-169, not stored, as in 3.2 above. Reports are PR 2.
14. **Minors.** Blocking needs no age check and shows nothing about anyone, so it is the same for every age group
    (ADR-028).
15. **Accepted limit.** A blocked player who knows a room has a free seat can guess why they were refused, as on
    Lichess. Quick match will refuse silently by never pairing, which leaves nothing to guess.

### 5. Changes after the review of #107 (2026-10-20)

The two reviewers of #107 confirmed four findings. Each was fixed with a test that failed first, and ADR-053 is
amended. Items 3, 4 and 9 above read as below from now on.

1. **The seat moved under the dialog (medium).** The page sent the seat's place only, saved when Block was pressed.
   If the player left and someone else sat down, or a rematch swapped the seats, the block could land on someone else.
   A room seat now also carries `seatId`, the id the room view shows for that seat (today the player id, opaque per
   room after CC-253). The server blocks only while that seat still holds that person, and answers the same 404
   otherwise. The dialog closes as soon as the seats change.
2. **A block landing on a guest who had just signed in (low).** A guest's blocks move once, when they sign in. A block
   written a moment later named the dead guest id and never applied to the account. The block now goes to the
   account when the guest's transfer exists, and `relationsOf` and `blockedWith` count blocks stored under a guest id
   that has become an account, on both sides.
3. **The rematch refusal told a player they were blocked (low, security).** "A rematch is not possible now" was used
   by blocks only, and came before every other check, at any moment. Now nothing is refused with words of its own.
   The blocked player's request opens as anyone's. When the blocker says yes (or asks back), the request ends as
   declined, exactly what a decline looks like. When the blocked player says yes to a request the blocker made
   before the block (or asks back), it ends as withdrawn, as if the blocker had taken it back. No game starts. The
   cost: until the blocker leaves the room, the blocked player can still ask them there, and the blocker declines or
   ignores it.
4. **One player could use up a network's limit (low, security).** The per address limit ran first and counted every
   request, so one player looping on the route could stop a whole school from blocking. The player's own limit now
   runs first, and the address counts only what it let through.

**Room changes in this PR, kept small for CC-253:** `createRoomComponent` takes a `blocks` dependency, and only
`joinRoom`, `askRequest` and `answerRequest` read it. Nothing else in `roomComponent.js` changes.

### 6. Research for PR 2, reports (2026-10-20, before any code)

Sources were read on 2026-10-20 unless another date is given. "General knowledge" carries a confidence level. The
brief for this pull request (coordinator, 2026-10-20) changes three things in §3.3: an optional note of up to 500
characters, masked as feedback is (ADR-059.2); a report from a public profile (ADR-065); and no "urgent" choice.
The note reverses the owner's answer 11 of ADR-051 ("free text in reports: not for now"), so ADR-071 records it as
the brief's direction for the owner to confirm, with one constant to turn it off.

#### 6.1 How our code does it today (`main` at `c3471eb`)

1. **Blocks name people by reference, never by id.** `blockComponent.personAt` resolves `{ roomCode, seat, seatId }`
   (the seat id the room view showed, ADR-057) or `{ matchId, seat }` (the match's real players through
   `matchComponent.playersFor`, CC-297), refuses the caller, computers and deleted stand-ins, and answers one 404
   for every miss. A guest who signed in since is followed to the account (`transfers.listFrom`). Reports need the
   same resolution, so it moves into one module both use.
2. **Profiles** are reached by a random handle (ADR-065). `profileComponent.publicView` shows a name only for an
   adult account; everyone else is "Hidden player". A report from a profile can therefore only name an adult
   account, and the server finds the person from the handle.
3. **The feedback inbox** (`/admin/feedback`, ADR-059) is the console's only per person view: `requireAdmin` with
   the same 404, `no-store`, `noindex`, values shown as text, a 180 day TTL, masking with
   `domain/feedback/maskContacts.js`, and limits per session, address, /48 and site (`feedbackRoutes.js`).
4. **No rename and no suspension exist.** `accounts.username` is written only at sign-up. Sessions carry the name
   in the identity, so a new name shows only after a new sign-in. `accountAccessor.bumpSessionVersion` ends every
   session of an account (CC-116), and `gateway.disconnectPlayer` cuts its sockets (CC-035). Leaderboard rows
   keep a copy of the name (`leaderboard_entries.displayName`). There is no suspended state anywhere, so suspension
   is a follow-up (below).
5. **Names** are 3 to 20 letters, digits or underscores and pass the filter (`parseUsername`, ADR-022). Guests'
   names are server-given (`Guest-XXXX`) and cannot be offensive.
6. **Ids.** No client receives another person's player id (ADR-057, CC-297, DATA.md Identifiers). Tests use
   `hasPlayerIdShape` as the last check.

#### 6.2 How others do it

1. **Lichess** (source, `modules/report/src/main/ReportApi.scala` and `Reason.scala`,
   https://github.com/lichess-org/lila): reasons include Cheat, Stall, Boost, Username, Harass and Other; up to
   1000 characters of text go with a report; a new report on someone with an open report in the same room
   **joins** it (`Report.make(scored, prev)`), so moderators see one item per player with a score; reports from a
   reporter marked `reportban` are **silently dropped** (`create` does nothing when `marks.reportban`); a cheat
   reporter's **accuracy** over their last 20 closed reports weights their score (fewer than 4 gives no weight);
   communication reasons also block (`autoBlock = comm`). Verified 2026-10-20.
2. **Chess.com** (general knowledge, medium confidence; its help pages answered 404 to the fetcher): a "Report"
   item in the player menu of a game and of a profile, with reasons (cheating, abuse, inappropriate username or
   picture, other) and an optional description; the reporter gets a generic thanks, and later a notice that action
   was taken on a fair play report without saying what; the reported player is not told who reported.
3. **Roblox** (general knowledge, high confidence; the help page answered 403): "Report Abuse" from a profile, an
   experience or chat, a category list and an optional comment, a generic confirmation, and the same menu offers
   Block. The reported user is not told who reported. Roblox's rules say false reports are a violation.
4. **Digital Trust & Safety Partnership best practices** (https://dtspartnership.org/best-practices/, read
   2026-10-20): easy in-product reporting; response time and prioritisation standards; remedies for those actioned;
   update reporters; transparency reports.
5. **UK ICO children's code, standard 15** (cited in §2): prominent, age-appropriate reporting tools. **OWASP IDOR**
   (cited in §2): resolve every object on the server, scoped to the caller. **OWASP XSS prevention**
   (https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Cross_Site_Scripting_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html): render
   untrusted text as text (React's default), never as HTML.
6. **Mass false reporting** (general knowledge, high confidence): platforms that act automatically on report counts
   are brigaded (coordinated reports get innocent accounts suspended). The common defence is that counts only order
   the queue, a person decides, and reporters with a record of rejected reports count for less.

#### 6.3 What is best for ClassiCade

1. **Report by reference, never by id.** The client sends `{ roomCode, seat, seatId }`, `{ matchId, seat }` or
   `{ handle }`. The server resolves the person with the block code (moved into `domain/players/playerReferences.js`
   and shared), and from a handle only an adult account other than the caller. Every miss is one 404.
2. **Four closed reasons** in `@classicade/shared` (`REPORT_REASONS`): offensive name, cheating or stalling,
   harassment through emotes or requests, something else. An optional note of at most 500 characters, plain text,
   masked as feedback is.
3. **Context is snapshotted** when the report is made, so it outlives the room: the game, the room code or match
   id, the name at the time, and for a room or match the last 10 moves and the last 5 requests between the two,
   with players named only by role (`reported`, `reporter`, `other`).
4. **A neutral thank-you** that says nothing about what will happen, with "Block too" in one tap. That block goes
   through `POST /api/blocks` with `{ reportId }`, the reporter's own report, so the block route and its limits are
   reused and still no player id is sent. Nothing is ever sent to the reported player: no socket event, no
   notification, nothing in any view.
5. **Limits.** One report per reporter per reported player per India day, by a unique index (a repeat answers the
   same report, as Lichess joins). 10 a day per player, then 30 a day per address counting only what the player's
   limit let through (as blocks do). **False report flooding:** counts never act by themselves; a person decides.
   A reporter with at least 3 dismissed reports and none acted on in the kept period is _set aside_: their new
   reports are stored and thanked as any, but marked and sorted last (Lichess `reportban` and accuracy, softened
   because we have one moderator and many children). Groups show distinct reporters, so ten reports from one
   person do not look like ten people.
6. **The console** gets `/admin/reports`, behind the allowlist with the same 404, listing open reports grouped by
   the reported player (never their id: the group is named by its newest report's id), counts by reason, every
   report's context and note as text, and two actions on a group: **dismiss** and **rename to a safe default**
   (`Player_` and six letters, which passes the name filter), which also renames the player's leaderboard rows and
   signs them out everywhere so the new name shows at once. Guests have server-given names, so only accounts can be
   renamed. **Suspension does not exist**, so it is a follow-up, not built here.
7. **Retention: 180 days from the report**, by a TTL index (the IT Rules record period, as feedback). Account
   deletion removes reports about the player, and on reports the player made it replaces their id by a stand-in and
   removes the note (their words), so a harasser's record survives the victim leaving. The export lists the
   player's own reports (reason, note, where, the name they reported, when), never the reported player's id and
   never what happened. Reports move with a guest who signs in (ADR-014).
8. **Copy** in Hinglish and English, always "tum", tested against "aap". The thank-you carries one help line for a
   child who feels unsafe (Childline 1098, cybercrime.gov.in, 1930), from §3.3.2.
