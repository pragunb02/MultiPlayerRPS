## Research (written before any code)

Sources were read on 2026-10-18 unless another date is given. "General knowledge" carries a confidence level.

### 1. How our code does it today

1. **Players cannot talk.** There is no chat and no free text. The only text a player chooses is an account's
   username, which passes the name filter (ADR-022). Guests get a random `Guest-<suffix>`
   (`identityComponent.js`), so a guest cannot choose an offensive name. ADR-022.2 accepts that joined lowercase
   words such as `bigdick` pass the filter "and reports (CC-044) cover it".
2. **Strangers cannot meet yet.** Rooms are joined only by code or link (`roomComponent.joinRoom`). Quick match
   (CC-051) will be the first way to meet a stranger. Emotes (CC-169), challenges (CC-139), friends (CC-052) and
   circles (CC-214) come later.
3. **What a player sees of another:** the seat's display name and player id in the room view
   (`components/Seats/Seats.jsx`), the opponent's name in match history, account names on leaderboards.
4. **Nothing to block, mute or report with.** No collection, route, socket event or button exists. Rematch can be
   asked again after a decline in the 30 minute window, which is the only way one player can press on another today.
5. **Moderation tools.** The owner console (`/admin`, ADR-036) is an allowlist of account ids, read only and
   aggregates only. There are no roles, no bans and no rename tool (CC-059 is Stage 3).
6. **Contact.** The privacy page's contact link is the public GitHub issues page (`PrivacyPage.jsx`). That is not a
   place for a child to report someone, and IT Rules and DPDP grievances need a named contact (below).
7. **Data rules that apply.** DATA.md's rule: a PR that stores something new updates DATA.md and the privacy page.
   ADR-034: analytics are counts with no ids. ADR-014: a guest's data follows them into an account.

### 2. How others do it

**Lichess** (source code, https://github.com/lichess-org/lila):

1. **Block** is a relation between two accounts (`modules/relation/RelationApi.scala`). Blocking also ends a follow
   the other way. The number of blocks is capped by a setting (`limit.block`), and the oldest are dropped above it.
2. Blocked pairs are **never paired** in the pools (`modules/pool/MatchMaking.scala`).
3. **Report reasons** (`modules/report/Reason.scala`): Cheat, Stall, Boost, VerbalAbuse, Violence, Harass, SelfHarm,
   Hate, Spam, Username, Other, plus automatic ones. **Every communication reason also blocks the reported player
   for the reporter** (`autoBlock = comm`, `ReportApi.isAutoBlock`).
4. Players who abuse reports get a `reportban` mark and their reports are ignored (`ReportApi.create`). A new report
   on someone with an open report joins that report rather than making a new one.
5. Blocks are silent: the blocked player is not told (general knowledge, high confidence).

**Chess.com, Roblox, Xbox and Discord** (general knowledge, medium to high confidence): block from a profile or a
player menu, silent, and it hides that player's messages and invitations. Report from the same menu with a list of
reasons, and a confirmation that the report was received. Roblox and Xbox let a player see that a report was acted
on without saying what happened to the other player.

**Mute** in games hides one player's messages, emotes or voice for the muter only, usually for the session or
for good, without telling the other player (general knowledge, high confidence).

**Children** (UK ICO Age Appropriate Design Code, standard 15, online tools,
https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/15-online-tools/):
reporting tools should be **prominent**, suited to the child's age ("suitable for use by the child without the help
of an adult" from 13 to 15), let the child or a parent **track the progress** of a report, let a child **mark a
report as urgent** with urgent ones handled first, and state response times. This is a UK code, not Indian law, but
it is the clearest public standard for children's reporting tools.

**OWASP.** No cheat sheet covers player safety tools directly. What applies:

1. Insecure Direct Object Reference prevention
   (https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Insecure_Direct_Object_Reference_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html):
   check access for every object on the server, scope lookups to the current user, and do not trust ids from the
   client. For us: a player may block or report only someone the server knows they met (same room or match), never
   an id typed in.
2. Rate limits against flooding (the reasoning of ADR-035), and logs that never hold free text or personal data
   (the Logging cheat sheet, and DATA.md's server log rules).

**India, IT Rules 2021 (as amended on 28 Oct 2022).** We host information that players provide (usernames, later
circle names), so we are very likely an intermediary (general knowledge, medium confidence. The lawyer confirms).
Duties under Rule 3 (general knowledge, high confidence. MeitY's PDF refused the fetcher, so the numbers need a check
against the gazetted text):

1. Publish rules, a privacy policy and terms, and remind users of them at least once a year.
2. Publish the **name and contact details of a Grievance Officer**. Acknowledge a complaint **within 24 hours** and
   resolve it **within 15 days**. Complaints asking to remove content that breaks the rules: **within 72 hours**.
   Content showing a person's private area, sexual acts or impersonating someone: removed **within 24 hours**.
3. Keep records of removed content and the user for **180 days**.
4. A user unhappy with the Grievance Officer's decision can appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee within 30
   days (https://gac.gov.in/).
5. The 2023 amendments for online gaming intermediaries and the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025
   (ADR-026) may add duties for a game. That goes to the lawyer (PRODUCT_VISION.md §6.10 item 7).

**India, DPDP Rules 2025** (https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules.html): Rule 9 asks for published contact details of a
person who answers questions about processing, and Rule 14(3) for a published grievance process answered within at
most 90 days. Both apply from about May 2027. A report holds personal data of two players, one possibly a child
(Rule 10).

**Child safety resources in India:** Childline 1098 and the National Cyber Crime portal, cybercrime.gov.in, with
helpline 1930 (general knowledge, high confidence. gac.gov.in names 1930 and the portal for cybercrime).

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **Block, by player id, for guests and accounts.** A guest's id lasts as long as their session, so a guest can be
   blocked too. Blocks follow a guest who signs in (ADR-014). Silent, never told to the blocked player. Effects,
   always both ways:
   1. Never paired in quick match (CC-051).
   2. A blocked player cannot take a seat in the blocker's room, and the blocker cannot take a seat in theirs. The
      refusal is the same "This room isn't available" as a full room, so it reveals nothing.
   3. In a finished room, rematch between them is off (the request is refused without saying why).
   4. Later features check the same rule: challenges, friend requests, presence, notifications, circle invites
      (ROADMAP.md §6).
   5. A game in progress is not stopped. The player can leave, and the block holds from the next room.
   6. At most 500 blocks per player. The oldest drops off above that, as Lichess does.
   7. Who can be blocked: anyone the server saw in a room or match with the player. The client sends a room code or a
      match id and a seat, never a raw player id (OWASP IDOR).
2. **Mute.** Today there is nothing to mute: no chat, no emotes, no sounds from the other player. Mute ships with
   emotes (CC-169) as "Mute <name>" in the seat menu, for that room, in the browser only, not stored, plus the global
   "Emotes off" setting CC-169 already has. CC-044 writes the rule in the ADR so CC-169 builds it.
3. **Report.**
   1. From the seat menu and from match history, with a **closed list of reasons** in plain words:
      "Rude or unsafe name", "Cheating or unfair play", "Keeps bothering me", "Something else". No free text box:
      every player counts as a minor until CC-152, a free text box invites a child to type a phone number or an
      address, and it would be the first free text we store (DATA.md). Revisit for adults after CC-152.
   2. An **"I feel unsafe"** choice marks the report urgent and shows, at once, help in plain words: "Tell a parent
      or a grown-up you trust", Childline 1098, cybercrime.gov.in and 1930.
   3. **Reporting also blocks** by default (Lichess's rule), with a box to untick.
   4. The server stores what it already knows: reporter id, reported id, the reported player's display name at the
      time, reason, urgent, game id, room code or match id, and the time. For "Cheating or unfair play" the match id
      is enough, because the match can be replayed (`replayGame`).
   5. One open report per reporter and reported player (a repeat updates it). At most 10 reports a day per reporter.
      Reports from a reporter whose reports are always dismissed can be set aside later (CC-059).
   6. The reporter sees "Thanks, we got it. We look at every report within 15 days, urgent ones within 24 hours" and,
      in their profile, the status of their reports (received, looked at, closed). Never what happened to the other
      player.
4. **Handling.** A "Reports" page in the owner console (ADR-036 allowlist, `no-store`, `noindex`): urgent first, then
   oldest. Actions in v1: dismiss, **reset the username** to a random `Player-<suffix>` and require a new name at the
   next login (the name filter list gains the word, with a test, ADR-022), and note. Bans and suspensions wait for
   CC-059. Every action is logged with the admin's id and the report id, never the reason text shown to players.
5. **Retention.** Open reports are kept until closed. Closed reports and the action taken are kept **180 days**, then
   removed by a TTL index (the IT Rules record period). Blocks are kept until unblocked or the account is deleted
   (CC-035 must remove both).
6. **The grievance contact.** A Grievance Officer name and an email on the brand domain (`grievance@<domain>`,
   forwarded to the owner by the registrar's free email forwarding, general knowledge, high confidence that common
   registrars and Cloudflare offer it), on the terms and privacy pages, with the 24 hour and 15 day promise. It
   replaces GitHub issues as the contact for anything personal. This needs CC-123's domain. Until it exists, the
   in-app report is the only channel and that is said on the page.
7. **Analytics.** Counts only, no ids (ADR-034): reports per day by reason, and the time to close, for the
   PRODUCT_VISION.md §6.10 measures ("reports per 1,000 matches, time to act on a report").

## Why

Block and report must exist before any feature that lets a player meet a stranger (ADR-028.8): quick match (CC-051)
first, then challenges, friends and circles. The name filter relies on reports. The law asks for a grievance contact.

## Scope

- In: blocks (data, API, room seat menu, effects on seating, rematch and quick match), reports (data, form, urgent
  help, limits, status for the reporter), the console's Reports page with dismiss and username reset, the grievance
  contact text, the mute rule in the ADR.
- Out: building mute (CC-169), bans and roles (CC-059), free text in reports, reports on circles (CC-214), parental
  accounts.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A player can block anyone they met in a room or match, from the seat menu, and unblock from the profile.
- [ ] Blocked pairs are never seated together, never rematched and never paired, both ways, and the refusal reveals
      nothing.
- [ ] A report is a closed reason with optional urgent, and the urgent choice shows the help lines at once.
- [ ] Reporting blocks by default. Limits hold (one open per pair, 10 a day).
- [ ] The reporter can see the status of their reports, and nothing about the other player's outcome.
- [ ] The owner console lists reports, urgent first, and can dismiss or reset a username. Non admins get the same 404
      as any unknown page (ADR-036).
- [ ] Guest blocks and reports follow the guest into an account.
- [ ] Closed reports expire after 180 days. DATA.md and the privacy page say what is kept and for how long.
- [ ] Tests cover the change. `npm run lint`, `npm run format:check`, `npm test` pass.
- [ ] Docs updated: ADR (block, mute and report rules), DATA.md, privacy and terms pages, BACKLOG, PROGRESS.

## Plan / notes

### Data

1. `player_blocks`: `{ blockerId, blockedId, createdAt }`, unique on the pair, index on `blockedId` for the "both
   ways" check. Pseudonymous.
2. `reports`: `{ reportId, reporterId, reportedId, reportedName, reason, urgent, context: { gameId, roomCode |
matchId }, status, createdAt, closedAt, action }`, TTL on `closedAt` plus 180 days. Personal data.

### Pull requests, in order

1. **ADR and blocks (M).** The ADR. `player_blocks` DAO, accessor and component, routes or socket events, the seat
   menu, the refusals in `roomComponent` (seat and rematch), the transfer step in `playerTransferComponent`,
   the profile list. CC-051 builds on this.
2. **Reports (M).** `reports`, the form with reasons and the urgent help, limits, the reporter's status list, the
   console Reports page with dismiss and username reset, the grievance text on the terms and privacy pages.

### Tests

1. Unit: the block rule both ways, the cap of 500, seat and rematch refusals with the neutral message, report limits,
   the auto block, the username reset, every route refusing an id that the player never met.
2. Integration (in-memory MongoDB): indexes, the TTL index, a guest's blocks and reports moving to an account.
3. Browser: block from the seat menu, the blocked player cannot sit in the next room, a report with urgent shows the
   help lines, the console lists it for an admin and answers 404 for others.

### Open questions for the owner

1. **Who handles reports and who is the Grievance Officer.** The owner alone at first is the only option, with a
   name published on the site as the IT Rules ask. Is the owner willing to publish their name, or name someone else?
2. The response promise: 24 hours to acknowledge, 15 days to close, urgent within 24 hours. Can the owner keep it?
3. Free text in reports for adults after CC-152: yes or never.
