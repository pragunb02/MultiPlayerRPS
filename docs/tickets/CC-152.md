## Problem

A 14 year old signs up and plays. Part 1 (#82) gave ClassiCade the age signal (a year of birth, the under 13
refusal, "Hidden player" on the boards), but each feature then decided for itself what a minor may do: boards,
profiles, profile cards and feedback each asked for "the adults" and wrote their own rule, and nothing told the
next features (friends, emotes, circles, notifications) what to read. A player whose year was wrong had no way to
get it fixed short of the owner editing the production database by hand.

## Job to be done

When a young player (or one who has not said their age) uses any social part of ClassiCade, the owner wants every
feature to apply the same safe defaults, so no feature can forget one and the law (DPDP Act section 9, the ICO
children's code) is met in one place.

## Appetite

M (up to 6 agent-hours) for part 2. **Cut line:** the owner console page goes first (the API alone is enough for a
correction); then the Hinglish lines.

## Proposed solution

1. A pure policy, `apps/server/src/domain/safety/agePolicy.js`: who the player is (adult account, minor, unknown
   age, guest; anyone else is unknown) to one frozen answer `{ canFollow, canBeFollowed, friendsMode, emotes,
quietHours, shareCardName, maxOwnedCircleSize, publicProfile, profileLink, boardName, replyEmail,
personalisation }`. `safetyComponent.policiesFor` reads the accounts' years once per list of players.
2. The features that exist read it: boards, profiles, profile cards, feedback, and the result share card in the
   browser through `GET /api/safety` (the player's own flags only, never the year or the group).
3. The owner's corrections: `PUT /api/admin/accounts/birth-year` `{ email, birthYear | null }` behind the admin
   check, and an "Ages" page in the console.
4. Hinglish and English for the age lines.

## Non-goals

- Friends (CC-052), emotes (CC-169), circles (CC-214), push and email (CC-147, CC-148, CC-293): not built yet.
  Each takes the safety component when it is. See the hand-off under Build notes.
- Verifiable parental consent (ADR-028.9), before 13 May 2027.
- Putting an adult's name on a share card. The policy allows it; CC-217 chose not to, and the feature freeze holds.
- Age verification beyond a stated year.

## Rabbit holes

- Rewriting every feature's tests for the new dependency: a test helper, `testing/fakeSafety.js`, wraps the old
  "which are adults" fakes in the real safety component, so the features' tests keep their meaning.
- A second copy of the policy in the browser: the browser keeps only the flags it assumes while waiting, and a
  test checks they equal the server's flags for an account of unknown age.

## Open decisions

None blocking. Four choices were made while building with the recommended default, written in ADR-048 (amended
2026-10-20, accepted by default) and listed for the owner below, who may change them after merge:

1. Corrections find the account by its email, not its username: the owner checks the request against that address.
2. A guest may own a circle of at most 10, like a minor (ADR-028.1 makes guests minors).
3. An adult's share card still carries no name, though the policy would allow one.
4. The Hinglish lines in `apps/web/src/components/YearSelect/ageCopy.js` are the builder's drafts.

## Instrumentation

None. Corrections write one log line (account id and admin id only).

## Threat model

1. Building: one policy every social feature reads, a read of the player's own flags, and an owner-only write.
2. What can go wrong: a minor unlocks the adult defaults (the year is set once; only the owner can change it); a
   non-owner calls the correction route (404 from `requireAdmin`, which checks the session's account against
   `ADMIN_ACCOUNT_IDS`; cross-site writes are refused by the CSRF guard); the flags leak an age (they carry no year
   or group); the log leaks personal data (it names no year or email); a new feature forgets the rule (the source
   scan test).
3. Good enough: every refusal has a test, including the integration test through the real composition root.

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** the year of birth (part 1), and now `accounts.birthYearCorrectedAt` when the owner corrects it.
- **Retention:** until the account is deleted, like the year.
- **Who sees it:** the player (profile, export); the owner (console, on request); logs carry only the ids.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** every default in the policy table; unknown age and guests count as minors.
- **Defaults:** the high privacy defaults; only the owner can change a year once set.

## Alternatives considered

- Each feature keeps its own check (as before): the next feature can forget it. Rejected.
- Send the age group to the browser: tells a child which age unlocks what (FTC FAQ). Rejected for flags only.
- Let players change their year freely: a minor unlocks the adult defaults with one click (ADR-048.9). Rejected.

## Research (written before any code)

### 1. How our code does it today

1. Sign-up asks for a username, an email and a password (`POST /api/auth/signup`, `SignupPage.jsx`). Nothing about
   age is asked or stored. The privacy page (section 8) says ClassiCade "is meant for people aged 13 and over".
2. ADR-028.1: unknown age means minor. Guests and accounts without a stated birth year get the under-18 defaults.
   PRODUCT_VISION.md §5.12 lists them: a friends-only private profile, emotes off, not followable, circles by invite
   link only, **the name never shown on public boards, share cards or feeds**, no push at night, no behavioural
   personalisation.
3. Which of those features exist on `main` today:

   | Feature                        | On `main`?                                         | Default needed now                          |
   | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
   | Public leaderboards (ADR-015)  | yes, every account by username, readable by anyone | **hide the name of a minor or unknown age** |
   | Public profile (CC-034)        | no                                                 | none yet                                    |
   | Emotes (CC-169)                | no                                                 | none yet                                    |
   | Friends, follow, circles       | no                                                 | none yet                                    |
   | Share cards, feeds             | no. The room share is a link with no name in it    | none yet                                    |
   | Push, reminders                | no                                                 | none yet                                    |
   | Personalisation, adaptive bots | no (CC-150 not built)                              | none yet                                    |
   | Telemetry                      | no, and ADR-034 makes it anonymous for everyone    | none                                        |
   | Rooms and the opponent's name  | yes, but only to people who have the room's link   | none: the player chose to share the link    |

   So the one default to build today is the leaderboards. Every later feature reads the same age group.

4. Every account today has no birth year, so on release **every name on the public leaderboards is hidden** until
   its owner states a birth year that makes them an adult. That is what ADR-028.1 asks for. It is a visible change
   on production, so the PR says it.

### 2. The law and how other products do it

Sources fetched on 2026-10-18.

1. **DPDP Act section 9.** A child is anyone under 18. Processing a child's data needs "verifiable consent of the
   parent", and a fiduciary "shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted
   advertising directed at children" (https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section9.html). These apply from
   13 May 2027 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023).
2. **DPDP Rules 2025, rule 10.** Before processing a child's data the fiduciary checks that the person consenting
   is an identifiable adult, using "reliable details of identity and age" it already holds, or details or "a
   virtual token" from an authorised entity such as DigiLocker (https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule10.html). The
   Rules do not require age verification of every user. A stated age is the usual first step, and the consent step
   for those who say they are under 18 is ADR-028.9, before 13 May 2027.
3. **UK Children's Code, standard 3.** Self-declaration "may suit low-risk processing". It recommends "neutral
   presentation of age declaration screens (rather than nudging towards the selection of certain ages)" and
   preventing users from "immediately resubmitting a new age" after a refusal, and warns against forcing hard
   identifiers (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/3-age-appropriate-application/).
4. **US FTC, COPPA FAQ H.3 and D.7.** A neutral age screen lets the user "freely enter month and year of birth". A
   drop-down that stops at the age limit is not neutral, and neither is a box saying "I am over 12". Do not tell
   users in advance that features are unavailable below an age, and stop the back button from re-entering a
   different age (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions).
5. **Practice in games.** PRODUCT_VISION.md §5 cites the FTC's 2022 Epic order: voice and text chat must be off
   by default for children and teens (https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/12/fortnite-video-game-maker-epic-games-pay-more-half-billion-dollars-over-ftc-allegations,
   2022-12-19). Large games ask for a birth date with no default and then switch on child settings without a
   message about features (for example Epic's cabined accounts). That last point is from memory, not re-fetched
   today.

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **Ask only the year.** It is enough for every rule we have, it is less data than a date, and the vision says not
   to collect more (§6.10 "Skip"). Stored as a four-digit number on the account, never a date.
2. **A neutral year select.** Label "Year of birth", no default (the first entry is "Choose a year"), every year from
   this year back 100 years in order, and "I'd rather not say". No hint that any year matters, no "you must be", no
   checkbox. Saying nothing is fine and gives the safe defaults.
3. **Age groups, worked out from the year only.** In India time, this year minus the birth year is the age the
   player reaches this year.
   1. 19 or more: an adult, certainly 18 already.
   2. 18: still counts as a minor until 1 January, because the birthday may not have come yet. Uncertain counts as
      a minor, like unknown.
   3. 13 to 17: a minor.
   4. Under 13 this whole year: sign-up is refused with "ClassiCade accounts are for people aged 13 and over. You can
      keep playing as a guest." This keeps the promise in the privacy page (we do not knowingly keep accounts of
      under 13s). The refusal is given only after the choice, as the FTC guidance allows. The form keeps the refused
      choice and does not let the year be changed and sent again in the same visit, which is the re-entry guard the
      codes describe, without storing anything in the browser.
   5. No year: unknown, so a minor.
4. **Existing accounts are unknown** until they set it. The profile's account section shows "Year of birth: not set"
   with the same select. It can be set once. A mistake is corrected by the owner on request, because a free change
   would let a minor unlock adult defaults with one click. Owner to confirm.
5. **The default built now: leaderboards.** A minor or unknown age account stays on the board and keeps its rank and
   score, but everyone sees "Hidden player" instead of the name. The player still sees "you" on their own row. Guests
   are never on boards (ADR-015). The age group is read at the moment the board is read, so stating a birth year
   shows the name at once and nothing stale is stored.
6. **The session answer does not carry the age.** Only `GET /api/account` does, for the player themself.

### 5. The law and the codes, for defaults (fetched 2026-10-20)

1. **DPDP Act section 9(3):** "A Data Fiduciary shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children
   or targeted advertising directed at children." 9(2): no processing "likely to cause any detrimental effect on
   the well-being of a child" (https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section9.html). A child is anyone under 18.
2. **DPDP Rules 2025, rule 10(1):** "appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure that verifiable
   consent of the parent is obtained" before processing a child's data (https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule10.html).
   Rule 12 and the Fourth Schedule exempt some classes of fiduciary and some purposes
   (https://www.dpdpa.com/dpdparules/rule12.html); a games site is not among them as we read it, for the lawyer to
   confirm. The consent step stays ADR-028.9, before 13 May 2027.
3. **ICO children's code** (https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/):
   1. Standard 3: establish age with certainty fit for the risk, or apply the code's standards to all users. That is
      our "unknown means minor" (ADR-028.1).
   2. Standard 7, default settings: "Settings must be 'high privacy' by default", and a child's data is "only
      visible or accessible to other users of the service if the child amends their settings".
   3. Standard 12, profiling: "Switch options which use profiling 'off' by default".
   4. Standard 13, nudges: among things detrimental to wellbeing, "timed notifications that respond to inactivity";
      for younger bands, "activation delays" for notifications.

### 6. How other products do it

1. **Instagram Teen Accounts** (17 September 2024,
   https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-teen-accounts): "default private accounts, teens need to
   accept new followers"; "they can only be messaged by people they follow or are already connected to"; "Sleep
   mode will be turned on between 10 PM and 7 AM", with notifications muted. The closest match to our list: private
   by default, no strangers, quiet at night.
2. **Roblox** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox, read 2026-10-20): November 2024 parental controls for under 13s,
   including whether a child "can exchange private messages with other users"; from 2025 "Users can only
   communicate with each other after they perform an age check". Talking to others is the risky part, so that is
   what age gates.
3. **Epic Games** (FTC order, 19 December 2022, cited in part 1): voice and text chat off by default for children
   and teens. Epic's "cabined accounts" turn features off without telling the child which age unlocks them (general
   knowledge, medium confidence: Epic's help page answered 403 today).
4. **The common shape.** Each keeps the rule in one account-level state that every feature reads (Instagram's Teen
   Account, Roblox's age band, Epic's cabined flag). None lets each feature work the age out itself, because then a
   new feature can forget.

### 7. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **One pure policy, one reader.** `apps/server/src/domain/safety/agePolicy.js` turns who the player is (an adult
   account, a minor, an account of unknown age, a guest) into one frozen answer. `safetyComponent.policiesFor` reads
   the birth years once for a list of players. Features take the safety component and read fields. No feature
   imports `ageGroupFor` or asks for adults itself any more, and a test fails if one does.
2. **The fields,** each tied to its rule:

   | Field                | Adult         | Minor, unknown age          | Guest          | Rule                       |
   | -------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------- |
   | `canFollow`          | true          | false                       | false          | ADR-028.5                  |
   | `canBeFollowed`      | true          | false                       | false          | ADR-028.5                  |
   | `friendsMode`        | `mutual`      | `mutual-known`              | `none`         | ADR-028.2, CC-052 rule 3   |
   | `emotes`             | `on`          | `off`                       | `off`          | ADR-028.6, CC-169          |
   | `quietHours`         | none          | 22:00 to 07:00 Asia/Kolkata | same           | CC-052 rule 3              |
   | `shareCardName`      | true          | false                       | false          | ADR-028.7                  |
   | `maxOwnedCircleSize` | 50            | 10                          | 10             | ADR-028.3, ADR-028.4.4     |
   | `publicProfile`      | true (opt in) | false                       | false          | ADR-065.4                  |
   | `profileLink`        | true          | false                       | false          | ADR-065.2                  |
   | `boardName`          | `own`         | `hidden`                    | `server-given` | ADR-048.10, ADR-060        |
   | `replyEmail`         | true          | false                       | false          | CC-290, ADR-059            |
   | `personalisation`    | true          | false                       | false          | DPDP 9(3), ICO standard 12 |

   `mutual-known`: friends are mutual and come only from someone the minor already met, a game played together or
   a friend link the minor chose to share. Never from search, a board or a stranger's profile. Guests get `none`
   because friends need an account (CC-052, owner).

3. **The client gets only its own flags** (`GET /api/safety`), never an age or a rule about which age unlocks what
   (FTC FAQ, part 1).
4. **Corrections through the owner.** An owner-only API, `PUT /api/admin/accounts/birth-year` with the account's email
   and a year (or `null` to clear it), behind `requireAdmin` (404 to everyone else). It keeps the under 13 refusal (that
   account is deleted instead), stores when it was corrected, and logs the account id and the admin id, never the
   year. The owner checks the request first, by a message from the account's own email address.
5. **Production data.** Nothing is migrated. An account with no year stays unknown, so a minor. The one new optional
   field, `birthYearCorrectedAt`, is written only by a correction. The legacy `users` collection is not touched.

## Build notes

Decision record: ADR-048.

1. **Changed from the research while building.**
   1. The picker starts at "I'd rather not say" instead of "Choose a year", and the year is optional. A forced
      choice adds a step for everyone, and starting at the choice that shares the least is the high privacy default
      the Children's Code asks for. It also keeps every existing sign-up journey working.
   2. The page checks the minimum age itself, so a child's year is never sent anywhere. The server checks again
      for any other caller.
2. **The rule** lives once, in `packages/shared/src/ages.js` (`ageGroupFor`, `isTooYoungForAccount`,
   `birthYearChoices`), and the server works out "this year" in India time (`utils/indiaTime.js`, `yearOf`).
3. **Server.** `accounts.birthYear` (optional, set once by `setBirthYear` only while absent). `POST
/api/auth/signup` takes an optional `birthYear`. `GET /api/account` and `PUT /api/account/birth-year`.
