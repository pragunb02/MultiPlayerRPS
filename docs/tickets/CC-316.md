Shaped on 2026-10-20 from the owner's request that the UI design be "decided and checked every now and then",
because many features are adding text and pop-ups. It checks the contract of [CC-314](CC-314.md) and the budget of
[CC-315](CC-315.md), and reads the friction numbers of CC-313 (being shaped in parallel).

## Problem

The design lens is one word in the spec review list (CLAUDE.md, "Decision-first tickets" point 4; PROCESS.md §4):
"design (for anything players see)". It has no checklist, so each reviewer looks at different things, and findings
below high severity become follow-up tickets (CLAUDE.md working agreement rule 9) that nobody gathers. No one looks
at the product as a whole: each PR is reviewed alone, so ten reasonable PRs can add ten lines of text and three
pop-ups to one screen and no review sees the sum. Tickets describe screens in prose, so the design is decided in
review, after the build, which is what CC-311 set out to stop. We have the material for a real review (the CC-155
screenshots of every screen in light and dark, the CC-183 axe scans, CC-313's friction numbers soon) and no habit of
looking at it together.

## Job to be done

When the product grows feature by feature, the owner wants someone to look at the whole of it on a fixed rhythm and
before each big ramp, so it stays calm and readable, and problems are caught while they are small.

When a builder starts a player-visible ticket, they want the screen already decided, so they build it once.

## Appetite

S (up to 2 agent-hours) to set up: the review procedure, the lens checklist, the template section and a reminder.
Each review after that is about 1 agent-hour and is its own small docs PR. **Cut line:** the scheduled reminder
workflow (the coordinator keeps the date in PROGRESS.md instead), then the CI presence check of the UI decision
section.

## Proposed solution

### 1. The review, every 2 weeks and before every ramp past 50 %

The coordinator runs it with a reviewer agent and writes it to `docs/ux/reviews/YYYY-MM-DD.md` (the template in
`docs/ux/reviews/README.md`). Five parts, each with its source:

| Part                       | What is looked at                                                                                                                                                                               | Source                                                      |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Heuristics                 | Nielsen's ten heuristics over the main journeys (first visit to first move, a full game to Rematch, a guest saving progress, a challenge from a friend), each finding rated 0 to 4              | NN/g ten heuristics and severity ratings                    |
| Accessibility, WCAG 2.2 AA | The axe results on `main` (CC-183), plus by hand what axe cannot see: focus order and focus not obscured (2.4.11), target size (2.5.8), reflow at 320 px (1.4.10), text spacing, reduced motion | `e2e/accessibility.spec.js`, WCAG 2.2                       |
| Screens                    | The CC-155 screenshots (desktop and phone, light and dark) of every screen, against the CC-314 contract: components used, most per screen, colours by role                                      | `e2e/visual-baselines/` on `main`, the refresh PRs (CC-300) |
| Friction                   | CC-313's friction numbers since the last review, and CC-315's notice counts (shown, waited, folded during play)                                                                                 | The owner console                                           |
| Copy                       | Every string added since the last review (git diff of the string files and components), against the CC-314 lengths, tone (no guilt, no shaming, ADR-050) and Hinglish first where used          | `git log` since the last review                             |

**Output:** a dated file with every finding (heuristic or criterion, screen, severity, screenshot link), a short
"what got better, what got worse", and one line per open finding saying where it went. Severity 3 and 4 become a
ticket each, at once. Severity 1 and 2 are gathered into one ticket per review. A severity 3 or 4 finding on a
flagged feature **blocks its ramp past 50 %** until fixed or accepted by the owner in writing.

**Before a ramp past 50 %:** the same review, narrowed to the flagged feature's screens and its CC-313 numbers on
against off, written as a section of the next review file or its own file.

**Reminder:** a scheduled GitHub Actions workflow (`ux-review.yml`, every other Monday 04:30 UTC, like the uptime
schedule of ADR-043) opens one issue "UI and UX review due" with the checklist, read-only token, no project code
run. The coordinator closes it with the review PR.

### 2. The design lens, tightened to the CC-314 contract

`docs/PROCESS.md` §4 gets a "Design lens" checklist that the design reviewer uses on every player-visible PR and
every ticket at spec review:

1. Every new or changed element is one of the CC-314 components, in a state the contract lists. A new pop-up type
   is refused unless the contract changed first.
2. Colours by role: status roles for status, player roles for players, never colour alone.
3. Most per screen respected (one dialog, one banner, the toast limit, one primary button per region).
4. Notices go through the CC-315 centre with a category; nothing new interrupts play.
5. Copy within the CC-314 lengths, no guilt or shaming words, the Hinglish line where the screen has one.
6. The PR's screenshots (desktop and phone, light and dark) match the ticket's UI decision section.
7. Reduced motion and keyboard: the state is reachable and readable with both.

A finding of the lens that breaks points 1 to 4 is a **missing requirement** (the contract is a requirement) and
blocks the merge, which fits the rule that reviews block only on correctness, security or a missing requirement.
Points 5 to 7 below high severity go to the next review's gathered ticket.

### 3. A "UI decision" section in the ticket template

`docs/tickets/TEMPLATE.md` gets a section after Proposed solution, required for any ticket that changes what players
see:

- **Screens and states:** each screen or state this adds or changes, with a sketch or a link to a screenshot.
- **Components:** which CC-314 components, in which states.
- **Notices:** each toast, banner or dialog, its CC-315 category, and when it shows.
- **Copy:** every new string, with its length, Hinglish and English.
- **Screenshots to add:** the CC-155 baselines this ticket changes or adds.

`scripts/tickets/checkTickets.mjs` checks that a full ticket at Ready or later whose type is `feature` has the
section, or the words "No player-visible change".

## Non-goals

- No outside user research, panels or paid testing tools (no budget, and no players to recruit yet).
- No new metrics: the numbers come from CC-313 and CC-315.
- No redesign: the review finds problems, tickets fix them.
- Not the contract itself (CC-314) or the notice budget (CC-315).

## Rabbit holes

- **Reviewing everything every time.** Each review covers what changed since the last one, plus the four journeys.
  The whole product is reviewed in full once a quarter (every sixth review).
- **Heuristic scores as a number to chase.** The score is not a metric; the findings are.
- **A reminder bot with write access to the code.** It only opens an issue.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** Given `docs/ux/reviews/README.md`, when a reviewer follows it, then it lists the five parts, the four
  journeys, the severity scale 0 to 4, where each finding goes, and the ramp rule. Test: review of the PR, and
  `scripts/docs.test.mjs` › "the review template has its five parts" (a heading check).
- **AC-2** Given a full `feature` ticket at Ready or later without a "UI decision" section or the words "No
  player-visible change", when `check-tickets.mjs` runs, then it fails with the file and the missing section. Test:
  `scripts/tickets/checkTickets.test.mjs` › "a feature ticket at Ready needs a UI decision section".
- **AC-3** Given a ticket written before CC-311 (emoji status) or a ticket at Shaping or Spec review, when the check
  runs, then it is not checked for the section. Test: `checkTickets.test.mjs` › "older and unready tickets are not
  checked for a UI decision".
- **AC-4** Given `docs/tickets/TEMPLATE.md`, when read, then it has the UI decision section with its five parts.
  Test: `checkTickets.test.mjs` › "the template has every section the check needs".
- **AC-5** Given `docs/PROCESS.md`, when read, then §4 has the seven-point design lens and says which points block.
  Test: review of the PR.
- **AC-6** Given the schedule, when it fires, then one issue "UI and UX review due" opens with the checklist, and a
  second run while one is open opens none. Test: `actionlint` passes, and a `workflow_dispatch` run on the branch
  shows one issue (link in the PR).
- **AC-7** Given the first review, when merged, then `docs/ux/reviews/2026-10-22.md` (or the answered date) exists
  with every part filled and each severity 3 or 4 finding linked to a ticket. Test: review of that PR.

## Instrumentation

None. The review reads existing numbers (CC-313, CC-315, CC-183, CC-155).

## Threat model

1. **What are we building?** A process, a template section, a ticket check and a scheduled workflow that opens an
   issue.
2. **What can go wrong?** The workflow gets more rights than it needs, or a review file shows a real player's
   name in a screenshot (the repo is public).
3. **What are we doing about it?** The workflow has `permissions: issues: write` only, runs no project code, pins
   its actions by commit. Reviews link only CC-155 baselines and `pr-evidence` images, which use test accounts
   only (ADR-039 amendment of 2026-10-19). Friction numbers are anonymous counts with `"<5"` (ADR-036).
4. **Good enough?** Yes.

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** none collected. Reviews quote anonymous counts and test-account screenshots.
- **Retention:** review files stay in git.
- **Who sees it:** anyone (public repo), so nothing about a real player goes in.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** nothing changes for them. The review checks that minors' screens keep their safer defaults.
- **Defaults:** not applicable.

## Alternatives considered

- **Only the per-PR lens** (today): it never sees the sum of many PRs.
- **A review once a quarter**: too slow for a product that merges several player-visible PRs a day.
- **Automated heuristic scoring tools**: none judges "is this the right component"; axe and the CC-154 probe already
  automate what can be.

## Research (written before any code)

### 1. How our code does it today

Read on 2026-10-20 on `main` (a5a801c8). The review lenses are named in CLAUDE.md and PROCESS.md §4 (code, security,
design, then a skeptic) and in ADR-072 point 5; only code and security have long-standing practice (ADR-039 point
4). The design lens has no checklist. Design findings below high severity become follow-up tickets (working
agreement rule 9). `check-tickets.mjs` checks decisions and acceptance criteria ids from Ready on. CC-155 gives 56
baseline images of 14 screens (desktop and phone, light and dark, ADR-056), refreshed after merge by CC-300.
`e2e/accessibility.spec.js` scans every screen with axe (WCAG 2.0 to 2.2, A and AA) in light and dark (CC-183).

### 2. How others do it (sources read 2026-10-20 unless marked)

| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| NN/g, [10 usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) (Jakob Nielsen, 1994-04-24, reviewed 2024-01-30)                                                                                                  | The ten heuristics, from visibility of system status to help and documentation                                                         | The heuristic part of the review                                    |

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

A product built by many agents in parallel needs one place where the whole is seen. Two weeks matches how fast
player-visible PRs land and is cheap with the screenshots we already make. The ramp past 50 % is the right second
trigger: before it, few players saw the feature and a fix is cheap; after it, most have. Tying the lens to the
written contract turns design review from taste into a check, so it can block on a missing requirement without
blocking on opinion.
