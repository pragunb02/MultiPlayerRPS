## Problem

A player in Dubai finishes a match at 23:00 her time. Our streaks and quests count UTC days, so the match lands on
"tomorrow" for the streak, her quests already reset at 04:00 in the morning, and the daily puzzle turned at 22:30
because it follows India days. Three features, three different midnights, none of them hers. ADR-013 was amended
on 2026-10-17 (PRODUCT_VISION D6) to say days follow the player's own time zone. Nothing builds it yet.

## Job to be done

When I play late in the evening, I want "today" to mean my today, so that my streak, my quests and the daily puzzle
all turn over at my midnight and the times on screen are my clock times.

## Appetite

M (up to 6 agent-hours, shared with CC-145 on one branch). **Cut line:** first the local reset clock on the quest
card (keep the countdown), then the week rule for the boards (stays UTC, as today), then the time zone line on the
streak card.

## Proposed solution

1. **One calendar per player** (`domain/playerDays/zoneCalendar.js`, pure): `dayOf(ms)` and `nextDayAt(ms)` for an
   IANA zone, worked out with `Intl` so summer time is right everywhere, including zones whose midnight does not exist
   on the change day. Unknown zone means India time (`Asia/Kolkata`).
2. **The browser says its zone.** When the `local_days` flag is on, the web app reads
   `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone` once a visit and, if it differs from the server's, sends
   `PUT /api/time-zone { zone }`. `GET /api/time-zone` answers the zone in use, whether the player chose it, and when
   it may change next.
3. **At most one change in 7 days**, counted from the last change, the first one included. A change inside the 7
   days is refused with 409 and `changeableAt`. Sending the zone already in use changes nothing and starts no lock.
4. **A change never repeats or skips a day.** The old zone keeps the current day until its own midnight. From then
   on the new zone applies, but never earlier than the day after the old one. So moving west cannot replay a day
   (no second set of quests, no repaired streak), and moving east up to 24 hours never skips one.
5. **Who uses it.** The play streak and quests (were UTC), the daily puzzle and its streak (were India, ADR-061.3:
   the injected calendar CC-144 left for this). Leaderboards keep weeks, now Monday 00:00 India time (ADR-015
   amended 2026-10-17), for everyone at once. Analytics, the console, flag counts and the anti-farm caps stay on
   India days (ADR-034, ADR-021).
6. **Reset times in local time.** The quest card adds the clock time of the reset ("at 12:00 am"), and the streak
   card says when the day ends, both formatted by the browser in its own zone. The puzzle page already does.
7. **Old data, read as it is.** Stats written before the switch keep UTC day keys. They are read with a marker
   (`dailyStreak.days`): a UTC day is taken as the latest local day it overlaps, never later than today. So the
   switch can never break a streak; at most it gives one day once. Nothing is rewritten in bulk, and no legacy
   collection is touched.

## Non-goals

- A time zone picker in settings. The endpoint takes one later without change.
- Analytics or console days in local time (ADR-034 stays).
- Per-player weeks for the boards (one board, one week for everyone).
- Notifications at local times (CC-147, with the quiet hours of CC-152).

## Rabbit holes

- **Time zone maths.** No library: `Intl` is in Node and every browser, and a binary search finds a day's first
  moment in any zone. Tested with summer-time zones on their change days.
- **Re-keying history.** Old quest days and puzzle days keep their keys. Only the streak's last day is read through
  the marker.
- **Flag flips.** Turning `local_days` off goes back to UTC days for streaks and quests and India for the puzzle.
  The marker makes the next switch on safe again. Turning it off can, once, show a west-of-UTC streak as broken.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** Given a zone with summer time, when the day is worked out around the change, then each day starts at the
  zone's own midnight (or its first moment when midnight does not exist) and lasts 23 or 25 hours. Test:
  `zoneCalendar.test.js` › "starts each day at the zone's own midnight across summer time"
- **AC-2** Given a moment one millisecond before and at local midnight, then the days differ by one. Test:
  `zoneCalendar.test.js` › "turns the day at local midnight, not UTC midnight"
- **AC-3** Given no zone or a zone that is not a real IANA zone, then India time is used and the bad zone is
  refused with 400. Test: `zoneCalendar.test.js` › "uses India time when the zone is unknown" and
  `playerDaysComponent.test.js` › "refuses a zone that is not a real time zone"
- **AC-4** Given a zone set less than 7 days ago, when another is sent, then it is refused with `changeableAt`; at 7
  days it is accepted; the same zone again changes nothing. Test: `playerDaysComponent.test.js` › "allows one change
  in 7 days"
- **AC-5** Given a change west, then no day repeats; given a change east, then no day is skipped. Test:
  `zoneCalendar.test.js` › "a change west never repeats a day" and "a change east never skips a day"
- **AC-6** Given `local_days` on and a player in New York, when a match ends at 23:30 New York time, then the streak
  and the quests count it on the New York day, and `resetsAt` is New York midnight. Test:
  `gamificationComponent.localDays.test.js` › "counts streaks and quests on the player's own day"
- **AC-7** Given a UTC streak stored before the switch, when it is read on local days, then it is not broken. Test:
  `streaks.test.js` › "reads an old UTC streak without breaking it"
- **AC-8** Given `local_days` on, the daily puzzle follows the player's day. Test:
  `dailyPuzzleComponent.test.js` › "follows the player's own day when a calendar is given per player"
- **AC-9** Given `local_days` fully on, the weekly boards turn at Monday 00:00 India time; off, at Monday 00:00 UTC.
  Test: `isoWeek.test.js` › "turns at Monday 00:00 India time with the India offset"
- **AC-10** Given a guest with a zone who signs in to an account without one, the zone moves; with one, the
  account's stays. Export lists the zone and erase removes it. Test: `playerDaysComponent.test.js` › "moves a guest's
  zone only when the account has none" and "exports and erases the zone"
- **AC-11** Given `local_days` on, the web app sends the browser's zone once when it differs, and the quest card shows
  the reset as a local clock time. Test: `useTimeZoneSync.test.jsx` › "sends the browser's zone once when it differs"
  and `gamification.test.jsx` › "shows the quest reset as a local clock time"
- **AC-12** Given `local_days` off, nothing changes: UTC days for streaks and quests, India for the puzzle, and the
  time zone endpoint answers 404. Test: `gamificationRoutes.test.js` › "with the local_days flag off (CC-312)"

## Instrumentation

None new. The flag's exposures and successes (ADR-073.6) are anonymous counts. Success is "a player's zone was set
from the browser". No zone is ever logged or counted.

## Threat model

We store one IANA zone name per player and use it to work out days. A player could fake the zone to repeat a day,
farm a second set of quests or repair a streak: the 7 day lock and the no-repeat rule (D3) cap that at nothing. A
bad string could break the maths or be stored as junk: the server accepts only names `Intl` knows, at most 64
characters. The zone is coarse location: it is never logged, shown to anyone else, or counted, and it is in the
export and erased with the account. Good enough: the worst case is one player's day being off by hours.

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** the player's IANA time zone (for example `Asia/Dubai`), the one before it, and when it changed. Coarse
  location of the player's own device.
- **Retention:** as long as the player's stats. Erased with the account (ADR-048) and moved with a guest (ADR-014).
- **Who sees it:** the player, in their export. Nobody else; no logs, no counts, not in the console.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** the same, because a day is a core function, not personalisation (ICO standard 12 allows
  what the service needs). Nothing is shown to others.
- **Defaults:** India time until the browser sends a zone; only while the flag is on.

## Alternatives considered

- **UTC for everyone** (today): wrong for every player outside UTC, and India is our main market.
- **India for everyone** (the puzzle today): right for India only.
- **A time zone library** (Luxon, date-fns-tz): `Intl` does it with no dependency (ADR-008).
- **24-hour windows like Snapchat**: no shared "today", so quests and the daily puzzle would make no sense.

- **Ramp:** admins by override first, then 10 %, 50 %, on. The board week rule switches only at on.
- **Rollback trigger:** support reports of a lost streak or quests that reset at the wrong time; more than 1 % of
  zone sends refused.

  Stored zones stay until the player is erased, and are used again when the flag comes back.

## Research (written before any code)

1. **How our code does it today.** `domain/gamification/calendar.js` gives UTC days to streaks and quests
   (`utcDay`, `nextUtcMidnight`). `domain/dailyPuzzles/schedule.js` has `INDIA_CALENDAR`, injected into the puzzle
   component so CC-146 can give each player their own (ADR-061.3), but the calendar takes no player, so the
   component needs a per-player lookup. `utils/indiaTime.js` is a fixed +05:30 offset for the console. Weekly boards
   use `domain/leaderboards/isoWeek.js` in UTC, although ADR-015 was amended to India time "built with CC-146".
   The web shows the quest reset as a countdown only; the puzzle page formats `nextAt` in the browser's zone.
2. **How others do it.**
   - **Duolingo** counts a streak by calendar days and sells or gives Streak Freezes for a missed day; its blog
     says nothing about time zones, so travel rules are its own product choice (Osman Mansur, "The habit-building
     research behind your Duolingo streak", Duolingo blog, 31 January 2022, read 2026-10-21).
   - **Wordle** (New York Times) gives a new puzzle at the player's local midnight, so players in different zones are
     on different puzzles for some hours. Our puzzle numbers work the same way.
   - **Snapchat** Snapstreaks lapse when "more than a day" passes without a Snap, with an hourglass when one "is about
     to expire" (Snapchat Support, "What are Snapstreaks?", read 2026-10-21). Rolling windows need no time zone but
     give no shared "today", so quests and a daily puzzle would make no sense.
   - The IANA tz database is the source for every zone; `Intl.DateTimeFormat` reads it in V8 and every browser
     (ECMA-402). Summer time changes at midnight in some zones (for example Chile, `America/Santiago`), so a day's
     start must be searched, not assumed to be 00:00.
3. **What is best for ClassiCade.** Local days from the browser, India when unknown (most players), a one-in-7
   change lock so a fake zone gains nothing, and a change rule that never repeats or skips a day. No library.
   Migrate on read so production data stays readable and no bulk job runs. Analytics stay on India days so the
   console's numbers keep one day for everyone.
