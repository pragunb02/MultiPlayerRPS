## Why

The owner played a game and found chess "very normal": no dragging, and nothing on screen said the pieces could
be moved. Players coming from chess.com and lichess.org expect to drag pieces, see where a piece can go, look
back through the moves, and download the game. Without that, chess feels like a demo rather than a product.

## What the big sites do (and what we copied)

Both chess.com and lichess.org let a player drag a piece or click it and then click the target. A picked up
piece follows the pointer, larger and with a shadow, while its square keeps a faint copy. Legal moves are dots
on empty squares and rings on captures, the last move and a king in check are tinted, and a drop anywhere else
puts the piece back. The cursor is a hand over your own pieces and a closed hand while dragging. Coordinates sit
in the corner squares. The pieces each side took and the material lead sit beside each clock, and a clock turns
red when time is short. The move list is clickable, and the arrow keys step through it. Promotion shows the four
pieces in a column over the promotion square, not a separate dialog. The result says why the game ended, and the
game can be downloaded as PGN.

## Scope

- In (all in `apps/web/src/games/chess/`, no server change):
  1. Drag and drop with mouse, pen and finger (pointer events), plus click or tap to move. Cursors grab, grabbing
     and pointer. A lifted copy with a shadow follows the pointer, the square under it is marked, and a drop on a
     square the piece cannot reach puts it back and leaves it picked up so a click can finish the move. A press
     that moves less than 4 pixels stays a click.
  2. Keyboard: the board is one Tab stop, the arrow keys walk the squares (mirrored for Black), Enter picks up
     and puts down, Escape puts down. Screen reader labels kept as before ("e4, white pawn, legal move").
  3. Legal move dots and capture rings, last move and check tints, rank and file letters, flip board.
  4. Pieces slide into place with Motion (`m.img` inside `LazyMotion`). A dragged piece does not slide again.
     Castling slides the rook too. Stepping back through the history slides the piece backwards. Nothing slides
     for a player with reduced motion.
  5. A move is shown at once, before the server answers, and taken back if the server refuses it.
  6. Player strips: name, pieces taken, material lead, clock. Under 20 seconds the clock turns red, and the
     player hears "Less than 20 seconds left on your clock" once (assertive live region). The last move is
     announced politely ("Riya played Nf3").
  7. Move list in chess notation with first, previous, next and latest buttons, clickable moves, and the left
     and right arrow keys anywhere outside the board and form fields. Looking back is view only: the old
     position cannot be played on, and "Looking at move 2 of 5." says so. Numbers and moves are never cut off:
     in production the old list showed "10." as "0", because its numbers sat in a fixed 3 character column. The
     list is now one grid whose rows share its columns (subgrid). The number column is as wide as the widest
     number ("100."), the move columns as wide as the longest move ("exd8=Q+"), and they line up in every row.
  8. Promotion picker over the board like the big sites (queen first, nearest the square), from the top or the
     bottom edge depending on the board's orientation. Escape or a click on the dimmed board takes the move back.
  9. Game over panel: the reason (checkmate, resignation, time out, stalemate, threefold repetition, fifty-move
     rule, insufficient material, draw by agreement, time out with too little material, opponent left), the
     winner, the score (1–0, 0–1, ½–½), and **Download PGN**, built in the browser from the move list (headers:
     Event, Site, Date, White, Black, Result, TimeControl, Termination).
- Out (follow-ups, not ticketed here because the board is not edited by this workstream):
  1. Premoves (playing your next move during the opponent's turn).
  2. Sounds and the draw offer popup: CC-103 and CC-104 build them from room updates. Every chess event they
     need is derivable from the RoomView (`moves` gives the SAN with `x`, `+`, `#`, `O-O`; `lastMove` the
     squares).
  3. A replay viewer for past games from the profile (rest of CC-041).
  4. A board that always fits a short laptop window. Today the board is up to 36rem wide, so on a 720 pixel tall
     window the page scrolls a little. The e2e helpers scroll the board into view before dragging.

## ADR-016 (accepted 2026-10-17, recorded in DECISIONS.md) — Motion for interaction animation

**Context.** Chess needs pieces that slide between squares, and CC-092 plans the same for toasts, dialogs and
results. CSS transitions cannot animate a piece from one square to another when React moves the element to a new
parent, and writing a small animation engine by hand would be code we maintain forever.

**Decision.** Use `motion` (14.3.0, latest stable, MIT, no native build step), imported only from `motion/react`.

1. Wrap animated parts in `components/Motion/MotionProvider.jsx`: `LazyMotion` with `strict`, so only the small
   `m.*` components can be used, and `MotionConfig reducedMotion="user"`.
2. The `domAnimation` features load from their own file (`motionFeatures.js`) after the board has rendered.
   Something that would animate before they arrive simply appears in place.
3. Components that must not move at all for a player with reduced motion also check `useReducedMotion()` (the
   chess board skips slides entirely, as the celebrations do).
4. Motion animates by setting `element.style` from script. The CSP (CC-030) blocks style attributes written in
   markup, not styles set from script, so no CSP change is needed. The e2e `players` fixture would fail on any
   CSP violation.

**Cost.** Measured with `npm run build:web` (gzip): the chess chunk grew from 13.8 kB to 32.9 kB (our new board
code plus the Motion core), and `motionFeatures` is a separate 17.9 kB file loaded only on the chess page. The
main bundle is unchanged (118.3 kB). Pages without chess load none of it.

**Alternatives.** CSS transitions and the Web Animations API with hand written measuring (no dependency, but every
future animation needs its own measuring code). `react-spring` (heavier for the same features, less used).
Framer Motion under its old name (the same library, now published as `motion`).

## Plan / notes

1. `chessGame.js` holds the pure helpers: board geometry for drawing and for finding the square under a pointer,
   replaying the SAN list into positions, material, castling rook moves, end reason texts, the PGN result and
   the PGN itself. chess.js runs only in the browser to show legal moves and earlier positions. The server still
   checks every move and decides the result.
2. `useBoardInput.js` turns clicks, pointer drags and keys into moves. Pointer moves are kept in a ref, not state,
   because they arrive far faster than React renders. After a real drag the board captures the pointer, so the
   square under the drop never gets the click, and a stray click some browsers still send is ignored once.
3. `ChessBoard.jsx` keeps a timeline of positions (replayed history, the server's newest position, and a move
   waiting for the server). `useSlide` decides which way pieces slide when the position on show changes by one.
4. The flip, the move being looked at and the waiting move are per tab state. A reload goes back to the latest
   position from your side, which is what players expect.
