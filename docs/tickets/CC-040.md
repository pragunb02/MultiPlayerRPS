## Why

Chess players need an opponent at any hour and at their level. The board title said "Stockfish WASM/worker"; this
ticket's engine part delivers three levels with a small engine of our own instead (ADR below). Seating the bot,
the worker thread and the UI come with the CC-038 wiring follow-up.

## Scope

- In: `packages/games/src/bots/chess/`:
  - `board.js`: a fast 0x88 board for search: legal move generation (castling, en passant, promotion, pins),
    make/unmake in place, Zobrist position fingerprints, reading the engine's move list (standard notation).
  - `search.js`: evaluation (material + Michniewski piece-square tables, endgame king table, "mop-up" bonus that
    drives a lone king to the edge) and search (negamax alpha-beta, quiescence on captures and promotions, check
    extension, killer moves, MVV-LVA ordering, iterative deepening, repetition and fifty-move draws, mate distance).
  - `chessBot.js`: the three levels on the CC-038 contract. Moves come back as `{ type: "move", from, to,
promotion? }` and go through the chess engine's `validateMove`.
- Out: resigning, offering or accepting draws; opening book; endgame tables; a transposition table (follow-up if
  Strong should get stronger); running off the main thread (wiring).

## Levels

| Level       | How it plays                                                                                                            |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Beginner    | Looks one move deep (plus captures), misjudges each move by up to 0.8 pawns, and 30 % of the time plays any legal move. |
| Club player | Iterative deepening to depth 3, at most 20,000 positions.                                                               |
| Strong      | Iterative deepening to depth 8, at most 60,000 positions (usually depth 4 to 5 in the middlegame).                      |

The budget counts **positions, not milliseconds**. Engines and bots are pure (no clock), and a position budget
makes a level play the same on every machine and every replay. Time is then a measured property (below), and a
slower machine just takes longer, it does not play differently.

## ADR-0xx (proposed): our own small chess engine, chess.js stays the referee

- **Context:** the board title assumed Stockfish (WASM in a worker). Our levels top out at "Strong club player",
  and the bot must be pure and deterministic with a seed (CC-038). chess.js is already a dependency but lists
  moves at about 0.5 ms per position because it writes their notation, so a search over chess.js sees only a few
  hundred positions in 300 ms.
- **Options:** (1) Stockfish WASM: far stronger than needed, large binary, needs worker plumbing and skill-level
  tuning, nondeterministic with threads, a native-like dependency to keep current. (2) Search over chess.js:
  no new code for rules, but too slow for anything beyond Beginner. (3) A small board and search of our own,
  checked against chess.js.
- **Decision:** (3). About 800 lines with comments and tables (`board.js`, `search.js`), no new dependency. chess.js remains the referee:
  the chess engine (`chessEngine.js`) still decides every move, and tests prove the bot's board agrees with chess.js
  (published perft counts for six positions to depth 3 or 4, legal move lists and check status in 1,000+ random
  positions, fingerprints after every move in random games, reading every move of random game records).
- **Consequences:** fast enough for three levels with no dependency or binary. Strength is limited (no
  transposition table, simple evaluation). If players want more than "Strong", Stockfish can be added later
  behind the same `Bot` contract without touching rooms.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Never plays an illegal move: every move in the contract kit games and in 250+ random positions for Beginner and Club is
      accepted by `chessEngine.validateMove`.
- [x] Mate in 1 (scholar's mate, back-rank mate) and mate in 2 (Légal's mate in a real game, and two composed
      positions, one per colour) are found; the bot then finishes the mate.
- [x] Avoids stalemate when winning; takes a repetition or fifty-move draw when losing.
- [x] Respects its budget: depth and positions per level, deterministic (same seed, same moves and node counts).
- [x] Strong beats Beginner over N seeded games, Club beats Beginner (numbers below).
- [x] Contract kit (CC-038) passes for every level; tests cover every branch.

## Verification

Measured on this machine (Apple M4 Pro, Node 24.21), whole games through the real chess engine, seeds 1000+,
colours alternating:

| Pairing            | Games | Result                                    | Avg move time (ms)       | p95       | Max       |
| ------------------ | ----- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | --------- | --------- |
| Strong vs Beginner | 10    | Strong 10, by checkmate in 15 to 86 plies | Strong 137, Beginner 1.1 | 184 / 2.7 | 197 / 7.4 |
| Club vs Beginner   | 10    | Club 10, by checkmate in 15 to 62 plies   | Club 12, Beginner 1.3    | 27 / 3.6  | 44 / 12   |
| Strong vs Club     | 6     | Strong 3, Club 2, 1 threefold draw        | Strong 135, Club 9       | 178 / 21  | 211 / 46  |

An earlier run with 80,000 positions for Strong averaged 210 ms with a p95 of 409 ms, over the 300 ms target, so
the budget was set to 60,000. The unit tests repeat a smaller version (3 Strong vs Beginner games, 4 Club vs
Beginner games, all won by the stronger level). They do not time moves, because time depends on the machine and
the load. They check the position budget instead, which is what decides the time (`search.test.js`: a search
never looks at more than its budget plus a few positions).

Move generator (`board.test.js`): perft matches the published counts for the start position, Kiwipete,
positions 3, 4 (both colours) and 5.
