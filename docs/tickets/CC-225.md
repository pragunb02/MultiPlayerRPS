## Why

In Rock Paper Scissors both players choose at once. Against the computer a player must be able to trust that the
server did not let Rocky see their throw first. A lock shown before they choose, and checked by their own browser
after, makes that provable.

## Scope

- In: whenever a computer player must act at the same moment as a person (generic, today only Rock Paper Scissors),
  it chooses at once, in the same save that starts the round, so no round is ever visible without its lock
  (`botTurns.settle`, called by the room component for every change it saves). The choice is stored with the game as
  a seal: the move as JSON text, a salt, and the commitment, the lowercase hex SHA-256 of `text:salt`
  (`domain/bots/seals.js`).
- In: the salt is an HMAC-SHA256 of the turn (match id, log position, player) under a server secret derived from
  `SESSION_SECRET` with its own label, so deciding the same turn again gives the same seal, and one shown salt says
  nothing about the next. No new setting is needed.
- In: the room view shows `fairness.locked` (the commitments not shown yet) to everyone, and `fairness.checks`
  (commitment, text and salt) once the computer has played the move and may act again, or the game is over. A
  computer's move that is not its sealed move is refused with FORBIDDEN, also after the person chose.
- In: the board shows "Rocky has locked in a throw. Lock 7f3a…c21" before the person throws, then "Rocky threw Rock.
  Lock checked ✓", checked in the browser with Web Crypto (`lib/sealCheck.js`, `hooks/useSealChecks.js`). "How we
  keep it fair" (`rps.fairness`) opens a dialog with the explanation and the full check. A lock that does not match
  says so in red. A browser without `crypto.subtle` (a page served without HTTPS) says it could not check.
- Out: commit and reveal between two people (they choose through the server, which never shows a pending choice,
  SEC-09), and an in-app proof page outside the room.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] The lock is visible to the player before they choose.
- [x] The choice and salt are shown with the result, and the browser checks them.
- [x] Tests prove the computer cannot change its choice after seeing the player's.
- [x] "How we keep it fair" shows the check.

## Plan / notes

The pending choice is never in the person's view (the engine hides it, SEC-09), and the salt is not either until the
round is over: the browser test checks that no `salt` reached the browser before the first round ended.

## Verification

- Unit: `seals.test.js` (the hash, the same seal for the same turn, a new salt per turn, a different lock for a
  different move, the secret), `botTurns.test.js` (sealed in the save that starts the game, the computer's other
  move refused after the person chose, the reveal matching the earlier lock and the move actually played, hidden
  while only the computer has played, shown when the game ends), `sealCheck.test.js`, `fairness.test.jsx`.
- Sockets and integration: every reveal in a full game hashes to the lock shown before its round
  (`computer.test.js`, `bots.integration.test.js`).
- Browser: `e2e/computer.spec.js` sees the lock, "Lock checked ✓" and the dialog, desktop and mobile.
