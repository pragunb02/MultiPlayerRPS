## Research (written before any code)

Sources were read on 2026-10-20. Where a source could not be opened from here, the line says "general knowledge"
with a confidence level, as the rest of `docs/` does.

### 1. How our code does it today

1. **Profile.** `/profile` (`apps/web/src/pages/ProfilePage/ProfilePage.jsx`) is the player's own page only: level,
   played, won, lost, drawn, rank, streaks, quests, achievements, match history, blocked players and, for accounts,
   account and privacy settings. There is no page about anyone else. ADR-011 left "public player profiles
   (`/players/:username`)" as a separate decision that needs a privacy choice. The legacy app's
   `/u/:name/userprofile` only redirects to `/profile`.
2. **Avatars.** None. A seat in a room (`components/Seats/Seats.jsx`), a leaderboard row
   (`pages/LeaderboardPage/LeaderboardPage.jsx`) and match history show a name only. Computer characters are
   labelled with their level (ADR-025). The art pipeline (ADR-012, CC-230, `docs/art/README.md`) says art is SVG
   drawn in React components over the tokens, with a row in `docs/CREDITS.md` checked by `scripts/credits.test.mjs`.
3. **Who sees whom.** Room views name players by seat ids (ADR-057), history by match player ids (CC-297), the
   leaderboards by display name only, with "Hidden player" for every account not surely an adult and guests under
   their server-given `Guest-XXXX` (ADR-048.10, ADR-060). `accountComponent.adultsAmong` is the one age check.
   DATA.md's Identifiers table lists every id a client can receive. A player id is `guest_<uuid>` or
   `user_<ObjectId>`, whose first 4 bytes are the account's creation time.
4. **Blocks.** `blockComponent.blockedWith({ playerId })` answers everyone on either side of a block with a player,
   guests who signed in since included (ADR-053). Every refusal that names a person is the same 404.
5. **Data that could fill a profile.** Progress (`player_progress`: XP, level, played, wins, losses, draws), chess
   rating (`chess_ratings`, CC-042: `ratingsOf` gives rating, deviation, games, provisional), gamification (streaks,
   achievements), the account (`username`, `createdAt`, `birthYear`).
6. **Gaps.** No stable public address for a player, no avatar store, nothing for a guest's choice to move with on
   sign up, and the boards cannot link to anyone.

What the rule books say:

1. **GDPR Article 25(2)** (gdpr-info.eu, read 2026-10-20): "by default personal data are not made accessible
   without the individual's intervention to an indefinite number of natural persons." A profile open to everyone
   from the first day is exactly that, so it must be the player's own choice.
2. **ICO Age appropriate design code, standard 7 "Default settings"** (ico.org.uk, read 2026-10-20): "Settings must
   be 'high privacy' by default", and a child's data is visible to other users "only if the child amends their
   settings". For us a minor can never amend it (ADR-028, ADR-048.10), which is stricter still.
3. **India's DPDP Act 2023, section 9**: no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at
   children, and verifiable parental consent (general knowledge, high; ADR-028 already cites it).
4. **OWASP IDOR Prevention Cheat Sheet** (read 2026-10-20): "replace enumerable numeric identifiers with more
   complex, random identifiers", keep the access check anyway, and "if the resource's existence is sensitive ... map
   both cases to the same public response", for example a 404.
5. **Discord's snowflakes** (docs.discord.com, read 2026-10-20) are "just numbers with a timestamp": the top 42 bits
   are the creation time in milliseconds. Our account ids carry their creation time in the same way, which is why
   no profile address may be, or be worked out from, a player id.

What we take from them:

1. A username in the URL (Lichess, Chess.com) links a person across the web and changes when CC-185 renames them.
   A numeric id (Steam, Roblox, Discord) can be counted through and gives the creation time. A random handle stored
   for the purpose does neither.
2. The three state answer (Steam): a private profile shows the name and the picture only. The name is already on
   the public boards for an adult (ADR-048.10), so nothing new is given away.
3. Children's services use a curated avatar set and no uploads (Xbox, Roblox). Uploads need moderation we do not
   have (ADR-051.2), so the set is ours alone. Steam and Roblox sell frames and catalogue avatars, which is what
   coin cosmetics (ADR-026, CC-143) will add later: each avatar needs an unlock rule from the start.
4. No product shows a last seen time or a daily streak on a public page to strangers by default. A precise join date
   or a daily streak shows when a person plays, a routine. We show neither.

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **Address.** `/players/:handle`, with a handle of 16 random characters (80 bits, lower case Crockford base32,
   no `i l o u`), stored in a new `player_profiles` document and made the first time it is needed. Not a keyed id
   (ADR-057): a keyed id would change when `SESSION_SECRET` is rotated and would be "derivable" by the server from
   the player id, which the brief forbids. Not the username (renames, CC-185). Never the room code or the match id.
2. **Who gets a link.** A handle is sent only where a person already appears by name and only for an adult account:
   a leaderboard row whose name is shown and is an adult's, and a past opponent in the viewer's own history who is an
   adult account. A hidden row, a guest row, a minor and a computer character get no link, so a handle can never
   join the anonymous rows of a minor across boards. The player's own handle is on their own profile ("Your public
   link"), so a minor can share theirs, and whoever opens it sees "Hidden player".
3. **What the page shows, in order of the checks, all decided on the server for each read:**
   1. Unknown handle, a deleted account, or a block either way with the viewer: **404**, one body for all of them,
      the same body as a malformed handle (OWASP, ADR-053).
   2. Not surely an adult (guest, minor, no birth year): `{ hidden: true }`, "Hidden player", no picture, no stats
      (ADR-048.10, ADR-028.1). The page cannot tell a minor from a guest or an unknown age.
   3. An adult whose profile is private: name and avatar, "This profile is private".
   4. An adult whose profile is public: name, avatar, level, chess rating (CC-042, "1500?" while provisional), games
      played and won. No join date, no streaks, no last seen, no history, no email, no id.
4. **Private by default.** Every profile starts private (GDPR 25(2), ICO standard 7). An adult account can switch
   it to public on their own profile, and back. Guests and accounts not surely adult see the switch locked, with
   why. The default is one constant, so the owner can change it (owner decision 1).
5. **Avatars.** Twelve original avatars drawn in code in the neon arcade style (ADR-012), each on a coloured tile
   from the tokens. The catalogue (`AVATARS` in `@classicade/shared`) gives each avatar an unlock rule; today every
   rule is `free`. CC-143 adds a rule kind (coins, a level, a story character) and its check on the server, without
   touching the picker, the room or the boards. A player who never picked one is shown a neutral silhouette.
6. **Where avatars show.** Beside the name in a room's seats (which stay on screen with the result), on leaderboard
   rows that show a name, on the public profile and on your own profile. A hidden row shows the silhouette, so a
   minor's choice is never on a public page. In a room everyone sees everyone's avatar, as they see the names.
7. **Guests pick too, and it moves on sign up** (ADR-014): the account keeps its own choice and takes the guest's
   when it has none. The guest's profile document is deleted after the move, so its handle stops working.
8. **Abuse.** Lookups are rate limited per network address (30 a minute) and per signed-in player (30 a minute).
   80 random bits cannot be counted through at that rate. There is no directory, no search and no list route.
   Avatar and visibility changes share a limit of 30 in 10 minutes per player.
9. **Deletion and export** (ADR-048): `player_profiles` is a participant. Export lists the handle, the avatar and
   the visibility. Deletion removes the document, so the handle is a 404 afterwards.

## Why

The first prerequisite for friends and invites (CC-052, ADR-028.8): a player must be able to look at someone they
met, safely, before they can add them. And players get something of their own to show beside their name.

## Scope

- In: the avatar catalogue and art, the picker on your own profile, avatars in rooms, on boards and profiles, the
  public profile page and API, the privacy switch, the guest move, deletion and export, DATA.md and the privacy page.
- Out: friends and follow (CC-052, CC-206), profile comments, uploads, frames and coin unlocks (CC-143, CC-153f),
  the wider privacy settings page (CC-278), a rating graph (ADR-027.4).

## Plan / notes

1. Owner decisions are listed at the end of ADR-065: private by default or public for adults, the silhouette on a
   hidden profile, the Hinglish drafts, and whether a room seat should link to a profile.
2. Kept on purpose: no join date, no streaks, no last seen and no history on a public profile (a routine is a
   fingerprint), and no handle for anyone not surely an adult, so nothing joins up a minor's anonymous rows.
3. A room seat keeps the avatar the person had when they sat down. Changing it shows in the next room.
4. The results screen shows the seats with their avatars beside the result. The result card itself names nobody.
