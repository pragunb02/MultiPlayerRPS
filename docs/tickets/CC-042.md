## Research (written before any code)

### 1. How our code does it today

1. There is no rating anywhere. ADR-027 decides the basics: chess only, Glicko-2, provisional with a "?" for the
   first 10 games, seeded by the one-tap level pick (CC-221), never reset, and a separate season rank later
   (CC-055). CC-221 left the seeding to this ticket.
2. Every finished game goes through the match outbox (`domain/matches/matchOutboxComponent.js`, ADR-047). The steps
   are listed in `matchPipelineSteps` in `server.js`: record match, judge, apply progression, apply gamification,
   apply leaderboards. Each step reads the stored match and must change nothing when it runs again.
3. Fair play (`domain/matches/fairPlay.js`, ADR-021) stores a verdict per player on the match. `creditFor(match,
playerId)` answers whether it counted. Computer characters have ids `bot_<character>_<level>`
   (`models/botIdentity.js`), and a deleted player's stand-in has `deleted_<uuid>` (`models/deletedPlayer.js`).
   `earnsNothing` covers both.
4. ADR-025 point 2: games against computer characters never count toward a rating or any leaderboard. Nothing in
   ADR-025 or its amendment makes an exception for chess.
5. Progress (`progressDao.js`, `progressAccessor.js`) is the pattern for a per-player store that is exactly once
   and follows a guest: a list of the last 200 match ids, a `mergedInto` marker on the guest's document, and a
   `mergedFrom` list on the account's (ADR-014). Deletion and export go through `playerDataComponent` participants
   (ADR-048).
6. The client already knows the match id of the room's game (`room.game.matchId`, CC-235). The chess board
   (`apps/web/src/games/chess/ChessBoard.jsx`) shows one `PlayerBar` per side and the `GameOver` panel when the
   room is finished. CC-163 is reworking the outcome screen (`components/Celebrations`) in parallel.
7. No chess game is "casual" today: there is no rated or casual option in the lobby. So every chess game between
   two people that counts under ADR-021 is a rated game.

### 2. How others do it (sources read 2026-10-20)

1. **Glickman, "Example of the Glicko-2 system"** ([glicko2.pdf](http://www.glicko.net/glicko/glicko2.pdf),
   revised 22 March 2022). The whole algorithm in eight steps, with the Illinois method for the new volatility
   (revised 2012, stable) and a tolerance of 0.000001. Unrated players start at 1500, RD 350, volatility 0.06.
   τ "between 0.3 and 1.2". A player who does not play in a period only gets step 6: the RD grows by the volatility.
   The worked example: a 1500 player with RD 200 and volatility 0.06, τ 0.5, beats a 1400 (RD 30) and loses to a
   1550 (RD 100) and a 1700 (RD 300), and ends at 1464.06, RD 151.52, volatility 0.05999. Our unit test pins these
   numbers and the intermediate ones (v 1.7785, Δ −0.4834).
2. **Lichess FAQ** ([lichess.org/faq](https://lichess.org/faq)): Glicko-2, a new player starts at "1500 ± 1000", the
   question mark "means the rating is provisional", concretely "the Glicko-2 deviation is greater than 110", ratings
   are never reset and the interval grows with time, so "around a year of inactivity" makes a rating provisional
   again.
3. **Lichess source** ([lila `Glicko.scala`](https://github.com/lichess-org/lila/blob/master/modules/rating/src/main/Glicko.scala)):
   default 1500, deviation 500 (also the maximum), minimum deviation 45, volatility 0.09, and
   `RatingPeriodsPerDay(0.21436)`, "chosen so a typical player's RD goes from 60 to 110 in 1 year". Lichess rates
   every game on its own as soon as it ends (one game is one rating period) and lets the deviation grow with the
   time since the last game.
4. **Chess.com** ([How do ratings work](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8566476-how-do-ratings-work-on-chess-com),
   [Chess ratings](https://www.chess.com/terms/chess-ratings)): Glicko, "your rating will fluctuate significantly
   during your first few games", and "if you haven't played in a while, your RD will increase". New members pick a
   skill level when they join and start from a rating that matches it (general knowledge from signing up, medium
   confidence: the pages read do not list the numbers).

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. **A pure Glicko-2 module, one game per rating period**, as Lichess does. Our players play a few games now and
   then, so waiting for a rating period of 10 to 15 games (Glickman's advice) would mean a rating that never moves.
   Glickman's own defaults (1500, RD 350, volatility 0.06, τ 0.5), because they are the published ones and our test
   checks them. The deviation grows with the time since the player's last rated game at Lichess's pace, never above 350.
2. **Provisional means fewer than 10 rated games**, as ADR-027 says, not a deviation threshold. It is simpler to
   explain ("your first 10 games") and the ADR decided it.
3. **Exactly once through the outbox.** A new step, "apply chess ratings", after judge. Glicko-2 is not a sum, so
   "add once" (progress) is not enough: both players' new ratings depend on both old ratings. Each player's rating
   document keeps the last 200 changes as `{ matchId, before, after }`. A retry after a crash between the two
   players' writes finds the first player's `before` there and rates the second against the same rating. Every
   write is guarded by a revision number, so two games of one player that end at the same moment cannot overwrite
   each other: the one that loses the race fails and the outbox runs it again (ADR-047).
4. **Only games that count for both players.** A rating game is between two people, both ADR-021 verdicts must say
   counted, and computer characters and deleted stand-ins never take part (ADR-025 point 2, ADR-048). The hourly cap
   can hold one side back. Rating the other side alone would move one rating and not the other.
5. **Seeding from the level pick.** A counted game against Queen Vee sets the starting rating of a player with no
   rated game yet: Beginner 1200, Club player 1500, Strong 1800, deviation 350. The game result itself never moves
   the rating (ADR-025 point 2). It is only the starting point, like Chess.com's level question.
6. **Guests are rated and the rating moves on sign up** (ADR-014). Glicko-2 ratings cannot be added, so the account
   keeps whichever of the two ratings has more rated games, and the account's own when they are equal. The guest's
   document stays as a marker, so a game that finishes during the sign in is rated on the account.
7. **Deletion and export** take part like progress: erase the player's document and the markers that point at it,
   export the rating, games and the list of changes. No other player's id is stored in a rating document, so
   nothing else needs to be anonymised.
8. **Display, small and in slots.** The match bar shows "1500?" (the question mark is Lichess's and ADR-027's
   wording) or "1532" next to each name. The result panel shows the change, "+12" or "−8", or "Provisional" for
   the first 10 games. The rating comes from a socket request scoped to a room the socket is in, and a push to the
   room when the step has run. CC-163 is building the outcome screen, so both pieces are slots it can move.

## Why

FR-PROG-2: a fair skill number for chess, the one game where skill decides (ADR-027). Quick match's rating window
(CC-051) and the season rank (CC-055) need it first.

## Scope

1. In: the pure Glicko-2 module with Glickman's worked example as its test, the rating rules, the `chess_ratings`
   collection, the "apply chess ratings" outbox step, seeding from a game against Queen Vee, the guest move,
   deletion and export, the room ratings request and push, the rating in the chess match bar and in the result
   panel.
2. Out: leaderboards (CC-291 is changing them), the profile rating graph (ADR-027 point 4, a follow up once the
   profile is redesigned), the season rank (CC-055), a rated or casual choice in the lobby (feature freeze, written
   in IDEAS.md only if the owner wants it), the rating window in quick match (CC-051), the outcome screen (CC-163).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The Glicko-2 module reproduces Glickman's worked example (rating, deviation, volatility and the intermediate
      values).
- [ ] A counted chess game between two people changes both ratings once, also when the step runs again, after a
      crash between the two writes, and when two games of one player end together.
- [ ] Games that do not count for both players, games against computer characters and games with a deleted
      stand-in change no rating. A game against Queen Vee seeds an unrated player's start from the level picked.
- [ ] A guest's rating moves to the account on sign up, and a game finishing during the sign in is rated on the
      account.
- [ ] Deleting an account erases its rating, export includes it.
- [ ] The chess match bar shows each person's rating, with "?" for the first 10 games. The result panel shows the
      change, or "Provisional".
- [ ] Tests cover the change. `npm run lint`, `npm run format:check`, `npm test`, coverage and the chess browser
      specs pass.
- [ ] Docs: ADR-062, DATA.md, ARCHITECTURE.md where it lists the pipeline steps.

## Plan / notes

The design is written down as [ADR-062](../DECISIONS.md) and summarised here.

### Layers

| Layer       | File                                                       | Does                                                                                   |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| shared      | `packages/shared/src/ratings.js`                           | `CHESS_RATING` (provisional games, starting values) for the server and the web app     |
| domain      | `apps/server/src/domain/ratings/glicko2.js`                | the pure algorithm: `rate`, `aged`, the scale conversion                               |
| domain      | `apps/server/src/domain/ratings/chessRatingRules.js`       | pure rules: which games are rated, the score, the seed for a level, the view           |
| domain      | `apps/server/src/domain/ratings/chessRatingComponent.js`   | applies a match once, seeds, moves a guest, erases, exports, answers a room's ratings  |
| data        | `apps/server/src/data/chessRatingAccessor.js`              | documents to `ChessRating` models, follows a guest's marker, stale writes to conflicts |
| persistence | `apps/server/src/persistence/chessRatingDao.js`            | `chess_ratings`, one document per player, writes guarded by `revision`                 |
| service     | `apps/server/src/service/realtime/ratingHandlers.js`       | `rating:room` for a socket in that room, `rating:updated` pushed to the room           |
| web         | `apps/web/src/games/chess/useRoomRatings.js`, `Rating.jsx` | fetch and listen, the badge in the bar and the change in the result panel              |

### The step, in order

1. Not chess: nothing. Against Queen Vee: seed the person if they have no rated game, then nothing more.
2. Not two people both counted, or two seats that are one person after a sign in: nothing.
3. Read both owners' ratings (a guest's marker leads to the account).
4. For each player, the rating before this game is the `before` of the change already stored for this match, or the
   current rating aged by the time since their last rated game.
5. Rate each player not yet changed by this match against the other's rating before, and write it guarded by the
   revision read in 3. A write that lost a race is a conflict: the step fails and the outbox runs it again.
6. When anything changed now, publish `ratings.updated` with both players' views. The gateway pushes it to the room.
