Decisions on this ticket were made by the design lead, owner-delegated 2026-10-21, and are recorded in ADR-079
("Accepted by default, owner may change"). The owner's brief of 2026-10-21 replaced the 2026-10-18 plan of a
separate "Find a person" button with one button and a parallel search (Research §4).

## Problem

Riya opens Rock Paper Scissors on her phone at 9 pm and presses "Play Rocky". Arjun does the same a second later
in another city. Both play a computer, because nothing on ClassiCade can ever put two strangers together: rooms
are joined by code or link only. The ADR-029 gate that would one day make "Find a person" the main button has
nothing to measure, so it can never open.

## Job to be done

When I press Play, I want to play a real person if one is free right now, and the character at once if not, so I
can play without waiting and still meet people when the site is busy.

## Appetite

M (up to 6 agent-hours). **Cut line:** first the adaptive timeout (keep a fixed 4 s), then the chess rating window
(chess first come first served), then the "Nobody was free" toast.

## Proposed solution

1. **One button.** Behind the `quick_match` flag, "Play <character>" sends `match:quick { gameId, level }`. The panel
   says before the press that it looks for a person first, for up to 4 seconds, and that the character plays if
   nobody is free. While it looks, the button says "Looking for a person…", a status line says "Still looking. If
   nobody is free, you play <character>, a computer player" and Cancel shows.
2. **In parallel.** The server makes the player's waiting room at once (marked `quickMatch: true`) and puts the
   search in its pool. A person found joins the older search's room, which starts the game, and the newer room
   closes. Nobody found: the character sits in the room already made when the search time ends, so the fallback
   adds no wait beyond the search. The answer to `match:quick` is the one outcome: `person`, `character` or
   `cancelled`, with the started room's view.
3. **Liquidity gate (ADR-029).** No search on an empty site: if no other player is connected and nobody it could
   meet is searching, the character starts at once. The search lasts 4 s until 20 searches found a person, then the
   80th percentile of the last 100 such waits, from 1 s to 4 s.
4. **Pools.** One in-memory pool per game, and per 300-point rating band for chess (CC-042 ratings), first come first
   served. Chess pairs within 100 points, widening 50 a second to 300. Quick match chess is 3+2 (ADR-051.6).
5. **Never paired:** the same person, a blocked pair either way, a pair that failed already in this search, a pair
   paired 10 times in 24 hours.
6. **Cancel** on Cancel, on leaving or hiding the page, on the tab disconnecting.
7. **Counts** per India day, hour, game and outcome, waits in buckets, logged once a minute, no ids.

## Non-goals

- Making "Find a person" a button of its own, or the main one per hour slot (CC-228 with the gate).
- Staying in the queue while playing the character, and "ping me when a person is free" (CC-226).
- A queue shared by several servers (CC-061).
- Marking stored matches with how the players met (a match without the mark already counts as quick match for a
  pair with a minor, ADR-066.5).
- Arenas (CC-210), rating ranges the player sets, a choice of chess time control.

## Rabbit holes

- **Races.** Two searches pairing with one waiting player, a cancel landing mid-pairing, the timer firing
  mid-pairing. Avoided by one state machine whose changes all happen in synchronous code (Research §5.4).
- **Waves.** Lichess pairs in waves of 12 to 60 s; our pools hold a few people, so we pair on arrival and sweep
  only chess, every 250 ms, as windows widen.
- **A prepared room per search** counts against the 3 waiting rooms per player and the network cap. It lives at
  most 4 s, so it does; no new limits.

## Threat model

We are building a way for two strangers to be seated together. What can go wrong: a player reaches someone who
blocked them, a stranger follows a child from the game (friend request, challenge, profile), a client names who it
wants to meet or learns another's player id, one player floods the queue, a pair farms rewards. What we do: blocks
read at the start and again by the room as the person sits down; the room is marked quick match so friend requests
and challenges again refuse a pair with a minor; no chat and no emotes for minors; the client sends only a game and
a level; views name players by seat ids (crawled); one search per player, the socket event budget and the room
limits apply to the prepared room; the pair cap. Good enough for a flag-off launch; the security review checks it.

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** the opponent's chosen, filtered username and avatar, as any room shows. In memory: one search per player
  (player id, socket id, game, level, rating, the ids on either side of a block) and quick match pairings per pair
  for 24 hours. One new optional room field, `quickMatch: true`.
- **Retention:** a search at most 4 s; pairings 24 hours; both forgotten on restart. The room field lives with the
  room (30 minutes to 24 hours).
- **Who sees it:** the two players see each other's name and seat id. Nobody sees a player id. The owner reads the
  counts in the logs, which hold no id.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** exactly what an adult gets in the game, and nothing after it: no friend request, no
  challenge again, no profile link, no emotes, no chat. A policy switch (`quickMatch: none`) keeps any audience out.
- **Defaults:** the flag is off. The owner ramps it from the console.

## Alternatives considered

- **A separate "Find a person" button** (the 2026-10-18 plan): honest, but asks visitors to choose before the crowd
  exists, and most would never press it. The brief chose one button.
- **Bots that look like people in thin queues:** forbidden by ADR-025.1 and the CCPA dark pattern rules.
- **Waves like Lichess:** need dozens per pool. Ours pair on arrival.
- **Pools split by age:** an adult can play as a guest, so it keeps nobody apart, and it halves a tiny crowd.
- **A MongoDB queue now:** survives restarts and works across servers, but a search lasts 4 s and we run one server
  (ADR-005); CC-061 brings a shared one.
- **Create the character's room only after the search:** simpler, but adds the room's round trips to every
  fallback, which the brief rules out.

## Research (written before any code)

Sources were read on 2026-10-18 unless another date is given. "General knowledge" carries a confidence level.

### 1. How our code does it today

1. **The main button is already a character** (CC-221, done). `apps/web/src/pages/PlayPage/PlayPage.jsx` leads with
   `components/PlayComputer` ("Play Rocky", "Play Pip", "Play Queen Vee"), with a Computer badge, a one-tap level
   and the note on how computer games count. It sends `room:play-computer` and `botHandlers.js` seats the character
   at once (about 0.5 s to the first move, CC-221 notes). Below it: "Play a friend" (create a room, `lobby.create`)
   and join by code (`lobby.join`). The characters are in `packages/shared/src/bots.js`.
2. **There is no way to meet a stranger.** Rooms are private: only someone with the code or link can join
   (`roomComponent.joinRoom`). There is no pool, no queue and no public room list. The result screen's
   "New opponent" (`room.new_opponent`) only leaves the room.
3. **No rating yet.** ADR-027 decides Glicko-2 for chess only, but CC-042 is not built, so there is nothing to put a
   rating window on.
4. **No gate yet.** ADR-029 says "Find a person" may become the main button for an hour slot only when 60% of
   searches in that slot find a person within 4 s, and the timeout is the 80th percentile wait, at most 4 s. The flag
   table (CC-228), the nightly job (CC-176) and the telemetry (CC-173 to CC-175) are not built, and EVENTS.md has no
   event for a search.
5. **What a start needs.** Games start only with everyone connected (CC-112, merged in #78). The abuse limits of
   ADR-035 count waiting rooms per player and per network, and ADR-021 caps counted games per pair per day.
6. **No block list** exists (CC-044), so today nothing could keep two players apart.

### 2. How others do it

**Lichess** (source code of lila, https://github.com/lichess-org/lila, `modules/pool`):

1. One pool per time control, 11 of them (`PoolList.scala`), each paired in **waves**: 1+0 every 12 s or at 40
   players, 3+2 every 22 s or at 30, 10+0 every 13 s or at 30, 30+20 every 60 s or at 20.
2. Pairing (`MatchMaking.scala`) is a weighted matching on rating difference. The largest gap allowed depends on the
   lower rating: 130 under 1000, 100 under 1500, then rating / 15 (about 133 at 2000). Each missed wave lowers the
   score by 12, so the window widens the longer someone waits. A player's own rating range is respected.
3. **Blocked pairs are never paired** in the normal path (`blockList(a, b) || blockList(b, a)` is a conflict).
   Players marked for cheating or boosting are paired only with each other.
4. Bots are a separate "Play with the computer" button. Lichess's own engine never sits in a human pool (general
   knowledge, high confidence).

**Chess.com:** "Play" starts a search in the chosen time control, inside a rating range the player can set, which
widens as the wait grows (general knowledge, medium confidence. The support page for this did not resolve). Its bots
are named characters with avatars (Martin, Nelson and others) in a separate "Play Bots" section, always labelled
(general knowledge, high confidence).

**Papergames** (https://papergames.io/en/tic-tac-toe): three separate choices, "Play online with a random player",
"Play with a friend" (a link to send) and "Play vs robot" to "face our bot at any hour". The page says nothing about
how long a search takes or whether bots fill it.

**Bots in empty queues.** Some mobile games fill thin queues with bots that look like people, especially for new
players (general knowledge, medium confidence. No source fetched). That is exactly what ADR-025.1 forbids: every bot
is labelled before and during the match. India's CCPA 2023 dark pattern guidelines list "bait and switch" (cited in
PRODUCT_VISION.md §6.9, "Bots in quick match ... always labelled before the match starts").

**Liquidity.** Lichess's waves assume dozens of players in a pool every few seconds. We expect a handful of people
online at a time for months (GROWTH.md §1.3, §3.5). ADR-029 is our answer: crowd features stay behind numeric
gates, and the honest fallback (a character) is shown until the crowd is there.

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade (2026-10-18, before the brief)

1. **Keep "Play <character>" as the main button** for every hour slot until its ADR-029 gate opens. It is what
   CC-221 built, and it is honest at any hour.
2. **Add "Find a person" as the second button**, above "Play a friend". It searches for at most 4 s (the ADR-029
   default, later the gate's measured 80th percentile). The search runs **in parallel with the page**: the character
   button stays usable, and the screen says plainly "Looking for a person… (up to 4 seconds)".
3. **When nobody is found**, say so: "Nobody is free right now." with two buttons, "Play Rocky (computer)" and "Try
   again". The search never seats a computer by itself, and a computer is never shown as a person, so no player is
   fooled (ADR-025.1). Keeping a search running while the player plays the character is a later step, not this
   ticket: it needs a way to offer the match between games and a rule for what happens to the computer game.
4. **One pool per game in server memory.** We run one instance (ADR-005), and a search lasts 4 s, so a restart losing
   the pool costs at most one retry. No new collection. Pair **at once** when two compatible players are waiting
   (no waves, the pool is too small). Rock Paper Scissors and Tic-Tac-Toe use their default options (best of 3).
   Chess uses one pool per quick time control, 3+2 and 10+0 (PRODUCT_VISION.md §6.1).
5. **Chess rating window, when CC-042 exists.** Start at ±150 (between Lichess's 100 and 130 at low ratings and its
   rating / 15 higher up) and widen to ±400 by the end of the 4 s, then to anyone after a "Try again". Provisional
   players ("?") get the wide window from the start. Until CC-042 ships, chess pairs first come first served, and
   the window is a pure function with tests waiting for the rating.
6. **Never pair** a player with themself (another tab), with someone either of them blocked (CC-044, both ways as
   Lichess does), or with someone they already played 10 counted games with today (ADR-021, so a pair cannot farm).
7. **Minors and strangers.** Quick match is the first feature that puts a player with a stranger. Players never talk
   (no chat, emotes off for minors and guests, ADR-028.6), and names already pass the filter (ADR-022). Lichess and
   Chess.com allow children in their pools. **Block and report (CC-044) must ship before "Find a person" goes live**
   (ADR-028.8 asks for them before contact features), so a player can stop meeting someone and report a name.
8. **Measurement for the gate,** without tracking (ADR-034): the server counts, per India hour slot and game,
   searches, searches matched within 4 s and the wait of matched searches in buckets. No player id. This needs a new
   server event in EVENTS.md (`quick_match_searched` with `game_id`, `outcome` `matched | timed_out | cancelled`, `wait_ms`
   rounded) that the owner accepts before it is sent. Until CC-174 exists, the counts can be a log line once a
   minute like the room gauge (ADR-035.6), with no ids.

### 4. Addendum, 2026-10-21: the code today and the brief

1. **What changed since 2026-10-18.** Blocks exist (CC-044, `blockComponent.blockedWith`, and `joinRoom` refuses a
   blocked pair as "full"). Chess ratings exist (CC-042, `chessRatingComponent.ratingsOf`, 1500 to start). The age
   policy exists (CC-152, `safety.policyFor`), and friends refuse a quick match stranger for a pair with a minor
   (`friendRules.mayReach`, reading `room.quickMatch` through `coPlayers`). Flags exist (CC-312, `flags.isOn`,
   `useFlag`, `flagPaths`). Room views name players by seat ids (CC-253, CC-297). Computer players think on worker
   threads (CC-308, ADR-067), so a fallback game costs the event loop no more than today's "Play Rocky".
2. **The brief.** One button, a parallel search of at most 4 s, the character with no extra wait when nobody is
   free, a liquidity gate on the people online, a fair order, cancel on leave or hide, exactly one outcome, the
   flag, anonymous counts with a friction guardrail.
3. **Sources read today.**
   1. Lichess pools, `modules/pool/src/main/PoolList.scala` (https://github.com/lichess-org/lila, read
      2026-10-21): "PoolConfig(3 ++ 2, Wave(22.seconds, 30.players))", "PoolConfig(1 ++ 0, Wave(12.seconds,
      40.players))", "PoolConfig(10 ++ 0, Wave(13.seconds, 30.players))". A pool pairs at most every 12 to 60 s, or
      sooner when 20 to 40 players wait: their time to match is seconds to tens of seconds with a crowd.
   2. Lichess pairing, `modules/pool/src/main/MatchMaking.scala` (read 2026-10-21): the largest score allowed is
      "if rating < IntRating(1000) then 130", "else if rating < IntRating(1500) then 100", "else rating.value / 15";
      each missed wave adds "(p.misses * 12)" to the bonus, so the window widens with the wait; a pair is a conflict
      when "blockList(a, b) || blockList(b, a)"; "cheatMembers" and "boostMembers" are paired among themselves.
   3. Chess.com's help page on matchmaking ranges did not resolve again today (the URL answered a page on another
      subject). General knowledge, medium confidence: a search in one time control within a range that widens.
   4. Wikipedia, "Matchmaking (video games)" (read 2026-10-21): "Many matchmaking systems feature a ranking system
      that attempts to match players of roughly equal ability together", and playlists "connect them to an existing
      session or create a new one". It says nothing on minimum population or bots.
   5. Fallbacks in casual games (general knowledge, medium confidence; no source fetched): mobile party and battle
      games fill thin lobbies with bots, often unlabelled, for new players. ADR-025.1 forbids the unlabelled part.
      Papergames (2026-10-18) keeps "Play vs robot" as its own choice.
4. **Minimum population and time to match.** With searches arriving at random at a rate of λ a second, a search of
   T seconds finds someone with probability about 1 − e^(−λT). For 60 % within 4 s (the ADR-029 gate), λ must be
   about 0.23 a second, about 14 presses of Play a minute for one game. We expect a handful of people online for
   months (GROWTH.md), so most searches will run out, which is why the fallback must be the character at once, why
   an empty site skips the search, and why the timeout follows the measured 80th percentile once 20 searches found
   someone. The counts measure λ per hour slot, so the gate can be tuned with data (ADR-029.5).

### 5. What is best for ClassiCade (2026-10-21, supersedes §3 where they differ)

1. **One button, said plainly before the press.** The brief keeps "Play <character>" as the main button, so the
   panel tells the player it looks for a person first and the character plays otherwise. A computer is never seated
   without a press of a button that names it (ADR-025.1), and a person is never a disguised bot.
2. **The character's room is made at the start of the search**, so the fallback is one seat away when time runs out.
   A person found joins the older search's room, the same room component join every player uses, so every room
   limit and the block check stay in one place.
3. **A small pool pairs on arrival**, first come first served. Chess bands of 300 and a window of 100 widening to 300
   follow Lichess's numbers at our ratings (1500 start). One chess pool at 3+2 (ADR-051.6).
4. **Races.** Every change of a search's state happens in synchronous code, so taking two searches out of the pool
   and marking them paired is one step nobody can interleave. A cancel during the pairing is kept and decided
   before the room is joined; after the join it is too late. The room's own join checks blocks again. Each search
   ends once, in its own answer.
5. **Minors.** Lichess and Chess.com let children into their pools. We do too, because players cannot talk, and we
   make sure nothing follows from the game: the room mark makes friend requests and challenges again refuse a pair
   with a minor (ADR-051.7, .8), emotes are off, names are filtered and seat ids hide player ids. The age policy
   gets a `quickMatch` field so a stricter rule for any audience is one line.
6. **Counts without tracking.** Per India day, hour, game and outcome, with waits in buckets: enough for the gate
   (matched within 4 s per hour slot), the time to a match, and the guardrail (cancels, and the waits that ran out).
