## Problem

Three reports from CI's browser suites, each with its own root cause.

1. **`match-shell.spec.js` › "chess: every state's main controls are on screen without scrolling" › my-turn,
   their-turn, computer my-turn** (desktop, mobile, Firefox) showed ✘ on every run. Root cause: no failure at all.
   These are the six states on `BELOW_THE_FOLD`, marked `test.fail` until CC-172 fixes the chess layout (A8).
   Playwright's list reporter prints ✘ for an expected failure too, and counts it as passed. CI's run on a4a90fd
   ended `success` on its first attempt in every job, and CI has no retries (`retries: 0`), so nothing passed "on
   retry". Firefox shows the same three because `formFactor` counts it as a laptop. Each run fails the same way
   ("below the fold": on 1280×720 the timer at 782-812 px and the bottom rank at 697-758 px; on 390×844 Offer draw
   and Resign at 931-1001 px), after `offScreen` has waited for every control to be on the page. So the board's
   lazy chunk (CC-322) is there when the page is measured, the numbers are the same on every run, and nothing moves
   afterwards.
2. **`user.spec.js` › "signs out the other devices from one browser, which stays signed in"** (mobile): "element(s)
   not found" for the status "Your other devices are signed out." Root cause: a real app race, on `main` too, not
   from CC-262. The test presses the button as soon as the profile page shows it, while the page's dozen requests
   and its realtime connection are still on their way with the old session id. `keepOnlyThisSession` gives the
   session a new id and destroys the old one (`regenerate`). Two things then reach the server with the old id
   after it is gone:
   - **"Who am I?"** The browser's socket was mid-handshake, so the gateway ended it as a signed out session
     (`io.on("connection")`, the CC-116 handshake check: its id is not the kept one). `SessionProvider` asks the
     server again at once, before the 204 with the new cookie has arrived, so it asks with the old id and gets
     `{"authenticated":false}`. The page shows the visitor profile and unmounts `AccountSettings` with its status.
     Trace of the reproduction: socket 101 at 47.010, `POST /api/auth/sign-out-others` at 47.077 (answered 47.160),
     `GET /api/session` with the old cookie at 47.155, answered `{"authenticated":false}`.
   - **Any other read**, such as `GET /api/progress`, that the server read from the store after the destroy: 401
     "No active session" ("Couldn't load progress" on the page; the fixtures fail the test for it). The CC-116 grace
     window only helps a request whose session was read before the destroy, and CC-326 only keeps such answers
     from setting the old cookie again.

   CC-262 (devices list, not merged yet) does not cause it. It adds one more request to the profile page's burst
   (`GET /api/auth/devices`), which can only make the race more likely; with this fix that request waits like
   every other one.

3. **`match-shell.spec.js` › "outcome moments › a win because the opponent left is quiet"** (Firefox): "Target page,
   context or browser has been closed" after 31 s. Root cause, from CI's trace (artifact `ff179`): a real app layout
   shift from CC-322's lazy boards. The error is the 30 s test timeout: the click on `room.leave.confirm` waited
   27 s for a dialog that never opened. The press on Leave room before it "succeeded" (191 ms), but it landed on
   the board instead. Alice's screen at the press: the room had just started, the stage said "Loading the board…",
   and Leave room sat right under it (y ≈ 161 of 450 in the screencast). The board's chunk arrived while Firefox was
   delivering the press. The board pushed Leave room below itself (the snapshot after the press has the nine
   disabled cells where the button was), so Leave room got the mousedown (it ends up focused, `[active]` in the
   page snapshot) but not the click, and the dialog never opened. Locally the chunk is there long before the press,
   which is why it did not reproduce: 10 of 10 alone, 40 of 40 with 6 workers, 156 of 156 for the whole file with 8,
   and 153 of 153 for Firefox part 4 with 11 of the laptop's 14 cores kept busy.

## Fix

1. Nothing in the app or the test: a comment at `BELOW_THE_FOLD` says why these six show ✘ and are not flaky.
2. `createApiClient` (`apps/web/src/lib/api.js`, "the only place the web app calls fetch") sends a renewal of the
   session id (`changePassword`, `signOutOtherDevices`) only once every request already on its way is answered,
   and holds every request made meanwhile until the renewal is answered. So the server never reads a request of
   this page with the old id after the destroy, and "who am I?" asks with the new id. A refused renewal (wrong
   password) lets the held requests go the same way. Review of the first version: a request that is never
   answered held the renewal, and every request after it, for ever. So the renewal waits at most 3 s
   (`RENEWAL_WAIT_MS`) for the requests on their way, then goes anyway. The renewal itself is given up after 90 s
   (`RENEWAL_TIMEOUT_MS`), answer included, as a network failure, and a request held behind a renewal waits 90 s at
   most. The data export, the one big answer, is given up after 120 s (`EXPORT_TIMEOUT_MS`). Other requests get no
   limit of their own. Production runs on Render's free plan, which sleeps, and a wake-up takes 30 to 60 s
   (ADR-005). The waking up screen (`WakingUp`, CC-232) shows for as long as the first "who am I?" waits
   (`session.status === "loading"`) and says "the page carries on by itself". A shorter cut-off would end that wait
   with an error, and 90 s stays above the longest wake-up. The limits use the page's own timer and an
   `AbortController` rather than `AbortSignal.timeout`, so the tests can move the clock.
3. `MatchFrame`: while the board downloads, `MatchStage`'s loading fallback keeps the board's place, as tall as
   the smallest board of that game at that width (`boardSpace` in `MatchShell.module.css`). The action bar stays
   outside the board's loading boundary, under the stage, as before. So Leave room, Resign and Offer draw can
   always be pressed, keep their focus as the game starts, and stay put when the board comes. The heights are each
   board's height on the stage during the game (my turn and their turn), measured at stage widths from 290 to
   776 px. `100cqi` is the stage's width, through a container on the fallback:
   - Tic-Tac-Toe: `min(100cqi + 137px, 541px)`, its square grid plus its card, exact at every width measured (Chromium with this laptop's fonts; other fonts can differ by a few px).
   - Rock Paper Scissors: 402 px, 432 px under 540 px and 456 px under 310 px, as its choices wrap. Exact.
   - Chess, side by side (over 48rem): `max(100cqi - 38px, 472px)`. Stacked: `100cqi + 410px`, the height on the
     player's own turn. Their turn is 29 px taller, because of one more status line. So when the board of a game
     that starts on the other player's turn arrives on a phone, the action bar moves 29 px down. Chess's action bar
     already moves by that much at every turn today (the CC-172 layout).

   The first version (review finding) drew the action bar inside the board's loading boundary. That hid Leave room,
   Resign and Offer draw for the whole download, and for ever when the board's promise never settles (the
   stale-build `RELOADING` path), and the focus was lost as the bar was hidden. The design lead decided that Leave
   must always be available. Research: web.dev's CLS guide says to reserve space for late content. CC-322 reserved
   the lobby art's space with an `aspect-ratio`.

**Research.** How our code does it today: `SessionProvider` defers its ask during its own sign ups, log ins and
log outs (`own`), but a renewal is not one of them, and `askServer` does not defer an answer that lands meanwhile.
The server side (CC-116 grace, CC-326 release) cannot tell an old-id request that was on its way from one sent
after, and keeping the old session readable for the grace window would let any copy of the old cookie in for 5 s,
which CC-116 decided against ("The old id is destroyed, so a copy of the old cookie no longer works"). Others:
express-session's README warns of this race ("a client makes two parallel requests to your server and changes made
to the session in one request may get overwritten") and leaves it to the app; OWASP's Session Management Cheat
Sheet asks for the id to be renewed and the old one invalidated at once on any privilege change, so the fix belongs
with the client, which knows which of its requests are on their way. Best for ClassiCade: hold this tab's requests
around the renewal, no server change, no security trade. Other tabs of the same browser can still race (rare, and
the next ask puts them right); that is not this test.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** Given the six chess layout states on `BELOW_THE_FOLD`, when the suite runs, then they show ✘ and count
  as passed, every run, and the comment there says so.
- **AC-2** Given the profile page with its requests on their way, when the player signs out the other devices or
  changes the password, then the renewal is sent only after those requests are answered, every request made
  meanwhile is sent after it with the new id, and the page stays signed in with its status. A request that is
  never answered holds the renewal for at most 3 s. A renewal is given up after 90 s, a request behind it waits
  90 s at most, the export is given up after 120 s, and other requests wait as long as a waking server takes.
- **AC-3** Given a room whose game just started and whose board is still downloading, when the board arrives,
  then Leave room was pressable and kept its focus the whole time, and it does not move (Tic-Tac-Toe and Rock
  Paper Scissors at every width, Chess on its own turn).

## Test

- `api.test.js` "a renewal of the session id (CC-346)": for both renewals, a held `GET /api/progress` keeps the
  renewal from being sent, and a `GET /api/session` made meanwhile is sent only after the renewal's 204. A refused
  password change lets the held request go. The first two fail without the fix (the renewal and the ask go out at
  once). "with a request on its way that is never answered", with a fake clock: the renewal goes after 3 s and a
  logout after it still goes out, and the slow request is still answered after 120 s (no limit of its own). A
  renewal that is never answered fails as NETWORK at 90 s, not before. A request held behind a renewal that is
  never answered is sent at 90 s. The export is given up at 120 s, not before. The first three fail on the 15 s
  version.
- `MatchShell.test.jsx` "keeps Leave room pressable and focused while the board downloads": a waiting room starts
  its game while the board is still downloading. The stage keeps the chess board's place, Leave room is the same
  focused button, and pressing it opens "Leave the game?", which stays open when the board comes. It fails on the
  first version (Leave room hidden).
- `match-shell.spec.js` "Leave room stays where it is, focused and pressable, while the board downloads": alice's
  `TicTacToeBoard` chunk is held with `page.route` until the game has started. Leave room stays focused across the
  game start and can be pressed during the download. Its place on the page is the same before and after the board
  comes. It fails on `main` (the button moves: 40 px down on the laptop) and on the first version (focus lost). It
  passes on Chromium desktop and mobile (3 of 3 each) and on Firefox (3 of 3).
- Browser tests, `--repeat-each=10 --workers=1 --retries=0` on loopback, before (`main`, a4a90fd) and after:

  | Test                                                | Chromium desktop + mobile                          | Firefox                                            |
  | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
  | chess layout: my-turn, their-turn, computer my-turn | 60 of 60 before, 60 of 60 after, all ✘ as expected | 30 of 30 before, 30 of 30 after, all ✘ as expected |
  | sign out other devices                              | 20 of 20 before, 20 of 20 after                    | 10 of 10 before, 10 of 10 after                    |
  | a win because the opponent left                     | 20 of 20 before, 20 of 20 after                    | 10 of 10 before, 10 of 10 after (\*)               |

  (\*) With the `api.js` fix alone, Firefox ran 9 of 10. The one failure had a different cause: Firefox wrote
  "“localhost” has been classified as a bounce tracker" to alice's console, and the fixtures fail on that. That is
  CC-347. After the `MatchFrame` fix and CC-347 it ran 10 of 10. Locally the board is always loaded before the
  press, so these counts cannot show the forfeit race either way. The CI trace and the unit test above are the
  evidence. Regression runs after the fix: `match-shell.spec.js`, `pass-and-play.spec.js` and
  `match-shell-presses-tictactoe.spec.js` on Chromium desktop + mobile, 147 passed and 1 skipped. Firefox
  `match-shell.spec.js` and `pass-and-play.spec.js`, 58 passed and 1 skipped.

- One worker does not reproduce the sign out race; a busy server does, as in CC-326. With `--workers=8`, Chromium
  desktop + mobile: **4 failed of 400 before** (1 "element(s) not found" for the status, the CI failure, and 3 "HTTP
  401 GET /api/progress"), **0 of 320 after**.
