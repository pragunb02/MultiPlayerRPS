## Research (written before any code)

### 1. How our code does it today

1. `apps/web/src/hooks/useAction.js`: a press sets the phase to "sending" at once and starts a 150 ms timer
   (`ACTION_TIMING.PENDING_AFTER_MS`). When it fires before the answer, the phase becomes "pending". The answer
   moves straight to "done" (600 ms) or "failed", however short the "pending" phase was.
2. `apps/web/src/components/Button/Button.jsx`: in "pending" the label is replaced by a spinner and
   `pendingLabel` ("Sending…"), and the button is `aria-busy`. Boards use the same phase through
   `games/useMove.js` (`pending`, for `aria-busy` on the pressed cell or hand).
3. The press itself (the face going down) is CSS `:active`, and the click sound comes from `data-sound="click"`.
   Neither depends on the phase.
4. The server runs in Oregon (Render free, ADR-005 deferred). From India a round trip is about 250 to 350 ms
   (ROADMAP: about 280 ms of it is distance). So almost every press passes 150 ms, shows "Sending…" and then swaps
   it for the result 100 to 200 ms later: a flash on nearly every press.
5. The "every press answers within 100 ms" browser checks (`e2e/match-shell.spec.js`, `e2e/controlProbe.js`)
   measure the first of a page change, a socket message or the click sound after the press. The pending label
   comes after 150 ms, so it never counted towards them. Changing its timing cannot break them.

### 2. How others do it

1. Jakob Nielsen, "Response Times: The 3 Important Limits" (NN/g, 1993,
   nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits): 0.1 s feels instant, 1 s keeps the flow of thought,
   10 s is the limit of attention. Feedback under 0.1 s is the press itself. A waiting indicator only earns its
   place for waits the player notices.
2. Katie Sherwin, "Progress Indicators Make a Slow System Less Insufferable" (NN/g, 26 October 2014,
   nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators): for anything under about 1 second, a looped animation is
   distracting.
3. Google's RAIL model (web.dev/articles/rail): give a visible response within 100 ms. For us that is the press
   and the click, which stay.
4. `spin-delay` (github.com/smeijer/spin-delay, read 2026-10-18): wraps a loading flag so the spinner shows only
   after a delay (default 500 ms) and then stays for a minimum time (default 200 ms), because a spinner for a fast
   request "does more harm than good" and a brief one flickers.
5. The Epic Stack (github.com/epicweb-dev/epic-stack, `app/utils/misc.tsx`, read 2026-10-18) uses the same idea
   with `delay = 400` and `minDuration = 300`, "to avoid a flash of loading state regardless of how fast or slow the
   request is".

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

1. Show the waiting label after 400 ms. It sits above our usual 250 to 350 ms round trip from India, so a normal
   press goes straight from the press to its result, and well under Nielsen's 1 second, so a slow press is still
   explained before the player wonders.
2. Once shown, keep it at least 300 ms, so an answer at 410 ms does not flash it for 10 ms. The result that
   arrived during that time is shown when the 300 ms are up. This matches the Epic Stack values.
3. Hold the shown phase only. The work's promise still resolves when the answer comes, so pages that move on after
   an answer (for example to a new room) do not wait.
4. Keep the press (the face going down) and the click sound unchanged. They are the answer within 100 ms.
5. Keep using timers only, so the unit tests use fake timers and read no clock.

## Why

A label that flashes on almost every press is noise, and it makes a quick game feel slow.

## Plan / notes

The board row was added by PR #66, which merged first. It is set to done in this pull request.

1. `ACTION_TIMING` is `{ PENDING_AFTER_MS: 400, PENDING_AT_LEAST_MS: 300, DONE_FOR_MS: 600 }`.
2. `phase`, `busy` and `error` follow the answer at once. A new field, `waitingShown`, is what `Button` reads for
   the spinner, the waiting words and `aria-busy`. It turns true with "pending" and, once true, stays true at
   least 300 ms. A success that arrives in that time is "done" at once (so `busy` is false), and its check shows
   when the 300 ms are up, for the full 600 ms. A failure ends the 300 ms at once, so the reason and Retry show
   straight away. A new press clears all of it.
3. Review finding on PR #71 (low, confirmed): the first version held the whole phase, so on boards `busy` stayed
   true and a refusal stayed hidden for up to 300 ms after the server had answered. A second press in that time
   was refused with "Sending your move". Fixed by holding only `waitingShown`. Two board tests (a refusal at
   410 ms shows at once and the next cell can be pressed, and the same after a success) failed before the fix.
4. Boards read `busy`, `pending` and `error`, which are never held. The pressed cell's `aria-busy` ends with the
   answer.
