## Research (written after the work, 2026-10-18)

This section was written after the work, on 2026-10-18. PR #57 was started, and merged on 2026-10-17, before the
"research before code" rule (working agreement rule 4, ADR-039). It records what the research would have said and
checks what we built against it. It changes no code.

### 1. How our code did it before (`git show 370c614:apps/server/src/service/http/authRoutes.js`)

1. `perIp`: 20 requests per 15 minutes per address for the account routes.
2. `failedLoginsPerEmail`: 5 failed logins per 15 minutes per email, counted from every address together
   (`skipSuccessfulRequests`), and once reached it refused every login for that email, the right password too.
   Anyone who knew a player's email could keep them out by failing five times every 15 minutes (SEC-16).
3. Login already answered every failure the same way after the same work (`verifyAgainstDummy`).

### 2. How others do it (sources read 2026-10-18)

1. OWASP Authentication Cheat Sheet
   (https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authentication_Cheat_Sheet.html): lockout is set by a
   threshold, an observation window and a lockout duration, and "care must be taken to prevent it from being used
   to cause a denial of service by locking out other users' accounts". It suggests letting a locked out player in
   through the forgotten password flow. One generic message for every login failure.
2. OWASP Credential Stuffing Prevention Cheat Sheet
   (https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Credential_Stuffing_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html): MFA is "by
   far the best defense". Then CAPTCHA only for suspicious logins, IP based responses that are graduated and
   temporary (blocking IPs "should not be used as the sole or primary defense"), device and connection
   fingerprints, checking passwords against breaches, and telling players about unusual sign-ins.
3. OWASP Denial of Service Cheat Sheet
   (https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Denial_of_Service_Cheat_Sheet.html): rate limits by address,
   and keep each user's cost to the server small.
4. lichess (`app/controllers/Account.scala`, lila `master`, read 2026-10-18): the password routes run behind a
   hashing rate limit (`HasherRateLimit`). The limits themselves are in other files and were not read.
5. GitHub: its docs read for CC-116 do not describe its login throttle, so nothing is claimed for it here.

### 3. Does what we built match?

1. Matches: the block is per email and address, so a guesser can no longer lock the real player out from
   somewhere else; the soft 2 second delay per email is OWASP's "graduated" response and refuses nobody; known
   addresses skip it (a simple form of "known device"); an unknown email counts like a wrong password, so the
   throttle reveals nothing; memory is bounded.
2. Accepted difference: a blocked address stays blocked even with the right password (owner, 2026-10-17, reason
   above). OWASP's way out for that player is the password reset, which does not exist yet (CC-033).
3. Gaps (findings, not fixed here):
   1. **G1. No second factor.** OWASP's first defense. Suggested ticket: security, P3, "Optional TOTP for
      accounts, asked on a new address" (owner to decide; the freeze allows security tickets).
   2. **G2. No breached password check.** Same finding as CC-116 G2; one ticket covers both.
   3. **G3. The reset flow must not be blocked by this throttle.** Suggested: a line in CC-033's acceptance
      criteria, "a player blocked by the login throttle can still reset their password and sign in with the new
      one".
   4. **G4. Counts live in one process** and a restart forgets them. Known and tracked (CC-061). The emails in
      the throttle's keys are being hashed by CC-250 (PR #81). No new ticket.
   5. **G5. The scrypt cost is below OWASP's minimum**, which matters most when a password list leaks. Same
      finding as CC-116 G1.

## Why

SEC-16: failed logins were limited to 5 per 15 minutes per email from every address together, and the limit
refused even the right password. Anyone who knew a player's email could keep them out of their account by typing a
wrong password five times every quarter of an hour.

## Scope

In: `service/http/loginThrottle.js` (named rules in `LOGIN_THROTTLE`), used by `POST /api/auth/login`. The old
per email limiter is gone.

1. Hard block per email and address: 5 wrong passwords for one email from one address in 15 minutes, then that
   address is refused (429, "Too many attempts, try again later") for that email until the window ends, even with
   the right password. The window counts from the first failure. A successful login forgives that address its own
   failures.
2. Soft delay per email: once one email has 10 wrong passwords in 15 minutes from all addresses together, every
   attempt for it from an address that has not signed in to it in the last 30 days waits 2 seconds before the
   password is checked. Nobody is refused by this rule.
3. Known addresses: the last 10 addresses that signed in to an email are remembered for 30 days. They skip the
   delay.
4. Unchanged: 20 requests per 15 minutes per address for all account routes together.
5. An unknown email counts exactly like a wrong password, so the throttle says nothing about which emails exist.
   A login that failed because storage failed is not counted.

Out: a "known device" cookie that survives logging out (would add a second cookie and a consent question). Sharing
counts across instances (CC-061).

## The lockout matrix

| Who tries                                              | What happens                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Attacker, one address, wrong passwords                 | 5 tries, then 429 for that email from that address            |
| Same attacker, right password after the block          | 429 (no oracle)                                               |
| Real player, home address, while attacker blocked      | Signs in normally                                             |
| Real player, new address, while many addresses guess   | Signs in, after a 2 second wait                               |
| Real player, known address, while many addresses guess | Signs in with no wait                                         |
| Real player who mistyped 5 times from home             | Blocked for the rest of the 15 minutes from that address only |
| Many addresses guessing one email                      | Each address 5 tries per 15 minutes, every try slowed down    |

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-17: a blocked address stays blocked even with the right password.
"A correct password always passes from a known-good address" holds unless that same address itself made 5 wrong
guesses for that email in the last 15 minutes. Letting the right password through then would turn the block into a
yes or no answer for whoever is guessing from that address.

## Plan / notes

1. Counts live in the process, like the other limiters. A restart forgets the failures and the known addresses
   together, so nobody is blocked by a count that outlived its reason.
2. Memory is bounded: once more than 10 000 keys are remembered, expired counts and old addresses are dropped, at
   most once a minute so a flood of logins cannot make every request walk every key. Each email keeps at most 10
   addresses.
3. Addresses are rate limit keys (`clientIpKey`, IPv6 per /56). Emails are never logged.
