Accessibility reuses this check (domain §1, boundaries). It is the machine half of the CC-314 contract's "most per
screen" and readability rules; [CC-316](CC-316.md)'s review reads its results.

## Problem

The owner found chess move numbers from 10 on showing only their last digit. Before that, a 20-character name broke
the phone header (CC-256) and confetti made the page wider than a phone (CC-270). Each was found by eye, after it
shipped, and fixed alone. Our browser tests check that things exist and can be pressed, and axe checks contrast on
the states the tests happen to reach with ordinary data. Nothing checks, on every screen and state, that text is
not cut off, that no two things overlap, that nothing covers a control, and that it still holds with long names,
long games, big numbers, a 320 px phone and 200 % zoom. As features add text (CC-315's problem), this is the class of
bug that will grow fastest.

## Job to be done

When I play on a small phone, with a long name, deep into a chess game, or with my text zoomed, I want to read
everything and press every button, so the game never looks broken or hides something from me.

## Appetite

M (up to 6 agent-hours), in two PRs: (1) the probe and its runs over every screen and state with stress data, (2) the
fixes of what it finds. **Cut line:** fixes beyond the appetite become one ticket each (with the probe's finding as
the failing test, marked as a known failure with that ticket's id); the 200 % zoom run before the 320 px run; the
WebKit run (advisory anyway, CC-273). The probe itself and the 320 px and 390 px runs are never cut.

## Proposed solution

A probe, `e2e/layoutProbe.js`, injected like `layout-width.spec.js`'s recorder, that walks the visible DOM of the
current page and reports four kinds of finding:

1. **Clipped text:** an element with text whose `scrollWidth` or `scrollHeight` is larger than its client box while
   its overflow is `hidden` or `clip`, or with `text-overflow: ellipsis` where the full text is not available
   another way (a `title` is not enough; the accessible name or a visible expanded view is).
2. **Overflow:** anything past the viewport's right edge (the CC-270 rule, generalised to every screen), or past its
   scroll container when that container does not scroll.
3. **Overlap:** two interactive elements, or an interactive element and text, whose boxes intersect by more than
   1 px, unless one is inside the other or the overlap is a documented layer of the CC-314 contract (toast region
   with `pointer-events: none`, a dialog over its backdrop, a celebration effect with `pointer-events: none`). A
   control covered by anything that takes clicks (`elementFromPoint` at its centre is not it or its child) is
   always a finding.
4. **Contrast:** axe's `color-contrast` and `link-in-text-block` rules (already used in CC-183), here run on the
   stress states too, plus a pure unit test that every text token pair in `tokens.css` passes 4.5:1 and every
   non-text pair 3:1 (shared with CC-314's AC-1).

`e2e/layout.spec.js` runs the probe on each screen and state from `e2e/matchStates.js` and the CC-155 screen list,
in these viewports: 320 × 640, 360 × 800, 390 × 844, 1280 × 720, and 640 × 360 CSS px as 200 % zoom of 1280 × 720
(WCAG 1.4.4 and 1.4.10's reflow equivalence), light and dark, with reduced motion so nothing is caught half-way.

**Stress data** (`e2e/stress.js`): a 20-character name (the sign-up maximum) on both seats, a guest name, a chess game
past move 120 (from a fixed PGN through the engine's replay, no clock waits), an RPS best-of-9 at 4–4, XP and level
numbers at 6 digits, 99+ achievements and quests at their caps, a long Hinglish line, and every result reason
(CC-163). Data is made through the existing test seams (personas, `replayGame`), never by editing the database by
hand.

A finding fails the test with a line that reads alone: the kind, the screen, the viewport, the element (role, name,
class), its box and the other box.

## Non-goals

- No pixel comparison (CC-155 does that) and no new axe rules beyond colour (CC-183 owns the full scan).
- No redesign of any screen: fixes are the smallest CSS change that removes the finding.
- No check of focus order or keyboard (CC-183, CC-048).
- Not the notice budget (CC-315); overlap of toasts with controls is checked, how many show is not.

## Rabbit holes

- **False overlaps** from decorations, focus rings and shadows: boxes are the border box, rings and shadows are not
  layout, and `aria-hidden` art with `pointer-events: none` is skipped.
- **Every state of every screen** could be hundreds of runs: the states are the ones `matchStates.js` and CC-155
  already reach, times the viewports, sharded with `e2e/shards.js`. If the suite grows past 4 minutes per part, the
  zoom run moves to the nightly workflow.
- **Long chess games by playing them** would be slow: replay from a PGN.

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-1** Given a fixture page with clipped text, an element past the right edge, two overlapping buttons and a
  button covered by a fixed bar, when the probe runs, then it reports exactly those four findings and nothing for a
  control fixture with none of them. Test: `e2e/layoutProbe.test.js` (unit, jsdom with layout stubbed, or a
  Playwright component page) › "finds clipping, overflow, overlap and covered controls, and nothing else".
- **AC-2** Given every screen and state in the CC-155 list and `matchStates.js`, when `e2e/layout.spec.js` runs at
  320 × 640, 360 × 800, 390 × 844, 1280 × 720 and 640 × 360, light and dark, with the stress data, then there is no
  finding. Test: `e2e/layout.spec.js` › "every screen and state, every viewport, no finding".
- **AC-3** Given a chess game past move 120 on a 320 px phone, when the move list is shown, then every move number
  shows all its digits. Test: `e2e/layout.spec.js` › "chess move numbers to three digits are never clipped".
- **AC-4** Given the longest allowed names on both seats at 320 px, when the room, the match bar, the result and the
  leaderboard show, then nothing is clipped without the full name in the accessible name, and nothing overlaps.
  Test: `e2e/layout.spec.js` › "the longest names fit every place a name shows".
- **AC-5** Given the stress states in light and dark, when axe's colour rules run, then no serious or critical
  violation. Test: `e2e/layout.spec.js` › "stress states pass contrast in both themes".
- **AC-6** Given a failure, when the test fails, then its message names the kind, screen, viewport, element and
  boxes in one line. Test: `layoutProbe.test.js` › "a finding reads without the report".
- **AC-7** Given the suite, when it runs in CI, then each browser part stays within its time budget (CC-236). Test:
  the CI timing in the PR.

## Research (written before any code)

### 1. How our code does it today

Read on 2026-10-20 on `main` (a5a801c8). `e2e/layout-width.spec.js` records the widest the page has been on every
frame at 360, 390 and 412 px (CC-256, CC-270), only for main screens and the win effects. `e2e/dead-space.spec.js`
checks empty columns (CC-259). `e2e/accessibility.spec.js` runs axe (WCAG 2.0 to 2.2, A and AA, best practices) in
light and dark on every screen the tests reach, with ordinary data (CC-183). CC-155's baselines cover 14 screens at
1280 × 720 and 390 × 844. The chess move list now uses a subgrid (`ChessBoard.module.css`, since CC-157), so the
owner's move-number bug may already be gone; AC-3 proves it either way. No test uses long games, 320 px or zoom.

### 2. How others do it (sources read 2026-10-20 unless marked)

| Source                                                                                                                                                                                                       | What it says                                                                                                                             | What we take                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| WCAG 2.2 [1.4.10 Reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html) and [1.4.4 Resize text](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html) (general knowledge, high confidence) | Content works at 320 CSS px wide without two-way scrolling (1280 px at 400 %); text resizes to 200 % without loss of content or function | 320 px and 640 px runs                                                                          |
| WCAG 2.2 [2.4.11 Focus not obscured (minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html) (read 2026-10-20)                                                                | Sticky bars, toasts and non-modal overlays fail when they fully hide the focused item                                                    | Covered controls are always a finding                                                           |
| WCAG 2.2 [1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html) and [1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)                                         | 4.5:1 text, 3:1 non-text                                                                                                                 | The token pair test and axe on stress states                                                    |
| Deque axe-core (in use since CC-183)                                                                                                                                                                         | Automated rules find a share of WCAG issues; layout and clipping are not among them                                                      | Our own probe for what axe cannot see                                                           |
| NN/g, [10 usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) (1994, reviewed 2024-01-30), number 1 and 8                                                                      | Visibility of system status; aesthetic and minimalist design                                                                             | A cut-off number hides status; overlap is clutter                                               |
| NN/g, [Modal and nonmodal dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/) (2017-04-23)                                                                                                     | A modal hides what the user may need; a nonmodal overlay leaves the page usable                                                          | A dialog over its backdrop is a documented layer; any other overlay over a control is a finding |
| W3C Design Tokens CG, [specification 2025.10](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/2025.10/) (2025-10-28)                                                                                                         | Colours as typed, aliased tokens by role                                                                                                 | Contrast is tested once per token pair, not per screen (CC-314)                                 |
| Material Design 3 and Apple HIG layout guidance (general knowledge, high confidence; the pages did not render for the fetch)                                                                                 | Text truncation only where the full text is reachable; minimum touch targets (48 dp, 44 pt)                                              | D4: ellipsis only with the full name available                                                  |

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

Our players are mostly on small Android phones (LOOK_AND_FEEL §2), with names they chose and games that can run long.
A DOM probe is cheap, deterministic and explains itself, so it can be a required check, and it catches exactly the
bugs the owner keeps finding by eye. Pixel baselines (CC-155) and the human review (CC-316) cover the rest.
