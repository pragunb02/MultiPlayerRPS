Part 1 (this pull request) is friends and presence. Part 2 is the invites, after CC-139 and CC-147. The design was
agreed with the owner on 2026-10-20 before the CC-311 templates, so this file keeps that design and its research
below the template's sections.

## Problem

Today a player can meet someone they enjoyed playing only by sending a new room link by hand, and the room's
rematch lasts 30 minutes. Nobody can keep a list of the people they play with, see whether they are on, or reach
them again from the site. Retention comes from people you know (ADR-028).

## Job to be done

When I have just played someone I know, I want to add them as a friend and see when they are on, so we can play
again without hunting for a new link.

## Appetite

L (part 1, up to 2 days of agent time). **Cut line:** the match history Add friend and the toast for a new request
go first, then Appear offline. Never cut: accounts only, silent declines and blocks, the minors' rules, no player id
to any client.

## Proposed solution

As built, in "Part 1 as built" below: Add friend from the result screen and match history, a one-use friend link,
accept or Not now, the friends page with presence for friends only and Appear offline, four new stores, pushes on
the player's own channel.

## Non-goals

- Invites to a game in any of the 7 scenarios (part 2, after CC-139 and CC-147).
- Web push (CC-148), email (CC-293), follow (CC-206), circles (CC-214), friends on leaderboards (CC-140).
- Search by name, "people you may know", friends of friends, a public friends list.
- The "who can add me" switch (CC-278).

## Rabbit holes

- Presence across several server processes: kept in memory, single instance (CC-061 later).
- Telling a block from a decline by timing or by a push: no push to a sender for a decline or a block, and every
  such request is stored and shown as "Request sent".
- Late answers and pushes overtaking each other: the page reads the view again after a push during a read.

## Threat model

We build mutual friends, a shareable link and presence. What can go wrong: a stranger reaching a child, a player
learning they were blocked, a player id or a link token leaking, spam requests, and presence used to follow
someone. What we do: friends only from a shared game or a link the maker confirms, minors never from quick match and
never named on a link, blocks and declines indistinguishable, handles and random ids only, tokens stored as SHA-256
and one use, caps and rate limits per player and per address, presence to friends only and never a room or a time.
Tests crawl every answer and push for player ids. reviewers check the rest.

## Privacy checklist

- **Data:** who is friends with whom and since when, requests (who asked whom, how, its status), friend links (the
  maker and a hash), the Appear offline choice. Presence is never stored.
- **Retention:** friendships until removed, blocked or deleted. requests 30 days (TTL). links 7 days or one use
  (TTL). the switch until changed. Account deletion erases all four on both sides.
- **Who sees it:** each player sees their own friends and requests. friends see each other's presence. nobody sees
  anyone else's friends. the owner through the database. Logs hold the player id of the actor only.
- **Minors (ADR-028):** mutual only, never suggested, a safety line beside requests, never from quick match, never
  named on their link, no profile link for them.
- **Defaults:** private by default. presence to friends only. Appear offline off (an owner decision above).

## Alternatives considered

- Follows like Lichess: ADR-028.5 keeps follow for adults and last.
- A link that makes friends at once: a forwarded link could add a stranger without the maker seeing who.
- Hiding a declined request from its sender: it would tell the sender they were declined, and a block too.
- Naming friends by a keyed per-pair id: the profile handle already exists for this and is what part 2 addresses.

## Research (written before any code)

Sources were read on 2026-10-20. **[read]** means the page or the source file was opened and read. **General
knowledge** carries a confidence level, as the rest of `docs/` does.

### 1. How our code does it today

1. **Meeting someone again.** A player can meet a past opponent only by sending a new room link by hand. Rematch
   lasts 30 minutes in the same room (`roomLifetimes.js`). There is no list of people, no request between players
   outside a room, and nothing that says whether someone is on the site.
2. **Requests inside a room** (`domain/rooms/roomRequests.js`, ADR-019, CC-159) are rematch and draw questions, kept
   in the room and pushed to both players on any page through the player's own channel `player:<id>`
   (`service/realtime/roomHandlers.js`, `request:updated`, CC-207). The web shows them in `SiteRequests.jsx`, and
   short notices go through the toasts and the notification categories (CC-208, `lib/notify/categories.js` has a
   `social` category nobody uses yet).
3. **Presence today is per room seat** (`domain/rooms/roomPresence.js`): here, away from the game screen, offline.
   The gateway knows each player's open sockets (`player:<id>` channel, `socketAdmission.js` counts them). Nothing
   says "on the site" outside a room, and nothing would say it to anyone who is not in the same room.
4. **"Only people you played with"** is already a rule for blocks (ADR-053.3): `blockComponent` names the other player
   by `{ roomCode, seat, seatId }` or `{ matchId, seat }`, checks that both sat in that open room or played that
   stored match, and answers one 404 for anything it cannot confirm. Friends need the same check, so it moves into a
   shared helper rather than being copied.
5. **Blocks** (`blockComponent.blockedWith`, `relationsOf`) are read by rooms before they seat or rematch a pair. A
   block refuses with words any player could get ("This room is full"), never words of its own (ADR-053.2).
6. **Public address.** A profile handle (16 random Crockford base 32 letters, `player_profiles`, ADR-065) is the only
   id for a person that is not tied to one room or one match. It is linked on boards and in history only for adults
   (ADR-065.2). No client ever receives another person's player id (ADR-057, CC-297, DATA.md Identifiers).
7. **Ages.** Unknown age means minor (ADR-028.1, ADR-048.8). `accountComponent.adultsAmong` is the one check. Most
   accounts on `main` have no birth year, so most players get the minor defaults.
8. **Guests** are real players with ids (`guest_<uuid>`) whose data moves on sign up (ADR-014). The owner decided
   guests cannot have friends.
9. **Link previews.** The server writes each page's head tags from one page list (`packages/shared/src/site.js`,
   ADR-042). A profile's head never names the player.
10. **Gaps.** No friend store, no site presence, no shareable token that is not an id, no way to reach a past
    opponent.

What the rule books say about children:

1. **ICO Children's code, standard 7 "Default settings"** **[read]**: "Settings must be 'high privacy' by default."
   Children's data should be "only visible or accessible to other users of the service if the child amends their
   settings", and never "visible to indefinite numbers of other users". New features default to high privacy.
2. **ICO standard 10 "Geolocation"** **[read]**: location options are off by default and go back to off at the end of
   each session. Presence is not location, and we never say _where_ (which room, which game).
3. **ICO standard 13 "Nudge techniques"** **[read]**: no uneven "yes" button, no cumbersome way out of a privacy
   setting. Pro-privacy nudges are fine.
4. **Ofcom Illegal Content Codes (user to user), measures ICU F1 and F2 (grooming)**, in force from 17 March 2025
   **[read]**: children are never suggested to others as connections and never shown "network expansion prompts"
   (F1.2), children's connection lists are not shown to other users (F1.3), a connection between children needs one
   to ask and the other to confirm (F1.5), a child's location is not visible (F1.6), and a child sees safety
   information before accepting a connection request (F2.3). F1 does not mention online status.
5. **India's DPDP Act 2023, section 9** **[read]**: a child is under 18, no processing "likely to cause any
   detrimental effect on the well-being of a child", and "shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of
   children". Presence that is never stored and never kept as history is not monitoring. The DPDP Rules 2025 are
   phased in (general knowledge, medium confidence).
6. **Link previews.** WhatsApp, Telegram, Slack and email scanners fetch a shared link with a plain GET to read its
   Open Graph tags (general knowledge, high confidence. Meta's crawler page lists `facebookexternalhit/1.1`
   **[read]**). So a GET of a friend link must never use it up: only a signed-in press does.

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

The agreed design holds. The research adds these points (each is in ADR-066):

1. **Mutual only, by request and accept**, as Chess.com and Roblox. Not follows (Lichess), which ADR-028.5 keeps
   for adults and last.
2. **Declines are silent**, as Chess.com says it in so many words. The sender sees "Request sent" until the request
   runs out after 30 days, whether it was declined, ignored or never seen. That is also what makes a block look
   like nothing: a request into a block is stored and shown to its sender as sent, and never shown to the other
   player.
3. **10 requests waiting at a time** per sender (Chess.com's number), **200 friends** per player (far under Lichess's
   400 and Supercell's and Roblox's 1000: our players are mostly children, and a list that long is not people you
   know), and rate limits per player and per address like blocks and profiles.
4. **The friend link is one use and 7 days** (Discord's default length, and one use because it may be forwarded).
   The link only shows a page. Pressing "Add friend" on that page sends a request to the link's maker, who
   confirms it. This is Roblox's "people you know" line and Ofcom F1.5 (one asks, the other confirms) in one rule:
   a forwarded link can at most ask once, and its maker still sees who asked. The token is 128 random bits, stored
   only as its SHA-256 hash, and never a player id.
5. **Minors.** Mutual only, never suggested to anyone, no lists of other people's friends (Ofcom F1.2, F1.3), and a
   safety line before a minor accepts (F2.3): "Only add people you know". Friends only from a room joined by a link
   or a friend link, never a quick match stranger (ADR-051.7): every room today is joined by a link, and the rule
   is written so CC-051 only has to mark its rooms.
6. **Presence** shows online, in a game or offline, to friends only, never which room or which game, never stored,
   never a "last seen". **Appear offline** for everyone, as Roblox's "no one". Whether minors should start with
   Appear offline on (ICO standard 7) is an owner decision (below).
7. **No "people you may know"**, no search by name, no friends of friends, and nobody can see anyone else's friends.

## Design (agreed with the owner, 2026-10-20)

Follows ADR-028 (mutual friends first, follow last and adults only, the minors' rules), ADR-031 (notification caps)
and ADR-053 (blocks are silent).

### Making friends

1. Tap someone you played with, or send a "be my friend" link that works well in WhatsApp.
2. The other person accepts. Friends are always mutual.
3. **Guests cannot have friends** (owner decision). Friends need a saved account, which is also a reason to save
   progress (CC-292, CC-097).

### Inviting a friend from the friends list

1. **Both online, the friend on any page:** a site-wide popup "X invites you to Chess" with Accept and go or Not
   now, a sound and a tab alert (CC-207). It waits about 60 seconds, then moves to the inbox.
2. **Friend online but in a game:** nothing interrupts the game. The sender sees "In a game", and the invite shows
   when the game ends.
3. **Friend offline:** the invite becomes a persistent challenge with its own link (CC-139). It waits in the inbox
   (CC-147) for 24 hours. A web push notification goes out if the friend allowed it, and an email if the friend saved
   one. The sender also gets a Send on WhatsApp button.
4. **The sender went offline and the friend accepts:** the friend sees "X isn't here right now, we've told them",
   and the sender is notified. After about 5 minutes the friend can keep the challenge for later or play Pip
   meanwhile.
5. **Later:** turn-by-turn (correspondence) chess (CC-222), so friends never need to be online together.
6. **Several friends invited to a 2-player game:** the first to accept plays, and the others see "Game filled,
   invite again?". Ludo takes up to 4.
7. **A decline or no answer** shows a gentle "Not right now". A block looks exactly the same (ADR-053).

### Rules

1. Presence is shown only to friends, with an "Appear offline" switch.
2. At most 3 open invites per sender, at most 1 every 10 minutes to the same friend, and a daily notification cap
   (ADR-031).
3. **Minors:** mutual friends only, no push or email from 22:00 to 07:00 India time, no follow (ADR-028).
4. Invites go to friends only. Everyone else uses the share link.
5. Every invite and presence check asks `blockComponent.blockedWith` first (ADR-053.2).

### Delivery channels, in order (owner decision)

1. The in-app inbox (CC-147) and WhatsApp first.
2. Then web push, which needs an installable PWA and an opt-in (CC-148).
3. Then email, once the brand domain (CC-123) and an email service (CC-293) exist.

### Part 1 as built (ADR-066)

1. **Asking.** "Add friend", for accounts, beside another person's seat on the result screen (not while a game is
   on, so a move is never one tap from a request and the seats keep their size beside the board) and beside a past
   opponent in match history. The page sends `{ roomCode, seat, seatId }` or `{ matchId, seat }`, the
   same references a block uses, and the server checks both played there (`domain/players/coPlayers.js`, shared
   with blocks). Asking someone who already asked you makes you friends at once.
2. **The friend link.** "Make a friend link" on the friends page gives `/friends/add/<token>`, with Send on WhatsApp
   and Copy. The page shows who made it (an adult's name, or "A ClassiCade player" for anyone else, ADR-028.7) and
   "Add friend". Pressing it uses the link up and sends its maker a request. A GET never uses it.
3. **Answering.** Accept, or "Not now", which declines quietly. The sender is never told. The maker of a request can
   cancel it.
4. **Friends page** `/friends`: avatar, name, presence (online, in a game, offline), Remove (quiet, both ways), the
   requests waiting for you and the ones you sent, the friend link, and the Appear offline switch. Guests see "Save
   your progress to add friends" with Sign up and Log in, and so does a guest who opens a friend link.
5. **Live.** Each change pushes `friends:updated` (the whole list) to the player's tabs, a new request pushes
   `friends:request` (a toast with "See request" on any page), and a friend's presence change pushes
   `friends:presence` `{ handle, presence }` to their friends only.
6. **For part 2.** A friend is addressed by their profile handle, so invites plug in as
   `POST /api/friends/:handle/invites` and read `friendComponent.friendOf({ playerId, handle })` and the friend's
   presence from the same tracker. Requests already carry `via`, and the push channels and notification category
   are in place.
