## Research (written before any code)

### 1. How our console computes today

The owner console (CC-233, ADR-036) is built in five steps.

1. `persistence/analyticsDao.js` runs read-only aggregations. `listPlayerActivity()` reads **every match ever**
   (no `$match`), unwinds `players.playerId`, drops `bot_` ids, and groups each player's matches by India day
   (`$dateToString` with `timezone: "Asia/Kolkata"`). For each day it keeps how many matches finished, how many
   counted for the player under fair play (not in `credit.notCounted`, ADR-021) and how many had another person.
   Only the id's prefix (`guest`, `user`) leaves MongoDB. `getMatchFacts(moments)` and the other reads are
   bounded by an `endedAt` (or `createdAt`) range served by an index.
2. `data/analyticsAccessor.js` freezes the rows.
3. `domain/analytics/metrics.js` holds pure functions over those rows: active players per bucket, the activity
   summary (average daily actives, weekly, monthly, stickiness as the 30-day average daily actives over the
   30-day actives), new players (first match ever), the current North Star "weekly returning players" (a counted
   match on 3 or more days of a Monday to Sunday week, PRODUCT_VISION.md 7.1, with its guardrails), and the
   cohort table. `privacy.js` turns any count of 1 to 4 people into `"<5"`.
4. `domain/analytics/analyticsComponent.js` builds the whole report in one `Promise.all` and keeps it for
   5 minutes per range in `utils/ttlCache.js` (concurrent requests share one build). The North Star headline is
   the last full week (`lastFullWeek`) against the week before.
5. `apps/web/src/pages/AdminPage` draws each section (Overview, Players, Games, Retention, Conversion,
   Engagement) as SVG charts plus a table and a CSV export (`ChartFigure`).

Indexes on `matches` today: `{ "players.playerId": 1, endedAt: -1 }` (history pages) and `{ endedAt: 1 }`
(console ranges, CC-233).

**Gaps.** There is one "active" number and one North Star, and nothing says what kind of player someone is on a
given day, how many are drifting away, or where this week's regulars came from. ADR-034 point 4 (owner, 2026-10-18)
asks for statuses instead. The full scan in `listPlayerActivity` is already the slowest read (ADR-036 says it
should move to the nightly rollup, CC-176, at about 500,000 matches), so the statuses must not lean on it.

### 2. How other products define engagement states

Sources, all fetched 2026-10-18:

1. Jorge Mazal, "How Duolingo reignited user growth", Lenny's Newsletter, 28 February 2023
   (https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth).
2. Erin Gustafson, "Meet the Growth Model", Duolingo blog, 17 February 2023
   (https://blog.duolingo.com/growth-model-duolingo/).
3. Amplitude docs, "Interpret your Lifecycle chart", last updated 2026-09-22
   (https://amplitude.com/docs/analytics/charts/lifecycle/lifecycle-interpret).
4. Amplitude docs, "Interpret your Stickiness chart", last updated 2026-09-08
   (https://amplitude.com/docs/analytics/charts/stickiness/stickiness-interpret).
5. Li Jin, "The Power User Curve", andrewchen.com, about July 2018 (no date on the page)
   (https://andrewchen.com/power-user-curve/).
6. Wikipedia, "Active users" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_users), on DAU/MAU.

What they do:

1. **Duolingo's growth model** (1, 2) puts every user who ever used the product in exactly one state each day:
   New (first day ever), Current (active today and on one of the 6 days before), Reactivated (first active day
   after 7 to 29 days away), Resurrected (first active day after 30 or more days away), At-risk WAU (not active
   today, active in the last 7 days), At-risk MAU (not active for 7 days, active within the month) and Dormant
   (30 or more days away). The states add up: the first four make DAU, plus at-risk WAU make WAU, and so on. The
   daily moves between states are rates (current user retention rate, CURR, and so on). Their key finding: CURR
   moved DAU about 5 times more than the next best lever, because a current user who stays current compounds.
   Lesson for us: states that are **mutually exclusive and cover everybody**, and the **moves** between them
   matter more than the totals.
2. **Amplitude Lifecycle** (3) classifies users per interval (day, week or month): New (first seen within the
   interval), Current (active this interval and the one before), Resurrected (active now, not in the previous
   interval, but older than one interval) and Dormant (active in the previous interval, not in this one). Its
   Dormant is a **flow** (who dropped out this interval), not a stock of everyone who ever left. Lesson: the
   stock of long-gone users only grows and says little. The number of people who dropped out this week does.
3. **Amplitude Stickiness** (4) is a histogram of how many days of a week (or month) each user did the event,
   in exact days or "at least N days". This is the shape behind our Casual (1 day), Regular (2 to 3) and Core
   (4 or more).
4. **The Power User Curve** (5) is the same histogram (L7 for a week, L30 for a month, a term from Facebook's
   growth team). It argues the spread of days active says more than DAU/MAU, which blends everyone into one
   number and hides a hardcore group.
5. **DAU/MAU "stickiness"** (6) is called a "rudimentary" estimate of engagement, with 0.15 a growth tipping
   point and 0.2 lasting success (attributed to a 2011 GamesBrief post). It suffers from loose definitions of
   "active". We already show it on the Players page (CC-233) and keep it.

### 3. What is best for ClassiCade

ADR-034 point 4 fixes the statuses and the North Star. What is left to decide is how to count the days, which
status wins when two fit, what a week shows, and how to stay cheap on Atlas M0.

**A day of play.** An India day (Asia/Kolkata, ADR-034 point 2) on which the player finished a match that
counted for them under fair play (ADR-021), against a person or a computer character. This is the same "real
play" the current North Star uses (PRODUCT_VISION.md 7.1), so farming does not make anyone Core. Computer
opponents (`bot_` ids) are never players. Guests and accounts are counted alike. A guest's games move to the
account when they sign in (ADR-014), so from then on they are that account's days.

**Counting days back.** Today is day 1, so "the last 7 days" are today and the 6 days before. Then the windows
of ADR-034 fit together with no gap and no overlap: a last game on day 1 to 7 is active, on day 8 to 14 is
Lapsing, on day 15 or earlier is Dormant.

**The rules, worked out for one player on one India day D, in this order (the first that fits wins):**

1. **New**: their first day of play ever is one of the last 7 days.
2. **Dormant**: their last day of play is 15 or more days back (on or before D minus 14).
3. **Lapsing**: their last day of play is 8 to 14 days back (D minus 13 to D minus 7).
4. **Returning**: they played today, and the day of play before was 15 or more days before today, so there were
   14 or more days in between without a game. Put another way, they were Dormant yesterday.
5. **Core**: 4 or more days of play in the last 7 days.
6. **Regular**: 2 or 3 days.
7. **Casual**: 1 day.

A player who has never played is not counted at all. New wins over Casual, Regular and Core because a first week
is a different question (did they come back at all?) and is already in the cohort table, so a busy first week
does not count towards the North Star until day 8. Returning only ever has 1 day in the last 7, so it can only
clash with Casual, and wins. Every player who has ever played has exactly one status each day.

**Weeks.** Weeks run Monday to Sunday in India time, as everywhere in the console.

1. **The weekly trend** shows the status of each player on the last day of each week (Sunday, or today for the
   week in progress, marked "not over"): New, Casual, Regular, Core and Lapsing. On the last day of a full week
   the last 7 days are exactly that week, so Regular plus Core is "played 2 or more days this week, after their
   first week".
2. Two statuses are about a moment rather than a state, so they are counted as **flows during the week**, as
   Amplitude does: **Came back** is the players who were Returning on any day of the week, and **Went dormant**
   is the players who turned Dormant on a day of the week (their 15th day without a game). A snapshot of
   Returning would only count people who came back on the Sunday itself.
3. **The Dormant stock** (everyone who ever played and stopped) is not shown. It only grows, it is mostly guests
   who cleared cookies or never came back, and counting it needs every match ever, which is the read we must not
   grow (see the cost section). Went dormant per week is the number that moves.
4. **The North Star** is Regular plus Core on the last day of the week. The Overview headline is the last full
   week against the week before, the same week the current headline uses (`lastFullWeek`), so it never shows a
   drop every Monday. The weekly returning players number (3 or more days) stays on the Overview, no longer
   labelled North Star.
5. **Moves between statuses** for that headline week: a table of each player's status on the Sunday before the
   week against their status on its Sunday, including "not playing yet" for players whose first game came later.
   "Dormant to Dormant" cannot be counted with a bounded read, so it says "not counted". This is the Duolingo
   lesson: Core to Core is the number that compounds, and Regular to Lapsing is the leak.

**Small groups and privacy (ADR-036).** Every count of people, in the trend, the flows and every cell of the
moves table, is `"<5"` from 1 to 4 on the server. Nothing but kinds and India days leaves MongoDB, and no new
data is stored or logged. The report is part of the existing report, so it shares the 5-minute cache per range.

**Cost on Atlas M0.** A new read `listPlayerDays(moments)`:

1. `$match` on an `endedAt` range, served by the existing `{ endedAt: 1 }` index. The range starts 14 days
   before the earliest Sunday we need a status for and ends after the last day shown, so at most the asked range
   plus about 4 weeks.
2. Unwind `players.playerId`, drop bots and the seats that did not count, group by player and India day, then by
   player.
3. For each player found, one `$lookup` into `matches` on `players.playerId` with `endedAt` before the range,
   newest first, limit 1, served by the existing `{ "players.playerId": 1, endedAt: -1 }` index. That one day says
   whether the player is new and how long they were away. Players who did not play in the range are never read.

So the work grows with the matches in the range and the players in it, not with all history. **No new index is
needed.** The existing full scan (`listPlayerActivity`) is not touched and not used by the statuses.

**Limits, said on the page.** A guest is a cookie (one person on two devices counts twice until they sign in).
Dormant players who stayed dormant are not counted. A move table cell of 1 to 4 is hidden.

## Why

ADR-034 point 4: the owner wants to see what kind of players the site has each week and how they move, with one
North Star (Regular plus Core each week), instead of a single "active" number.

## Scope

- In: the status rules as pure functions, the bounded MongoDB read, a Statuses section in the console (weekly
  trend, flows, moves table, each with CSV), the North Star headline on the Overview, tests at every level.
- Out: the Dormant stock, statuses per game, telemetry, any per-player view, changes to PRODUCT_VISION.md 7.1
  (follow-up for the owner, see the PR).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Pure unit tests for every rule boundary: 7, 8, 14 and 15 days back; 1, 2, 3 and 4 days in a week; India
      midnight; bots and not-counted matches.
- [ ] MongoDB integration test of the read (known answers, India midnight, bots left out, the earlier day,
      no ids in the answer, same as the empty double on an empty database).
- [ ] Web tests for the Statuses section and its CSV.
- [ ] Browser test in `e2e/owner.spec.js`.
- [ ] Groups under 5 hidden everywhere.
- [ ] `npm run lint`, `npm run format:check`, `npm run test:coverage` pass.
